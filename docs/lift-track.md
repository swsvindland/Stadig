# Stadig Lift: how the doc maps to the code

## SystemCore: the shared core

Everything the apps must agree on lives here, so scores are identical wherever they're computed. It contains:

- **Models**
  - sessions (`SessionSummary`: "a session happened, here are the metrics")
  - logged sets and the three-tap `SetRating`
  - stats, quests, recommendations and goals
  - the published `AppSummary`
  - the shared profile, so setup is asked once
  - nutrition phases, life modes, measurements, mobility tests and endurance benchmarks
- **Strength scoring** (`StrengthStandards`, `StrengthEstimator`). Symmetric Strength style: each lift category's estimated 1RM is scored against bodyweight-relative standards with allometric scaling.
  - Only free-weight variants with a known ratio count. Machine weights are never converted.
  - Imbalances become priorities, e.g. "legs are well ahead of upper body, so upper body comes first".
- **RPE trust calibration** (`TrustState`, `Calibration`)
  - Trust starts from experience: novices 0.3, 1–3 years 0.5, 3+ years 0.7.
  - It moves only on evidence. A rep-out right after a rated set reveals the true reserve, and a heavier later set proves more was left. The sets in between are allowed for by `SetFatigue` (how hard each was and the rest after it), with a fifth of that fatigue as the benefit of the doubt, since tiring more looks just like overrating.
  - Contradictions count more than confirmations, because "0–2 left" is often right by chance.
  - Machines (safe to fail) calibrate it. Free-weight ratings use the result.
  - Trust is rebuilt from the session history every time, so devices never disagree.
- **Set-to-set autoregulation** (`Autoregulation`, `SetFatigue`)
  - Reps fall from set to set at the same weight, and that still builds muscle, so it's planned for rather than called a miss. `SetFatigue` says how much a set takes from the next: more after a set near failure, after a short rest, and on lifts with a high systemic cost (squats and deadlifts more than a machine press). 225 lb squats for 8 rated Just right, with 150 s of rest, expect 6 next and 7 after three minutes.
  - Easy bumps the weight, or the reps when load steps are coarse, from what's left after the rest.
  - Just right holds the weight and aims for the reps the rest gives back ("Same weight, aim for 6: reps fall a bit as the sets add up"). At the bottom of the range it takes a little off instead, to stay in the range.
  - Missed holds the weight while the reps, with a rep in hand, stay in the range ("Missed reps are just data. Same weight, aim for 5"), and drops slightly below it.
  - The sets after the next one carry on falling as if each comes out as aimed, never shown below the range; each is worked out again as the one before is logged.
  - A rest cut short (skipped, or ended by a recovered heart rate) works the next set out again for the rest taken ("A shorter rest, so aim for 5."). A rest that runs long changes nothing. The rest before a set is counted from the exercise's last set, so a skipped timer still records it.
  - Finding-weight sets aim for the middle of the slot's range (7 for a 5–8 squat, 10 for an 8–12 curl, 13 for 10–15), where the sessions after aim, and keep moving until a set rates Just right; the next one then holds that weight with the reps falling.
  - With low trust, verification pushes run on safe setups only: machines, or a rack with safeties. They ask for the reps the rest gives back, one step up.
- **Progression and stalls** (`Progression`, `Stalls`)
  - The target reps in reserve fall across the block, RP-style.
  - A block's first week comes down to its target. It starts with more in reserve than the last block ended (and more reps when a size block follows a strength one), so the weight isn't held within a step of the last block's heaviest, as it is on an off day later in a block.
  - Fine increments aim for the middle of the slot's range every session and progress by load. Coarse ones (dumbbells, stacks, bodyweight) use double progression.
  - A missed first set holds the next session back, and so do two later misses that fatigue doesn't explain (more than a rep short of what the set before left after its rest). Reps falling set to set don't.
  - Deloads and injury ramps adjust the targets. A deload during a ramp scales the load actually lifted, so an assisted lift gets more help, not less, and loads stay on the gym's steps.
  - A session is the sets logged with no gap of more than three hours, so a workout that runs past midnight is one session.
  - A stall is three or more weeks without a new best. It's read by phase: expected in a cut, a recovery problem when beat up, a programming problem otherwise. Each reading comes with a progressive-overload prompt, built on the top set of the last session that wasn't a deload.
- **Failure tiers** (`FailurePolicy`)
  - Safe to fail from day one.
  - Fail with setup once earned, and only with pins.
  - Never to failure for some lifts.
  - Rep-outs are capped per session and concentrated in the assessment and check-ins. Low-trust lifters stop free-weight rep-outs at form breakdown.
  - There are no rep-outs while a lift ramps back after an injury, check-ins included.
- **Fatigue Budget** (`FatigueBudget`). Hidden, never shown as a level.
  - Systemic and local cost per set, with failure multipliers (deadlifts are high).
  - Martial arts and other outside training spend the same budget.
  - Sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, soreness, joint pain, energy, performance trend and life stress all feed it.
  - "Beat up and stalled" reduces systemic cost before total work. Stacked signals call an early deload, but only after at least two loading weeks.
- **Scheduler and life modes** (`BlockScheduler`, `LifeModes`)
  - Blocks are 5+1 weeks by default, 3–8 by context: shorter in a cut, longer in a bulk and for beginners.
  - Vacations reflow the block: near the deload they become it; early in a block they cut it short. A trip that starts during the deload keeps the deload's start and stretches its end through the trip.
  - Crunch mode (time, equipment, food dials), injury mode (joints and severity, with a re-entry ramp) and illness mode (above the neck vs systemic).
  - Re-entry weeks, and the system-wide `SystemCalendar` of deloads.
  - A deleted life mode is kept as a tombstone and shared like an edit. The newest edit or delete of each period wins, so a vacation deleted in one app stays deleted in the others and on the person's other devices.
  - A period must end on or after its start, an injury has to say where it is, and only an injury or illness can be open-ended.
- **Discipline**: a rolling four-week adherence rate.
  - The target is the 80–90% band. Above it earns nothing extra, and one miss barely dents it.
  - Rest is part of the plan. "Didn't log" is unknown, not failure.
  - Vacation, illness and exempt crunch days never count.
- **Endurance, Agility and Physique**
  - Endurance: heart-rate zones, heart-rate-guided rest with minimums for heavy compounds, heart-rate recovery between sets, benchmarks.
  - Agility: five mobility tests, with the weakest becoming the limiter (a tie goes to the same test every time), and "see a physio" when a test hurts or worsens.
  - Physique: shoulder-to-waist and waist-to-height ratios, a tape body-fat estimate as a soft signal, and gaps described in neutral words. The body weight it uses is the one measured last: Health's, or one typed on the tape on a later day.
- **Steps** (`Steps`): a daily aim, never a stat or a streak.
  - It starts from your usual count (the four weeks before the current phase began), not a universal 10,000. Days under 1,000 steps (the phone on a desk) and illness days are unknown.
  - In a cut it heads for your usual plus about 2,000, at least the floor (8,000, or 7,000 from 60, where the benefit levels off in large cohort studies); a usual count over 12,000 is held. Otherwise it holds the usual count, lifted to the floor if it's under, and nothing over 10,000 is prescribed.
  - Each calendar week's aim is last week's average plus 1,000, never under the usual count and never past the target, so a missed week doesn't raise the next.
  - It's judged as the last seven days' average. It asks for something (a walk) only when there's something to do: always in a cut, otherwise while building to the floor or when steps slip more than 1,000 under the aim.

## ExerciseLibrary

The partial library from the first pass (258 exercises, every id kept) is now fully tagged, plus mobility drills, dynamic warm-ups, static stretches and conditioning pieces. Each entry carries:

- movement pattern
- muscles
- joints loaded, with stress levels
- systemic and local fatigue, stimulus and the failure multiplier
- safety tier
- strength-category mapping (free weights only)
- load basis
- default rest and reps by goal

Injury filtering is deterministic: an exercise is excluded when it stresses the injured joint at or above a threshold set by severity, and the person can add exercises back or take more out. `Scripts/build_library.py` regenerates `library.json` from frozen source tags.

## SystemStore

Each app owns its own **SwiftData + CloudKit** database: one generic `RecordRow`, one JSON payload per record, CloudKit-compatible. On top of that it provides:

- **Records:** schema versioning and step-by-step migration from day one, newest-wins dedupe of rows CloudKit delivers twice, and soft deletes that sync. Changes that sync in while an app is open are announced (`observeChanges`), so Stadig Lift shows them without a relaunch, and a local save updates only its own kind of record in the cache.
- **The shared App Group container** (`group.dev.svindland.stadig`): versioned envelopes, coordinated writes, and change notifications across apps. It holds the profile, life modes, the calendar, each app's summary, Stadig Macro's nutrition summary, and what the hub app left ([shared-container.md](shared-container.md)). Life modes are at schema 2, with deletes as tombstones. A summary or calendar is rewritten only when its content changes.
- **Backups:** the full backup file and restore (merge, newest wins), and CSV export. A backup restores only into the app that wrote it: another app's is refused before anything is written. CSV fields that start like a formula get a leading apostrophe.
- **Sync state and a second device:** A second device, or a reinstall, that finds the app not set up asks iCloud once whether the app has records there (`ICloudArrival`, SystemStore): the account, then one record id from the store's zone. Nothing there, no account, or no answer within 10 seconds goes straight to onboarding, after a blank moment rather than a flash of the wait. Records there show "Bringing your training from iCloud" instead of onboarding until this launch's first import has ended; the data arriving opens Today, and a failure to set up or import ends the wait with its reason under the spinner. "Set up as new" skips the wait. Settings' Sync row follows CloudKit's own events (`SyncState`): "Syncing…", "Up to date" with when it last synced, or "Not syncing" with the reason in words (not signed in, storage full, iCloud turning records down because its schema hasn't got them), and the shared setup's state when that's the part failing.

## HealthBridge

- **Reads** body weight, height, sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, VO₂ max, heart-rate samples, daily steps (merged across iPhone and Watch the way Health shows them), and workouts other apps wrote. Those workouts are mapped to activity types with an estimated intensity and imported as outside activities.
- **Writes** strength workouts (keyed by sync id, so edits replace them), waist and body fat. Deleting a measurement removes its waist and body-fat samples, and clearing a field removes that one.
- **On watchOS**, `LiveWorkoutSession` streams heart rate. A workout whose mirroring to the iPhone fails is discarded, not left running.
- **Under tests**, `HealthBridge.shared.disable()` turns Health off for the process: no prompts, empty reads, no writes.

The program generator works like this:

- **Split** by days per week.
- **Exercise selection**
  - The scored classic lift goes in main slots.
  - Stimulus is weighed against fatigue on a log scale, so cheap isolation can't crowd everything else out.
  - Exercises that are working stay. Stalled ones rotate out.
- **Volume** by experience and phase, plus priorities. These come from the person's chosen focus, strength imbalances, physique gaps, the mobility limiter and lagging endurance, and auto-prioritisation can be switched off.
- **Fatigue Budget pass**
  - It spends the systemic budget on high-SFR options, swapping the costliest lifts before trimming sets.
  - Declared classes spend the same budget.
  - Cheap local volume is added for lagging physique areas.
- **Elastic time budget**: each block has a minimum, a desired length and a priority, and goals reorder the priorities.
  - **30 minutes:** ramp-up sets are the warm-up, the limiter drill happens in the rests, two compounds are supersetted, and conditioning becomes a quest.
  - **60 minutes:** five easy minutes on a bike or rower and three drills, the limiter slot, the main lifts, a few accessories and brief conditioning.
  - **90 minutes:** a fuller warm-up, more sets and a 20–30 minute conditioning finisher.
  - Time the lifting doesn't use goes to more sets first (main lifts up to four work sets, accessories up to three), but only as far as the week's recovery budget allows, then to conditioning; no lift is ever a single set. When the budget is what keeps a session short, it says so ("About 45 minutes today, under your 60…") rather than leaving the hour feeling unfinished.
  - **Every scored lift each week:** when the budget would leave one out of the whole week (a third scored lift behind two main lifts at strength rests), the first day that had it fits it again, ahead of the cheaper accessories after it but behind a priority put first: at its sets if conditioning's minutes above its minimum make room, else at two sets, else that day's conditioning becomes a quest and the time it frees gives the main lifts their sets back first. The slot and the Why? say so. Plans that already train every scored lift don't change, and "Short on time?" doesn't do this.
- **"Short on time today?"** refits the day to 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes. A refit weighs the same fatigue, priorities and recommendations as a new block and gives the same day each time. Lengthening a 30-minute day undoes its supersets and the drills in its rests.

**Session planning** turns a day into exact sets:

- warm-ups before every lift, main or accessory, the way it's loaded (`Warmup`):
  - **On a bar** (barbell, trap bar, EZ bar): the empty bar first, then up to three even jumps of about 30 kg at most, then the working weight for two to four reps (about half the work's). 140 kg squats for 5 go 20 × 5, 50 × 5, 80 × 5, 110 × 3, 140 × 3; a 405 lb deadlift warms up at 45, 135, 225 and 315 lb; work with the bar alone is tried once with it.
  - **Plate-loaded and Smith machines** (leg press, hack squat): a plate a side more each set, the biggest plate that goes on at least once under the work, up to four sets spread evenly, every one lighter than the work. A 360 lb leg press goes 90, 180, 270, then the work.
  - **Isolation work, and the accessories on dumbbells, cables and machines:** one set of two to four reps at the working weight, to see that it feels right. Dumbbell, cable and machine lifts that lead a day climb at about half and three quarters of the work (45, 65 and 82% from 60 kg).
  - Bodyweight, assisted and timed work warm up by doing. With no working weight yet, the lifts that climb climb toward a guess at it, on the light side, and the rest go straight to finding it.
  - Fewer reps the nearer a set gets to the work (8, 5, 3), never more than the work asks. Warm-ups aren't rated, rest 45 seconds, and the last one before the work rests half the work's rest (at least 45 seconds). A swap before anything of the exercise is done or skipped warms the new exercise up its own way.
  - The elastic time budget doesn't count them yet (a main compound still gets 2½ minutes, an accessory none), so a day's estimate runs over its minutes by about a minute for each warm-up it didn't have before; see `decisions.md`.
- rep-outs placed on safe-to-fail anchors first, judged on the exercise actually done after any swap, so a stand-in that isn't safe to fail gets none
- deloads keep Zone 2 and mobility
- check-ins land at the end of the deload
- crunch mode gives the minimum effective dose, with equipment stand-ins
- injuries swap out what they must, and injury ramps ease lifts back in
- a head cold means light work only
- a gym with a sauna or steam room gets an optional session at the end, outside the session's time. Setup asks (no preset assumes one), and Settings → Gym → Recovery changes it. Changing the gym's kind keeps the rooms, and the exercises taken out or added back. Rooms run hot and cold, so you read the thermometer and the minutes follow: the dose is minutes weighted by heat (twice as hard every 6 °C / 11 °F hotter, so 10 minutes at 190 °F is more than 20 at 175 °F), starting at ten minutes' worth at 80 °C (176 °F), capped by how hot the room is, and moved by how the last one felt (easy +15%, too much −20%, two just-rights in a row +5%). Time, temperature, rating and heart rate are kept apart from the lifting, so they never count as sets or calibrate ratings.

**Analysis** produces the "Do this next" actions. Each one ends in a concrete step, e.g. "Next session, 82.5 kg for 3×5" for a stall. When Stadig Macro data exists, it adds cross-app context such as "Protein was under target 4 of the last 7 logged days." Strength trends read each lift by its own load basis, so less help on an assisted lift counts as progress. It also produces the day's quests (three at most), the training discipline score, and the summary published for Stadig Macro and the stats. With a fast running in Stadig Macro (published while its meal timing is on), a strength session still to do today, outside a deload, gets "Lifting on a long fast" once the fast is a day old, or will be by the usual start (the middle of the last eight sessions' start times) while its goal still runs: eat a meal with protein an hour or two before, or keep the session light.

**Steps** show on the Today tab whenever there's an aim (on the phone a row, today against the aim on a ruler, that opens the full card): today so far against it, the last seven days and their average, where it's heading and why. Without one, the card asks to connect Health (or waits for a week of steps). On a rest day with the week behind, a quest offers the gap as a brisk walk ("20-minute walk"), but not on lift or class days, while ill, with a hip, knee or ankle injury, or once today reaches the aim. A long day on foot nobody logged (15,000 steps and 7,000 over the usual) spends the Fatigue Budget as walking; a logged hike counts once, as the hike. The last eight weeks of steps and the aim are published for Stadig Macro. Health is asked again for steps only by people who already connected it.

**Life modes** are added from Today and Plan. In the editor, moving "From" past "Until" moves the end with it, "Until" offers only days from the start on, and Save stays off for a period that can't apply. "Until it's better" (no end yet) is offered only for an injury or illness; a vacation or crunch another app saved with no end shows "Until", so it can be given one. "End it now" ("It's better: ramp back in" for an injury, "I'm better" for an illness) ends a period yesterday. One that started today is called off if it's a vacation or crunch; an injury or illness keeps today, and its ramp or lighter week back starts tomorrow. A period still to come has nothing to end: Delete cancels it.

## The Watch

A session is logged on the wrist whichever device started it. Started on the Watch, the Watch runs it; started on the iPhone, the Watch logs into the phone's. Either way it's the same screens and the same `LiveSession` engine:

- the set face: the exercise and where the set sits (a bar for each set), the set as one big "70 × 7" (tap either number to give it the crown, which sets the load, snapped to what the gym can make, or the reps), and a band of the accent at the foot. On a rated set the band is **Just right** and Double Tap logs it so, the rating the phone's dock fills too; its ⌃⌄ half opens the rating face. A warm-up's or a timed set's band is Done, a rep-out's Log reps. The set's note, the hint and the crown's toggle scroll below
- the rating face: Easy, Just right (in the accent, and Double Tap) and Missed as three big stacked zones; a miss asks how many reps were done
- the rest face: the accent rising from the foot of the screen toward go, the countdown huge, heart rate small, the next set, and Go now (Double Tap); what the last rating changed, the drill for the rest, ±15 s and the heart-rate note scroll below. Rest ends early once heart rate recovers (accessories only)
- a timed set (mobility, a hold, a carry): its seconds, Start, then the countdown large over a fill draining toward Done; a carry's load reads "24 kg for 40 seconds"
- conditioning with heart-rate zones
- the exercise list: Do This Now, Swap Exercise (the gym's substitutes, minus what an injury rules out or was set aside, as on the phone), Add a Set, skip a set or an exercise
- a quick feedback screen, then the summary: the session and its minutes, what moved (each lift whose current estimated one-rep max it raised, before and after, from the history the phone sent: `EstimateMove`), the sets and their ratings

**Started on the Watch.** The iPhone keeps the Watch's application context current: today's plan and the session after it in the program, the gym, trust, injuries and set-aside exercises, and recent history for both plans' exercises and their likely swaps. The Watch runs the session on its own, so the phone can stay in a locker or at home. The finished session comes back to the phone both live and queued. The Watch's workout session saves it to Health. A Watch session left unsaved for more than six hours (the Watch died or restarted mid-session) is sent to the phone as it stood at its last logged change, without feedback, rather than dropped. A file the Watch can't read is renamed `<name>.unreadable-<seconds>.json` rather than written over.

Home offers the phone's plan. When that plan is from an earlier day and was already done on this Watch (the phone hasn't been heard from since), it offers the session after it instead, "next in your program", so training without the phone can go on. The complication shows the next session. Once that session's day has gone by, it says to open Stadig Lift on the iPhone. Whenever the Watch app comes back on screen with no context or an old one, it asks the phone for today's, and keeps asking, live and queued, until a newer one arrives.

**Started on the iPhone.** Starting the session asks the Watch for a workout (`HKHealthStore.startWatchApp`). The Watch shows heart rate while it mirrors the workout back (`startMirroringToCompanionDevice`) and asks the phone for the session, and then shows the session itself. Each thing done on the Watch (a set logged and rated, a set or exercise skipped, a set added, a swap, Do This Now, a rest skipped or changed, conditioning) is a `SessionChange`: the Watch makes it to its copy at once and numbers it, and the phone makes it to the session and sends the session back (without heart rate, compressed). Items go by id, so a reorder on either device can't misplace a set. The Watch sends every change the phone hasn't taken yet, in order, with each new change and again every 2 seconds; the phone takes only the next number (`LiveSession.take`, `takenChanges`), so a change that arrives twice, or ahead of one still on its way (a set logged on a set just added), is never taken twice or out of order, however long the phone was out of reach. The Watch shows its changes on top of any copy from the phone that hasn't taken them (`MirroredSession`), and drops copies older than the one it has. Messages go by both the mirrored workout and WatchConnectivity, and none is waited on (a send over a workout can hang; the Watch stops using that route while three sends there are out, each for at most ten seconds, and heart rate then goes the other way). The phone sends copies a quarter of a second after a change, so a burst of changes sends one. Once the Watch has joined, the phone keeps talking to it over WatchConnectivity when the mirrored workout drops (out of range for a minute), and the Watch starts mirroring again. A late end or copy of a session the Watch has already left is ignored, so it can't end the next one's workout. A set logged on both devices is corrected, not adjusted twice. The phone's session screen follows along, since its logger is the session's current set. A rest whose time is up moves the Watch on to the next set without ending the rest on the phone (the next set's rest is timed from it). The sauna or steam room is logged on the phone, where the thermometer is read; the Watch says so and offers Skip.

Ending the session on the Watch (feedback, then Save Session, or Discard) asks the phone, which saves it with that feedback, once the sets logged on the Watch have landed. The Watch shows "Saving on your iPhone" until the phone says it's done, then the summary ("Saved in Stadig Lift on your iPhone"); Leave It to iPhone stops waiting. Finishing on the phone takes the Watch off the session too, with the same summary. **Continue on iPhone** (in the session's options) stops the Watch's heart rate and leaves the session to the phone.

The phone shows live bpm in the session header, zones against the aim in the warm-up and conditioning (logged as minutes per zone), and ends heart-rate-guided rests early. At the end the Watch saves the workout under the session's id, with heart rate and energy, and the phone doesn't save a second one. That message goes over the mirrored workout alone, so a copy by the other route can't have the Watch save while the phone, its own send failing, saves too. If it doesn't get there within 15 seconds (out of range, the Watch's workout already over, or a send that hangs), the phone saves the workout itself and tells the Watch, the other way, to throw its own away. Stopping on the Watch, or the system ending its workout (another workout app starting, say), discards the Watch's workout, and the phone saves the workout to Health itself at the end. When the session has nothing to keep, or Save to Health is off, the phone tells the Watch to discard its workout; the Watch still shows the summary when the session was kept.

The Watch's DEBUG launch arguments (`Watch/Model/SampleSession.swift`): `-sampleContext` seeds a small context; `-sampleScreen set|rating|rest|timed|conditioning|finish|saving` starts a sample session (without HealthKit) on that screen (`saving` saves it after a bench set heavier than last week's, to show the summary with what moved), `-sampleScreen home` shows Home, and `-sampleScreen phone` shows the sample session as if the iPhone ran it (`phone-set`, `phone-rating`, `phone-rest`, `phone-finish` and `phone-saving`, the wait after Save, move it on; with no phone to answer, what's logged stays pending); `-largeText` previews an accessibility text size.

**In the gym:** "Do this now" on any exercise moves it (and its superset partner) ahead when the next machine is taken. Tapping a logged set corrects its weight, reps or rating, and the sets still to come are worked out again from the corrected log. Undo in the rest (on the phone, the floor, the iPad's scoreboard, the Mac) takes back the set just logged, as if it never was. Tapping the weight types it in, and holding − or + keeps stepping. With no history, the weight starts from the strength standards (scored lifts) or a bodyweight ratio for the movement and equipment, scaled for experience and sex, so a 400 lb leg press isn't fifty taps from an empty sled. A typed weight over 1,500 kg is refused. Swapping an exercise partway through keeps the sets already logged under the exercise they were done on (in the session, its record, the analysis and the CSV export), and the replacement's first weight never comes from the exercise it replaced. "Add a set" isn't offered on conditioning or the sauna and steam room, so a second bout can't overwrite the first. When a rest's time runs out, the logger comes up for the next set, and the minimised session and the Live Activity move on with it. A heart-rate recovery only ends a rest that's still running.

**Finishing:** a session with nothing logged saves nothing and shows no summary. One with only a sauna or steam room in it is kept, and counts as the day's session. A session's day is the day where it was recorded, so travelling afterwards doesn't move it. A session left open for more than six hours is saved as it stood, ending at its last logged change, and its summary shows at the next launch. A session in progress survives an app update that adds fields to it; a `live-session.json` that can't be read is moved aside to `live-session.unreadable.json`.

## DEBUG launch arguments

- `-seedSampleData` fills an empty store with a lifter three weeks into the assessment. Every session runs through the real planner, live session and autoregulation.
- `-inMemoryStore` keeps nothing between launches. The store lives in memory and never syncs, and the shared container, the session in progress and the widget snapshot go to a fresh temporary folder. So a sample run no longer reads or writes what the other apps publish (Stadig Macro's phase), and the home-screen widget doesn't show it. It still uses Health and can ask for permissions.
- `-uiTesting` is what the UI tests launch with: everything `-inMemoryStore` does, plus no Health, no permission prompts and no animations. There are no rest notifications, Live Activity, Watch connection or StoreKit either. The other arguments work alongside it.
- `-resetData` erases the store and the session in progress first.
- `-access undecided|free|lapsed|extended|subscribed|bought` is still read by `SystemPurchases` in DEBUG, but with Stadig Lift free it changes nothing on screen.
- `-gymSauna` gives the gym a sauna, for the recovery screen.
- `-startSession` starts today's session. `-sessionAt lift|set|rest|summary` then moves it on: to its first main lift (skipping what comes before it), past that lift's warm-ups to its first work set, into the rest after that set, or through the main lifts, every set Just right, to the finished session's summary.
- `-scoreboard` shows the iPad's scoreboard whichever way the iPad is turned (unless its toggle is off), for a simulator left in portrait: `-startSession -sessionAt set -scoreboard` shows a set on the board, `-sessionAt rest` the rest with Undo.
- `-floorMode` keeps Floor mode on whatever the motion sensors say (the simulator has none), on an iPhone with a session on screen: `-startSession -sessionAt set -floorMode` shows a set on the floor, `-sessionAt rest` the rest.
- `-tab plan|program|library|history|progress|body` opens a destination (program, library and history are the sidebar's; in a narrow window they show the tab that reaches them).
- `-exercise <id>` opens Library on that exercise (`-exercise barbell-bench-press`); in a narrow window, the exercise's page on Plan.
- `-openSettings` opens Settings: the sheet on the iPhone, the iPad and Vision Pro, the Settings window on the Mac.
- `-simulateICloudData` shows the wait a second device shows while its training comes down from iCloud, over a store that never gets any ("Set up as new" leaves it for onboarding).

`SIM_DEBUG=1 swift test --filter simulationReport` in `Packages/SystemCore` prints every simulated lifter's sessions and calibration evidence. `PRINT_PROGRAMS=1 swift test` in `Packages/LiftEngine` prints generated programs.

Run them with `Scripts/build.sh unit lift` (the app's tests and its packages' tests) and `Scripts/build.sh ui lift`, on the checkout's own test simulator (see [testing.md](testing.md)). After a build, `Scripts/leaks.sh lift` runs `leaks` on the seeded app on Today, Plan, Progress, Body and a started session.
