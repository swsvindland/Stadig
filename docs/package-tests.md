# Tests: the logic is the product

The app tests, and how to run everything, are in [testing.md](testing.md).

- **SystemCore (112 tests).** Includes simulated users trained for 12 weeks through the real progression, autoregulation and calibration code:
  - An **honest novice** earns trust (0.3 → 0.73), and estimates track true strength within 12%.
  - A **sandbagger** is caught in week one. Machine rep-outs pin their real strength within 10%; their barbell estimate stays conservative but in range (above 80%).
  - An **overconfident veteran** loses trust (0.7 → 0.52), and estimates don't run ahead of reality.
  - A **plateaued veteran** gets a stall that reads as a programming problem at maintenance, expected in a cut, and a recovery problem when beat up.
  - A **growing lifter** is never flagged as stalled.
  - Also covered: strength standards, autoregulation for every rating, assisted exercises, finding weights, deloads, injury ramps, vacation reflow, early deloads, life-mode effects, the discipline band, Fatigue Budget states, physique, agility, rest rules and quests.
  - Steps: the usual count from before the phase (desk and illness days left out), a cut's target and its 1,000-a-week climb, holding outside a cut, when the aim shows, long days on foot, and a fall in steps against the month before.
  - Private Cloud Compute counts as entitled only when the Info.plist sets `SystemAIPrivateCloud` to true (not false, a string or nothing), and the test runner isn't.
  - Days: a phone set to the Buddhist, Japanese, Hebrew, Islamic or Persian calendar still counts Gregorian days (ordinals, weekdays, ISO strings, 400 days in a row and the way back to a date); days follow the time zone over three years at 6-hour steps in four zones, through New York's clock changes and São Paulo's skipped midnight in 2018; only real dates parse ("2026-02-30" and a 19-digit year, month or day don't), and a corrupt timestamp lands on 1970 instead of crashing. A time on the clock (07:00, 23:30) keeps its time and its day across New York's 2027 clock changes, a skipped 02:30 comes out as 03:30, and the Buddhist and Japanese calendars give the same instants.
  - Units: only the US defaults to pounds and inches (the UK gets kilograms and centimetres), and the shared least-squares slope gives nothing for fewer than two points, mismatched lengths or every x the same.
  - Progression and the schedule: a deload during an injury ramp scales the load actually lifted (bench at 87.5 kg stays on 2.5 kg steps; an assisted pull-up with 20 kg of help at 80 kg gets more help, not less); no rep-out while a lift ramps back, at a check-in or in a loading week; a workout past midnight is one session; after a deload the stall prompt still builds on the last real session (stalled at 100 kg, deloaded at 87.5: 102.5 next); a vacation that starts inside the deload keeps the deload and only stretches its end, and a trip during an early deload, or two overlapping trips, stretch it to cover them; an injury's overrides toggle between allowed and set aside without listing an exercise twice.
  - Scoring and stats: each training priority appears once (legs ahead and pressing ahead of pulling merge, emphasis summed, both reasons kept); every strength ratio table is complete and rising; a mobility tie goes to the first test in all 24 orders of the results; FFMI uses the current weight over one typed long ago; trajectories for goals that go down and goals past their deadline; the heart-rate recovery trend.
  - Supplement labels: a tablespoon or teaspoon serving isn't a tablet, and a macro line with no unit counts as grams while vitamins and misreads stay out. A birth year that makes someone under 10 or over 100 gives no age, so the apps' default applies.
  - Shared contracts: golden JSON in the shape the apps write today, with SystemJSON's settings (whole milliseconds since 1970, sorted keys), for Day, DayRange, LoggedSet, TrainingBlock (with and without its overrides), SystemCalendar and SharedProfile, plus a fully filled AppSummary round trip. A change that stops one decoding needs a schema bump and a migration, or an optional field. Stored names don't follow renames in the code: the 2.4 km run is still "run2_4k".
  - Life modes across apps: a delete is kept as a tombstone that beats an older live copy in either order, a newer edit beats an older delete, millisecond ties settle the same way everywhere, the merge comes out in a fixed order, and a corrupt timestamp doesn't crash it; the engines ignore tombstones, a deleted injury ramping nothing back afterwards; editors save only what makes sense (no end before the start, an open end only for injury and illness, an injury with a part), a trip or crunch with no end gets a week, and moving the start past the end carries the end; periods written before tombstones (schema 1, no `deletedAt`) still decode, and live ones write the old shape.
  - Launch options: flags and values (a negative number is a value, a flag after a flag isn't); `-inMemoryStore` keeping the store in memory without going quiet, in DEBUG builds; and both `-uiTesting` and the unit-test host keeping everything in memory and quiet.
- **LiftEngine (87 tests).**
  - Every day count from 1 to 6, and every scored lift covered each week.
  - The easy bike gets its five minutes and the drills fit the rest; no lift gets a single set at 45 to 90 minutes, and an hour and a half is more lifting than an hour.
  - "Do this now" (with superset partners), a mistyped weight or rating corrected with the next sets following it, starting weights in range for leg press, dumbbell bench, pulldown and squat, the sauna or steam room only where the gym has one, and minutes per heart-rate zone.
  - Sauna dose: 10 minutes at 190 °F outweighs 20 at 175 °F, today's minutes follow today's thermometer within sensible caps, each rating moves the next session, and heat is recorded apart from sets. Four BJJ sessions a week mean fewer lifting sets, and a short day says why.
  - 30, 60 and 90-minute budgets. Endurance goals rank conditioning higher.
  - Hotel gyms, injuries, "legs ahead means upper body first", and beat-up-and-stalled swaps.
  - Physique gaps, declared classes, crunch mode, swaps, gym loads and CSV export.
  - The step aim from a cut's start, the walk quest and when it stays away, a long unlogged day spending the budget once, and the published steps.
  - A full four-week assessment that ends with all working weights found, seven lifts scored within about 3% of the simulated lifter's true strength, and the next block prioritising the lagging upper body with its "Why?".
  - Sessions: a swap partway through an exercise keeps each exercise's sets under its own id (in the record, the analysis, the CSV and edits after the swap, and in records already saved as one mixed log); rep-outs go by the stand-in actually done, so a never-to-failure stand-in gets none; a set added after a rep-out is an ordinary hard set at the weight just used; "Add a set" does nothing for conditioning or recovery items; a session is kept when anything at all was done in it (lifting, conditioning, mobility or heat); a heart-rate recovery counts only while the rest is still running; "Do this now" on a superset behind an open lift alternates the pair with the 20 s changeover, rests once both are done, then goes back to the first lift; crunch mode's gym is the same on every call; a long session keeps the last 4,000 heart-rate readings; a session's day stays in the zone it was recorded in (23:30 in Pago Pago), and older records read in the phone's zone.
  - Blocks: a next block accepted during the deload waits for its start, with the current block, deload, check-in and calendar kept, and a start planned twice goes to the later plan; the early deload is offered only after two loading weeks, lands today even when a vacation pushed the block's start back, and reaches the plan and the calendar only once taken; vacations move today's sessions and the assessment week as they move the calendar; adherence at the four-week window's edge, no classes counted on the start day, and assisted pull-ups trending up.
  - Fitting the day: refitting a 30-minute day to an hour clears its supersets and rest filler and adds a limiter, refitting twice gives the same day (ids and cool-down included), the cool-down starts with the main lifts' muscles, and refitting back to 30 brings the pairing back; four BJJ classes a week swap a costly unscored lift for a cheaper one before any sets are trimmed, and one trimmed set still gets its reason.
  - Gym loads: belt loads start at a single zero; plates per side match the old exhaustive search for every 1.25 kg from 20 to 150 kg and every 5 lb from 45 to 315 lb; 1,500 kg, the most anyone lifts, is 29 plates of 25 kg and one of 15 a side, and anything past it, infinity or NaN gives none; an 82,500-tonne slipped load rounds, steps and fills the crown from bounded tables in kg and lb, and a limit of minus infinity doesn't crash; switching a gym's kind keeps its id, name, sauna, steam room and exceptions.
  - Body weight: today's tape beats a Health weight from months ago, for strength and physique alike; a same-day Health weight beats the tape, an older tape entry loses, a Health weight with no date stands as current, and with neither the guess is used.
  - Saved sessions: frozen JSON fixtures in `Tests/LiftEngineTests/Fixtures`, written once by this version: `live-session.2026-10.json` (strength, superset, mobility, conditioning and sauna items, a running rest, heart-rate samples and quests) and `session-records.2026-10.json` (a full record with a swap, zones, heat, feedback and time zone, and a minimal older-style one). Each decodes and re-encodes with every saved key and value back, and the restored session logs its next set and records it. An older shape gets each missing field's default, a rest timer that can't be read is dropped, and with no items the session rebuilds from its plan. The fixtures are never regenerated: a new shape gets a file of its own and the old files stay.
  - Deleted life modes: a deleted vacation never reaches the analysis. Two hub priorities naming one target come out as one.
  - A gym edited on an old copy: the visit's changes merge onto the newest gym item by item (equipment, fixtures, exceptions), and the unit moves together with its loads.
  - The planning screens: a slot's sets week by week end in a deload that matches what `Progression` prescribes for one to six sets; the block's calendar marks training days (none on a vacation day), days trained, the deload, the check-in and today, and a block cut short leaves its last days outside; an exercise's own history counts sessions, keeps one best set per session (never a warm-up), ranks timed work by reps, and its trend and change follow the estimates; History's rows put an activity's source and effort beside a session's device, work sets and top set (by estimate, never a warm-up), and search names and exercises; reordering a session lands a dragged item where it's dropped, moves a superset whole and never between two partners, and leaves a running rest alone.
- **ExerciseLibrary (39), SystemStore (48) and HealthBridge (20)** cover the data, migrations, dedupe, backups and mappings.
  - ExerciseLibrary's filters: each narrows on its own tag, together they all apply, a muscle puts its primary movers first, "easy on" a joint keeps only light or no stress, "in my gym" and "safe with my injuries" follow the gym's and the injury's overrides, a search still ranks within them, and the menus offer only patterns and equipment that exist.
  - ExerciseLibrary: one typo forgiven (two letters swapped, or one dropped, added or changed, at either end too) but not two, the 5-letter floor only on the library's word, a match that drops a word it can't place isn't a confident one, and a custom exercise sharing a bundled id searched by its own words.
  - SystemStore's cache: a write touches only its own kind, counted in decodes over 1,000 notes; a save or delete patches the kind's list in place (one decode per saved record, the same order and content as reading the rows, over duplicate CloudKit rows and a v1 row too), while batches of more than 32, restores, erases, remote changes and failed saves read it again. The echo of a store's own save isn't a remote change; a burst of writes from a second store on the same file gives one observer call and one revision bump, and releasing the token stops the calls.
  - SystemStore's family sync (FamilySync), with two devices over one database: a file written on one reaches the other and doesn't bounce back, the newer copy wins in both directions, a file at a newer schema on disk is never replaced, a path that would leave the container is skipped without holding up the rest, only envelopes go up, every app uploading the same path writes one record, and starting up brings down what's there and sends up what's new. `deletedIDs` names only real deletions: not a record from a newer app version, not one that was never there, and not one saved again after its delete.
  - SystemStore's backups and files: another app's backup is refused before anything is written; a shared-file failure after the records were saved still refreshes; async export and import; payloads survive a backup byte for byte (non-ASCII text, Int64 above 2^53, 1e-7, explicit null, base64-only data); bulk saves keep the usual rules (older writes ignored, duplicates converge, a newer schema left alone); a save in a tombstone's millisecond wins, so a delete then a save under one id keeps the save; `writeIfChanged` skips content that only differs by sub-millisecond dates and upgrades an older file; CSV fields that start like a formula get a leading apostrophe, numbers such as -5 and +2.5 don't; `SharedContainer(directory:)` uses its folder as given. A life-mode tombstone travels through the shared file, and a file written at schema 1 still reads.
  - HealthBridge: a disabled bridge is empty everywhere (reads empty, writes skipped, no observers) on every platform; dietary mapping (macros win over same-named micros, units and types match the catalog keys); sleep nights across clock changes; the same age under six non-Gregorian calendars; food writes for one id run in the order asked, so an undo waits for its save. The anchored weigh-in read keeps additions from other sources since the start date, oldest first, drops a sample also reported deleted, round-trips its anchor, starts over from one it can't read, and gives nothing (never "everything deleted") when Health is unavailable or disabled.
- **NutritionEngine (100 tests).** Simulated eaters with a known true maintenance, who log most days (some partly), weigh in most mornings with noisy scales and a week-one water drop, and occasionally misread:
  - After four weeks, maintenance lands within about 150 kcal on average, and the honest range holds the truth in 38–40 of 40 runs at every noise level tried (33–38 of 40 at three weeks, when the formula guess was far off).
  - The trend follows a steady loss within 0.1 kg; a plain 7-day average would sit about 0.8 kg high.
  - Weekly check-ins walk an overestimate of 2,800 down to the true 2,400 in about four weeks, never moving targets more than 150 kcal at once.
  - Steps falling from 11,000 to 6,000 in a cut hold maintenance once, with the reason, then the data leads; steady steps change nothing; and steps move the formula either side of 7,500.
  - Food quality: calorie shares by processing group, "fair" between the 20% aim and the 30% worth acting on, unknown under three complete days or when too little says how processed it is, fermented days counted once a day (a custom "kimchi" by its name, frozen yogurt never), and processing kept through copies, old saved entries and the CSV.
  - The phase planner's rules, including the doc's worked example (off a long cut with low maintenance: reverse diet, then a lean bulk), review triggers, target modes (diet break in a deload, vacation, illness, crunch, assessment), discipline unknowns and "Log again" ranking.
  - Supplements and drinks: a day's doses by time of day, with the pre-workout only on training days; every other day and chosen weekdays; doses scaled from the label's serving (one softgel of a two-softgel serving); a dose taken off the plan still showing on its day; supplements added to the week's vitamins unscaled by food coverage (3 µg vitamin D from half the calories plus a 25 µg multivitamin is 31 µg, not 56); a supplement alone enough to call zinc fine; vitamin D at 145 µg and 400 mg of supplemental magnesium flagged first, while 500 mg of magnesium from food isn't; supplement calories only on days with food; caffeine left at bedtime, a 400 → 200 mg cut-back in three weekly steps, and the week's average from days with caffeine logged; caffeine estimated for a latte or a Monster without a value, never for coffee cake; drinks told from foods named after them (chocolate milk and cold brew are drinks; coffee ice cream, oats cooked with milk and tuna in water aren't); and the supplement and water CSV tables; the diary, supplement and water tables write their times at local time on the matching day. Bedtime stays at 22:30 on both of 2026's clock-change days and is the same moment on a Buddhist calendar, and a bedtime after midnight falls on the next morning.
  - Check-ins across phases: a cut, a diet break and a cut again, a mini-cut then a bulk, and a late check-in each start the new phase at its own calories, not 150 kcal steps from the last. A misread holds one check-in, also when the reading after it arrives after a check-in has run; fortnightly or monthly readings on a steady loss aren't taken for misreads, while a spike by a gap still is. Of two check-ins on one day the newer is in force, and the check-ins the engine makes, a plan's first included, take the day's id, so two devices make one record.
  - Phase planning: ending a cut mid-week starts the diet break today, a mini-cut after a surplus starts today, a review past the plan's end plans afresh, a phase saved before early endings still reads, and "rising faster than planned" doesn't add its reason twice.
  - What's published and shown: each published day is judged by its own targets (partial days and today carry no hits), a vacation today doesn't unjudge the month before, and a rejected reading is never the weight. Today's actions: protein to go before and after 15:00 by the state's own clock, the weigh-in, logging yesterday, a plan ready, a whoosh in a deload diet break, "Plan finished" without Lift Track's calendar, and their order.
  - Swaps and one-tap fixes: a table of 21 foods, each given its own swap tip by whole words (milk chocolate and chocolate chip cookies don't get the drink tip; pastrami and sliced ham get the processed-meat one); the portion rule (the usual one unless it's over 300 kcal, otherwise the nearest to 150); the best fix per calorie, with a 1.3 bonus for your own foods; staples for every watched nutrient, the fermented ones counting as fermented.
  - Deleted life modes: a deleted illness is never seen.
  - History (the desktop's day table): a row a day from the start to today with the day's food, weight and trend; statuses as the analysis reads them; each day judged by its own targets (protein only in the assessment, the cut's calories after) and only complete days judged; the counted weigh-in; a vacation's days keeping its targets; sorting that puts days without a value last either way.
  - The wrist (Macro Track's Watch): calories and protein left against the targets the phone shows, the assessment's protein-only day, rings that stop at their target, Log Again ranked as on the phone (with yesterday's meal while nothing's logged near now), water only when drinks are tracked, the doses due by now, the weigh-in dial's start, step and unit, a snapshot past its day, and which settings are in force.

- **SystemAI (8 tests).** Light jobs on the device and heavy ones on Private Cloud Compute when both are there; each falling back to the other only for failures the other might not have (a refusal isn't retried elsewhere); words for every failure.
