- **How the apps are paid for.** On 2026-11-03 you asked for $30 an app, an optional $3-a-month subscription, 21 free days with no signup that the app keeps track of, chosen at the end of onboarding, and a prompt on opening after that with "just one more day", like WinRAR.   - **The subscription and the purchase unlock the same things.** Someone who buys while subscribed is told it's still running, with a way to cancel, because nothing else stops it charging.

  - **What building it takes, whatever the source.**
    - **A new file, `menus.db`.** Built by its own script, as `supplements.db` is, in the same v3 schema:
      - `brand` is the chain as it writes itself and `name` is the menu item. A size gets a row of its own wherever the recipe changes (a Grande isn't two Talls).
      - No barcode, and `markets` "us".
      - Values per 100 g where the chain gives grams, on a `serving` basis where it doesn't, with the item's own serving as its first portion.
      - `food_search`, `terms` and `catalog_meta` as now, plus an as-of date for each chain.
    - **Stable ids.** Use `menu:<chain>:<item>` or the source's own: there's no fdcId or barcode, and favourites and the meal planner look foods up by id across refreshes. An item that leaves the menu stays findable by id.
    - **A third `Catalog.Kind`.** It is ranked whole, like USDA, and searched by brand, like Open Food Facts. It needs a new `FoodSource` with its label. Favourites and custom foods store `Food`, and older builds don't decode an unknown source, so this needs a schema bump and a migration test.

The brands go through the same stop words as queries ("in n out" is searched as "n out", "chick fil a" as "chick fil"). Chipotle fails `isChain`'s packaged-names test: Open Food Facts has "chipotle" in 2,973 names and 14 brands. So the menu catalog needs a say when the other words name one of the chain's items.
    - **Where it lives depends on the source.** FNDDS rows go into `usda.db` itself. An ODbL catalog (OpenNutrition) is published beside `off.db` with its recipe. Data used by permission ships only as that permission allows, and stays out of the public ODbL directory. Menus change monthly, which favours Background Assets over app releases. That's the same call as shipping `off.db`.

- **CSV exports.** A text field that starts like a formula (`=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a return) gets a leading apostrophe, which Excel hides and Numbers shows; plain numbers such as -5 are left alone. Open: doing it only in Stadig Macro, whose food names come from Open Food Facts, or using another marker, such as a leading space.
- **A save against a delete.** In the record store, a save in the same millisecond as a delete wins (Stadig Macro deletes a day's state and saves it again under the same id), while imports, de-duplication and the life-mode merge let the delete win a tie; the pass tried making the delete win here too, and it lost those saves. Open: keeping that exception, or making a delete win every tie, with Stadig Macro no longer deleting a day's state before saving it.
- **Apple's models.** SystemAI tries each engine, on-device and Private Cloud Compute, at most once a request, doesn't retry a refusal, and stops when cancelled. Open: whether a failed engine should get a second try.
- **Health on returning.** Health is read again each time the app comes back, at most every ten minutes in Stadig Lift and every five in Stadig Macro, and both start their Health observer at every launch, not only after "Connect Apple Health". Open: the intervals, and whether the two should match.
- **Test launches.** `-inMemoryStore`, `-uiTesting` and the unit-test host keep the shared container, the widget snapshot, Stadig Lift's `live-session.json` and Stadig Mind's drafts in a temporary folder, so a sample run never reaches the App Group, the other apps or the widgets, and the two flags work only in DEBUG builds. Open: letting `-inMemoryStore -seedSampleData` publish to the App Group again, so the other apps and the widgets show the sample.
- **UI test retries.** The generated UI test plans retry a failed test once (`retryOnFailure`, two runs at most), since a slow simulator frame can fail a good run, so a flaky test can pass without anyone noticing. Open: no retries, so every flake shows.
- **Days on other calendars.** A `Day` is now always the Gregorian date in the device's time zone; days saved on a phone set to another calendar before the fix sit at the wrong dates (Buddhist ones 543 years ahead), and there's no migration, since nothing has shipped. Open: a migration, if a test device ran another calendar.
