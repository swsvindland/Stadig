# Macro Track

Macro Track is built for the least time in the app. Most visits are one of these:

- **A glance.** Today shows calories left as a ring, protein (and carbs and fat, if shown) as bars, today's weight and trend, and the one or two things worth doing. The widget shows the same on the Home Screen and Lock Screen.
- **No meal slots.** A day is a timeline: foods logged within 45 minutes of each other are one moment, with its time and total, whether you eat three times a day or five. Logging defaults to now, with "30m ago", "1h ago", "2h ago" or any time one tap away, and **+** on a moment adds to it. A time picked on the wheel for today goes on the day nearest now (11:30 PM picked just after midnight is last night, 7 PM picked at noon is this evening), and the logger's title, its time bar and the Add button name the weekday when that isn't the day on screen. A picked time lasts only while that logger is open.
- **One tap.** "Log again" ranks foods by how recently and how often you eat them at this time of day, and logs the same amount with one tap. "Same as yesterday" copies what you ate around this time on a recent day. Every log shows Undo, and so does every delete, even of a whole moment ("Deleted 3 foods"). Tapping an entry edits it: it reopens in the portion it was logged in while that portion still holds the logged amount, otherwise in g, ml or servings at the amount logged, so a catalog update can't change it. Changing only the time keeps its numbers.
- **Vitamins and minerals, quietly.** Every entry keeps its micronutrients, scaled to the portion. Progress shows the last week of complete days against your targets (the US National Academies' recommended intakes for your age and sex; fibre at 14 g per 1,000 kcal; sodium and saturated fat as limits), with "unknown" rather than "low" when too little of what you ate lists a nutrient: packaged foods often carry only the label basics. When a watched one runs short, Today offers one food that fixes it, preferring ones you already eat, at an ordinary portion, logged with one tap; foods you open show their vitamins and minerals as a share of your targets. They go to Health and the CSV export too.
- **Food quality, not just macros.** Protein shakes and Pop-Tarts can hit any macro target, so every food carries how processed it is (NOVA: whole, kitchen staple, processed, ultra-processed) and whether it's fermented with live cultures, and entries keep both. Progress shows the last week's calories by group, ultra-processed against an aim of under 20% (people eat more and gain more fat on the same macros when most of it is ultra-processed), fermented foods against "most days", and fibre. Over 30% ultra-processed, Today names the biggest source and what does its job as whole food ("Oats with fruit fill the same spot"), going by whole words in its name, so "chocolate" isn't taken for cola or "barbecue" for a bar; cookies, brownies, doughnuts and candy get "Fruit, or yogurt with honey", and pastrami or ham gets "Chicken, eggs or fish cooked plain", however much protein it has. Short on fermented foods, it offers one to log with one tap, yours first. Open Food Facts supplies its own NOVA group (or one from the product's category); USDA's are estimated from each food's group and name. Foods without one (quick adds, your own) count as unknown, not as whole or processed.
- **Meal prep in an afternoon.** The Plan tab plans a week of meals sized to your targets: pick which meals to prep (any of breakfast, lunch, dinner and a snack), your diet and what to leave out. Lunches and dinners are two bowls (a protein, a carb and a vegetable in one of seven flavours, Japanese curry among them, alternating so no day repeats a dish), or one for all of them from a single batch ("One dish all week" in the setup, which carries over to the next week, or "the same meal for lunch and dinner" in a request), breakfast is overnight oats, egg muffins or a yogurt bowl, and the snack is whichever fills what's left, protein first. Every portion is solved from catalog nutrients, so a full day lands within a few percent of your calories and protein; meals you don't prep keep their room ("Dinner is yours: about 700 kcal, 45 g protein"). Then: the grocery list by aisle, in the units things are sold in (3 cans, 1 jar, 2.9 lb), with checkboxes and Share; and prep day as a timed list that overlaps the pot, the oven and your hands (about 2 hours for 14 containers and 7 jars; a grain two bowls share is cooked once, and each bowl's roast is its own step), with what goes in each container and where it keeps (fridge Mon–Wed, freezer Thu–Sun, a reminder each evening to move tomorrow's). Swap any dish with one tap, or type "no chicken, something spicy": word rules map the request to the building blocks, and Apple's on-device model (Private Cloud Compute when allowed and the app is entitled) reads it too, for wording the rules get the wrong way round ("anything but chicken"). The model only decides whether something the request names is wanted or left out; anything else it returns is dropped, since it lists the whole menu or an allergy from words that name none of it ("high protein" came back as no rice, no pasta, no potatoes). The diet, cheaper, quicker and one dish come from the words alone. Negatives and allergies leave things out ("I don't want fish", "my partner doesn't like fish", "no fish and pork", "allergic to soy", "lactose intolerant", "a nut allergy", "coeliac"), while "no rice and use quinoa" and "no pasta, just rice" still ask for the rest. A dish's name is read whole ("Japanese curry" is the Japanese curry, not teriyaki and an Indian curry), and words are matched whole ("breakfast" isn't "fast"). "High protein" changes nothing, since every plan is sized to the protein target. Once the week has started, a swap, "Other dishes" or a typed change touches only the dishes still to come: days already eaten and meals logged keep theirs. On the day, "From your plan" on Today logs each meal with one tap, as one entry that keeps its processing and fermented flags.
- **A weigh-in in seconds.** The scale button in the quick-log bar opens the weigh-in with your last weight ready to nudge (± 0.1 kg or 0.2 lb) and save; Today has an inline field until you've weighed in; and there's a Weigh In control for the Lock Screen and Control Center. Health weigh-ins from a smart scale arrive on their own: Macro Track watches Health from launch and reads it again whenever you come back to the app (at most every 5 minutes; pulling down on Today reads it at once). A weigh-in deleted in Health leaves Macro Track too; one typed here stays. A weight the trend can't use (20 kg or less, or 400 kg or more) is refused by the weigh-in, Today's field and onboarding, and Siri asks for it again in your unit.
- **A few taps.** The quick-log bar sits above the tabs on every screen: search, camera, barcode, describe and weigh in.
  - **Search** is offline and instant over 3.2 million foods: USDA, plus Open Food Facts' packaged foods. Your own foods rank first, and each result shows the portion it would log (your last one, or a natural unit like "1 container"). A brand typed before or after the food is read as one, packaged brands and the restaurant chains USDA lists alike, and that brand's food comes first, even where the other words usually mean something else: "papa johns 14 inch pepperoni" is Papa John's pepperoni pizza, not sliced pepperoni, and "tillamook cheddar" is cheese, not cheddar crackers. A size in inches (14", 14-inch, 14in) puts that size first without being required, and typos and joined words ("papajohns") are corrected. Restaurant food is USDA's: SR Legacy's chain items (analysed 2006–2012) and FNDDS 2021–2023's 191 fast-food and restaurant foods ("Chicken nuggets, from fast food", "Pizza with pepperoni, from restaurant or fast food, thin crust", "Big Mac (McDonalds)"), with portions such as "1 small fast food order" or "1 McDonald's Big Mac". A chain's name for an item finds the food FNDDS counts it as: "mcrib" is a barbecue rib sandwich, "frappuccino" a frozen coffee drink, "egg mcmuffin" an egg sandwich on an English muffin with ham, each just after the chain's own item where USDA or Open Food Facts has one. Most chains' own menus aren't in the catalogs yet; see `docs/decisions.md`. The **+** logs it, clears the search and keeps the keyboard up for the next food. A portion switched to pounds reads in tenths (200 g is 0.4 lb).
  - **Scan** is live, like filming the package: the barcode, the front and the Nutrition Facts in one sweep. A barcode the catalogs know opens the food with the usual portion ready. An unknown one is kept while the panel is read: a few frames a second go through Vision on the device, each frame's part of the panel is parsed, and every value fills in on screen as it's read, trusted (a check mark) once two frames agree and most frames that read it do. A misread frame is outvoted, and a label too tall or curved for one photo is read a slice at a time as the camera moves down it. Only what Macro Track tracks is kept: cholesterol, trans fat and added sugars are read for the label's own checks, then dropped. Every couple of seconds while scanning, Apple's on-device model reads the text gathered so far, copies the panel's values and names the product ("Checked by Apple Intelligence" on the card). Its reading counts as two votes on each value: it settles a tie such as the per-serving column against the per-container one and fills a line the frames missed, but it can only use a number printed on that nutrient's own row of the panel, never a % Daily Value, and three frames that agree outvote it. Review runs a last check (Private Cloud Compute too, when allowed). The hint says what's still missing ("Keep going: still reading protein"), and there's a torch. Review checks the numbers, and the food is kept with its barcode for next time. A photo of the label still works from the ⋯ menu.
  - **Photo** and **Describe** ("two eggs, toast with butter and a flat white") use Apple's on-device model, and Private Cloud Compute when allowed once the app has Apple's managed entitlement: without it, Foundation Models reports Private Cloud Compute as available and then stops the app on the first request, so until the Info.plist sets `SystemAIPrivateCloud` to true everything runs on the device and Settings hides the switch. The model only names foods and amounts; nutrients always come from the catalog. The review screen fixes a wrong match in one tap and logs everything at once. An amount you say wins over the model's guess and is read whole ("1/2 bagel", "1½ slices", "a couple of", "half a dozen", "an 8 oz steak"); "a few" or "a handful" leaves the model's amount, and "2% milk" or a "6 inch sub" isn't a count. The model's weight for a food sold in slices or pieces is checked against them: four slices' worth or more (a photo's whole loaf, a whole cake) becomes the pieces it counted, at least one, unless you said the amount. A coffee drink takes its milk and size only from its own words ("a latte with an almond croissant" isn't an almond-milk latte), and milk or coffee ordered as an item of its own stays one. A description reaches the model whole up to 2,000 characters (a photo's note, 500).
  - **Quick add** takes calories and protein.
- **Supplements, ticked off.** The Supplements screen (Plan tab, Settings, or Edit on Today) holds your stack. Scan a bottle's barcode and its label comes from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (127,000 US labels, 98,000 with barcodes, offline); search it by name (every match is ranked, so a current label isn't lost behind hundreds of old ones); or keep the camera on the Supplement Facts panel, read live line by line like a food's and checked by Apple's model the same way (a photo works too), which a parser turns into lines ("Vitamin D3 (as cholecalciferol) 25 mcg (1,000 IU)" is 25 µg of vitamin D; folic acid becomes µg DFE; EPA and DHA add up to omega-3), with Apple's model only naming the product. Only the lines Macro Track tracks are kept (vitamins, minerals, omega-3s, caffeine, calories and macros): a bottle's herbs, extracts, amino acids and "fish oil concentrate" add nothing it counts, so the NIH labels, photos, live scans and the editor all leave them out. Each supplement gets one or more doses: a time of day (morning, midday, afternoon, evening, bedtime), how many (2 softgels, 1 scoop), and which days (every day, chosen weekdays, every other day, or training and rest days from the shared schedule and Lift Track's sessions). Today lists the day's doses by time of day: a tap ticks one off, "Take all" a time's worth, swipe to skip, and the section folds to one line once everything's marked. Optional reminders fire at each time with doses still open, with a Taken button that ticks them off without opening the app; "Take my supplements" does the same from Siri. Doses taken add their vitamins, minerals and caffeine to the week's report as measured (a capsule has no calories, so it doesn't stretch the estimate for foods that skip a nutrient), and their few calories count on days with food logged. Anything over a safe upper limit with supplements part of why (vitamin D over 100 µg, supplemental magnesium over 350 mg, iron, zinc, B6 and the rest of the National Academies' ULs) goes first on Today, naming the supplement and the fix: every other day, or a lower strength. Scanning a supplement in the food scanner offers to take a dose or add it; protein powders the food catalog also knows stay food. Doses go to Health and the CSV export.
- **Drinks and caffeine, if you want them.** Off by default, since counting every glass is a chore. Turned on in Settings, Today gets a Drinks card: water against a daily aim (3 L for men, 2.2 L for women, from the National Academies' intake less what food supplies) with a tap for a glass or a bottle, plus the fluid in coffee, milk, juice and energy drinks already in the food log. Coffee, espresso and tea are one tap each (logged as food from USDA, so their caffeine is exact), and your own drinks follow. Caffeine from food and pre-workouts counts against a limit (400 mg, or your own), with when the last one was and how much will still be active at bedtime at a five-hour half-life; past the limit, or with 50 mg or more left at bedtime, Today says what to do ("Caffeine before 2:30 PM leaves less in the way of deep sleep"). Drinks logged without a caffeine value (a café latte, a can Open Food Facts lists without it) get a typical one by name and brand. "Cut back" starts from the week's real average and steps down a fifth a week (at least 25 mg) to your goal, so withdrawal stays mild; each week's limit shows on Today. The daily limit itself stays as it was: stopping early keeps it, and stopping once the goal is reached makes the goal the limit. Water goes to Health.
- **Meal timing and fasts, if you want them.** Off by default, since nudges about when to eat aren't right for everyone. Turned on in Settings, everything comes from the log, with nothing to tap: a meal is a moment of 50 kcal or more (black coffee, tea or a splash of milk isn't), and an eating day runs from 4 AM, so a snack after midnight ends the evening. Today shows the time since the last meal on a small ring toward 5 hours (under 2 hours orange, 3–5 green) and the night's fast ("Overnight fast 13 h · 8:05 PM to 9:10 AM"); each meal's header has the gap before it ("+3 h 20 m"). Progress has the week: gaps by band, meals a day, the typical gap, overnight fast, eating window and last meal before bed, meals with about 0.4 g/kg of protein in a lean bulk or recomp, and one piece of advice (grazing first, then too few protein meals while building, an overnight fast under 12 hours, eating within 2 hours of bed). Only complete days (and today so far) are read: an unlogged snack would look like a long gap. **Start fast** sets a goal (13 to 72 hours with a tap, up to 7 days on the stepper) from the end of the last meal or a time picked; Today then shows a ring toward the goal, the phase (digesting, insulin low, fat burning, ketosis, deep ketosis, as ranges with what's reasonably established in people) and when the next begins, with End fast. Logging a meal ends it, on any device, and its Undo puts the fast back; dessert added to the meal it began from moves its start instead. Starting and ending both have Undo, a start can't be set before the last meal, and turning meal timing off ends the fast. Past its goal, a fast counts only as far as its goal for the scale, the estimate and fasted days unless it was ended by hand, and one with no meal logged 12 hours past its goal is taken as over at its goal. The first goal over a day asks you to read who shouldn't (insulin or a sulfonylurea, an SGLT2 inhibitor, pregnancy, an eating disorder, under 18 or underweight, gout or heart medicines) and when to stop; under 18 or underweight, nothing over a day is offered; over 3 days says to involve a doctor; a lean bulk or recomp says what a long fast costs it. Notifications come at 12 hours, a day (salt and minerals) and 3 days, and at the goal with how to break it. Whole days inside a fast count as fasted days. A fast of a day or more leaves its weigh-ins and the 3 days after out of the trend, the estimate and the check-in learn as if those days were eaten at maintenance, the check-in holds meanwhile, and Today says the scale is water. The fast is on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (a Live Activity, started again when the app opens, since the system ends one after 8 hours), in a Fasting widget (Lock Screen and Home Screen), on the Watch, in History's Overnight column and in the CSV export (`fasts.csv`).
- **No app at all.** Siri and Shortcuts: "Log food in Macro Track" (a description logged in the background), "How many calories are left", "Log my weight", "Take my supplements", "Log water", "Start a fast", "How long have I been fasting", "End my fast", quick add, and "Open food logger" on the camera, scanner or weigh-in for the Action Button. Controls: Snap a Meal, Scan Barcode, Log Food and Weigh In. Siri, Shortcuts and a reminder's Taken button catch up to today first, so an app left open overnight doesn't log to yesterday, and a widget link, a control or the Action Button opens its screen in place of any sheet that's up.

## Apple Watch

The Watch app is the shortest visit of all: a glance at what's left, and a tap to log. It runs on its own, with or without the iPhone nearby. The Digital Crown moves down through its pages:

- **Today.** Calories left as the outer ring, protein inside it, carbs and fat beneath (when they're shown on the phone), the day's badge (the phase, "Day 5 of 21", or a life mode with its note), **Weigh In** until there's been a weigh-in today, and **Mark Day Complete** once something's logged. The scale button at the top opens the weigh-in any time.
- **Log Again.** "Same as yesterday" while nothing's logged near now, then the ten foods `LogAgain` ranks for this time of day, as on the phone. A tap logs the same amount, now, with a haptic and a few seconds of Undo.
- **Water**, when drinks are on: water and drinks so far against the day's aim, and a glass or a bottle with a tap (8 oz and 16.9 oz for people who weigh in pounds).
- **Fasting**, when meal timing is on: the running fast as a ring toward its goal with its phase, and End Fast; or the time since the last meal, with a 13, 16, 18 or 24 hour fast a tap away (longer ones start on the phone, where the safety notes are). Undo takes back a start or an end.
- **Supplements**, when the day has doses: the open ones whose time has come (the time of day nearest now, and earlier ones still unmarked), a tap each or **Take All**, and how many are due later.
- **Weigh In.** The Digital Crown, starting at the last weigh-in, 0.1 kg or 0.2 lb a detent, inside the weights the trend accepts; Save.
- Back after a couple of minutes away, it opens on Today again.
- **Complications.** Calories Left and Protein Left, each as a circular, rectangular, inline and corner complication, Today (both, with bars) for the Smart Stack, and Fasting (the fast toward its goal, or the time since the last meal), whose timeline steps through the phases with the app closed. Tapping one opens the app. The app writes `WristSnapshot` into the Watch's App Group whenever the numbers change and reloads them; a second timeline entry starts the new day at nothing eaten at midnight.

**The same numbers as the phone.** The Watch opens Macro Track's own CloudKit store (`RecordStore(name: "MacroTrack", cloudKitContainer: "iCloud.dev.svindland.stadig.macro")`) and reads and writes the same records as the iPhone, iPad and Mac. They're declared once, in `Apps/MacroTrack/Records`, which both apps compile; custom foods, favourites and meal plans stay in the iPhone app, which has the catalog and the planner. It also mirrors the family's shared files through `FamilySync` (read only: the phone writes them), so Lift Track's calendar (deload weeks), summary (training days, steps, measurements) and the life modes reach it. `MacroAnalyst.analyse` runs over all of it, off the main thread, and `WristToday` (NutritionEngine) turns the analysis into the pages. An entry carries its own numbers, so logging again needs no food catalog (off.db is 590 MB; FoodIntelligence and MealPlanner don't build for watchOS). The Watch never applies the weekly check-in, which is the phone's job; until the phone has, the Watch shows the targets the due check-in sets, as the phone would. CloudKit's pushes wake it (the `remote-notification` background mode) so the complications follow what's logged elsewhere: it stays awake for the import, then until the analysis has written the complications' snapshot and reloaded them (`WristModel.analysisFinished`, at most 12 seconds more), so watchOS doesn't suspend it halfway.

**Apple Health, once.** The Watch writes nothing to Health: a sample's sync identifier only replaces samples from the same app, and the Watch app is a source of its own, so a food written by both would be there twice. Instead the iPhone (and the iPad and Vision Pro, the same app) writes what syncs in from devices that don't write it themselves: `HealthBackfill` checks the last 30 days of foods, water, doses taken and typed weigh-ins against Health by sync identifier and version, at launch, whenever another device's changes arrive, each time the app comes to the front, when the phone is unlocked and when Health is connected. What Health already holds from Macro Track on any device is left alone; the rest is written. Every write, here as something's logged and by the backfill, takes its version from `Date.healthVersion` (the record's `updatedAt` in whole milliseconds, rounded as the store rounds it), so the backfill knows the phone's own samples; one written by an earlier version, which truncated, a millisecond lower, counts as the same. A ledger on the device remembers what's been handled, so a sample deleted in Health isn't put back. A Health copy comes out only for a deletion the store holds (an Undo on the Watch, a delete on the Mac) or a dose unmarked; a record that's only missing (the store emptied while iCloud changes accounts), written by a newer app version this one can't read, or dated back out of the 30 days keeps its copy. The store's deletions are read only when something handled has gone missing, and only of its kind. It waits while the phone is locked or Health isn't connected yet (and tries again on those triggers), writes nothing while "Save to Health" is off (what arrived meanwhile stays out), and skips an ignored weigh-in and the weigh-in setup saved. So the Mac's logs reach Health too, once the iPhone or iPad sees them.

`Scripts/build.sh macro-watch` builds the Watch app and its complications; the iPhone build (`Scripts/build.sh macro`) carries the Watch app, the Mac and Vision Pro builds don't. On a Watch simulator: `xcrun simctl install <udid> <DerivedData>/Build/Products/Debug-watchsimulator/MacroTrackWatch.app`, then `xcrun simctl launch <udid> dev.svindland.stadig.macro.watchkitapp -seedSampleData -seedPlan -seedSupplements -inMemoryStore`. Its DEBUG launch arguments: `-inMemoryStore` and `-resetData` as on the phone; `-seedSampleData` (the phone's sample person, with USDA numbers in place of the catalog, and today logged up to lunch), `-seedPlan` (starts the recommended plan, so there are calorie targets), `-seedSupplements` (a stack due through the day, two weeks taken, coffee and water, drinks on), `-imperial` (pounds and fluid ounces), `-page logAgain|water|supplements|weigh`, `-scrollToEnd` (Today scrolled down), `-demoLog` (logs the first Log Again food and keeps its Undo up), `-complications` (every complication drawn in the app, since a script can't put one on a face), `-shareSnapshot` (with `-inMemoryStore`, writes the sample's snapshot to the App Group anyway, so the real complications show it) and `-largeText`.

## How the doc maps to the code

| Doc item | Where |
|---|---|
| Nutrition and bodyweight; daily weigh-ins drive adaptive targets | `Weight.trend` smooths both level and rate (Holt), so a steady loss doesn't lag; a lone misread (more than 3% off both neighbours, on the same side of both) is left out, so sparse readings on a steady loss stay in. `Energy.observe` fits a line through the weigh-ins against mean intake on complete days. `CheckIns.review` moves maintenance a third of the way each week and targets at most 150 kcal (or 7.5%) within a phase, holding on jumps and, once, on a misread; a planned phase change (a diet break, maintenance after a cut, a bulk after a mini-cut) starts at the new phase's calories. Each check-in has its day's id (`CheckIn.id(for:)`), so two devices don't make two; when a day has two anyway, the newer one is in force. In a deficit, when Lift Track's steps fell (by at least 1,500 a day and an eighth, against the month before) and that explains at least half of a lower maintenance, it holds maintenance once and says to walk back toward the aim before calories come down; a week later the data leads again. |
| 2–3 week assessment; week 1 is water; a wide preliminary estimate at week 2; no deficit or surplus | `MacroAnalyst` (21 days, or 14 with a fortnight of earlier Health weigh-ins) and `Energy.estimate`: the formula (Mifflin–St Jeor × activity from Lift Track's sessions and, when Lift Track has them, steps in place of an assumed everyday average) blended with the data by their uncertainties, with an honest range ("Early estimate", "Getting there", "Solid"). Targets during the assessment ask only for protein. |
| Goal-driven phase recommendation, chained, three horizons, one recommendation with "Why?", honest confidence | `PhasePlanner.recommend`: cut, mini-cut, maintenance, diet break, reverse diet, recomp or lean bulk with rate and length, from goals, waist-to-height (Lift Track's measurements), strength, diet history and maintenance against the formula. Thin data keeps the first phase to 4 weeks and says so. The Plan tab shows now, next and the year's arc. |
| Review at mesocycle boundaries with rule triggers | `PhasePlanner.review`: a cut ends with a diet break when strength regresses or you reach lean; a surplus slows when gaining too fast; a new recommendation when the plan runs out. Shown as "Plan check" on Today and the Plan tab when Lift Track's block ends. Once the last phase is over, Today shows "Plan finished", with or without Lift Track. A phase ended partway through keeps the days it ran, and the diet break or mini-cut starts the day the review is accepted, at its full length. |
| Publish the phase to the core | `AppModel.publish` writes `NutritionSummary` (phase, maintenance, day-by-day logging, and target hits for complete days, each judged by that day's own targets) and Macro Track's `AppSummary`, only when they differ from the files on disk (`SharedContainer.writeIfChanged`), so a launch doesn't rewrite them and wake the other apps. Lift Track already reads them. While meal timing is on, the summary also carries the running fast (its start and goal), which Lift Track uses to say something before a heavy session a day into one. |
| Diet breaks during system deloads ("whoosh") | `Targets.today`: in a deficit during Lift Track's deload week, calories go to maintenance, with a note that a stalled scale is water and expected. |
| Vacation, crunch and illness | Shared life modes (set in any of the apps). Vacation and illness expect nothing; the days after are left out of the trend; crunch asks for protein and maintenance only. Of these, only illness can run "Until it's better"; vacation and crunch always have an end, and "End today" shows once a period has started. `AppModel.syncLifeModes` merges the shared file with Macro Track's own synced copy of every period (the newest edit wins), keeps what arrived and writes the merged list back; a delete is kept as a tombstone, so it reaches the other apps and devices. |
| Discipline: "didn't log" is unknown | Only complete days score; unlogged days are unknown, never misses. Logging rate is shown separately. |
| Reuse the shared profile (staggered start); backfill weight from Health | Onboarding reuses Lift Track's answers and asks only about recent dieting and today's weight; when it asks for height, it asks for units first. Health weigh-ins from 60 days before the assessment are imported. Later changes made in Lift Track (units, height, birth year, sex, training days) are taken up. A second device keeps a setup that already synced in; if both finished setup before syncing, the one set up first is in force (`MacroSettings.inForce`, which the Watch and the Health backfill use too). Settings changes go through `updateSettings`, which edits the copy the store has, so they can't undo a change that just synced in, and edits from another device show while the app is open (`RecordStore.observeChanges`). |
| Export and backup; one-time purchase | Settings: CSV (diary, weight, targets, days), a `.macrobackup` file, restore. Logging, AI logging, the assessment, export and backup are free; starting the plan needs the unlock. CSV times are local clock time, and a text field that starts like a formula (=, +, -, @) gets a leading apostrophe. Backups are made and restored off the main thread, with a progress row, and restore only into Macro Track: another app's is refused ("This backup is from Lift Track. Restore it there."). |

## Packages

- **NutritionEngine** is pure Swift over SystemCore: records, the trend, energy balance, check-ins, the phase planner, targets, the analysis behind every action, "Log again" ranking and CSV tables. `MealTiming` reads meals, gaps, overnight fasts and the week's pattern from the log; `Fasting` has fasts' runs, phases, milestones, fasted days and what a long fast does to the scale and the estimate (`Fasting.adjustment`), and `FastSafety` who shouldn't, when to stop and how to break one. Supplements (the stack, dose schedules, taken and skipped doses, upper limits) and drinks (water, fluid from drinks by name, caffeine timing and the cut-back) live here too. So do the swap lines (`QualitySwap`) and the rules behind the one-tap fixes (`FoodFix`: which food to offer, at what portion); the app finds the foods in the catalog and the person's own.
- **FoodDatabase** ports the React Native app's catalogs and search: SQLite FTS5 over `usda.db` (bundled in the package) and `off.db` (bundled from `Apps/MacroTrack/Catalogs`), ranking with stemming, synonyms, spelling and brand detection (packaged foods' brands, and the chains USDA writes in capitals before a food), sizes in inches, the portion-units engine, barcode forms, and the nutrition label parser. `NutritionLabel.frame` reads whatever part of a panel one camera frame shows, and `LiveLabel` and `LiveSupplementLabel` merge frames by vote into one panel. `SupplementCatalog` reads `supplements.db`, and `SupplementLabel` reads Supplement Facts panels; the rules that turn label names and units into nutrients (`SupplementIngredients`) are in SystemCore, shared by both. `FoodUnits.logged` decides which portion a logged entry reopens in, and `FoodUnits.parseCount` reads other scripts' digits ("١/٢", "１/２") and refuses counts over 1,000,000. Each catalog keeps at most 4 idle SQLite connections, and `FoodCatalog.releaseMemory()` drops its caches on a memory warning and when the app goes to the background.
- **FoodIntelligence** ports the two-pass AI: the model names foods and amounts, candidates come from the catalog, a second pass breaks near-ties, and amounts resolve from catalog portions. Label photos go through Vision OCR and the parser, nutrition and Supplement Facts panels alike; `LabelOCR.frame` reads one live camera frame in a single pass, and `checkLabel` and `checkSupplement` have the model read a live scan's text, keeping only a number printed on that nutrient's own row (never a % Daily Value) and lines Macro Track tracks. Without Apple's model, `LabelOCR.label(in:)` reads a label photo in one Vision pass. The on-device model's guardrails refuse requests that call a product a dietary supplement, so those prompts say "a product label". It picks the on-device model first (with vision for photos) and Private Cloud Compute when allowed and `PrivateCloud.isEntitled` (SystemCore reads `SystemAIPrivateCloud` from the Info.plist); without the entitlement it never builds a Private Cloud Compute model, not even to ask whether one is there.
- **MealPlanner** builds a week from 55 whole-food building blocks tied to USDA foods (so the numbers never move), seven flavour profiles, breakfasts and snacks, as two bowls for lunches and dinners or one (`PlanPreferences.oneDish`). A small bounded least-squares solver sizes each container to its share of the day; the grocery list converts cooked amounts to raw and dry ones with cooking yields; the prep guide schedules oven rounds of two trays, pots and hands-on jobs on one timeline, one pot per grain. `PlanRequests` maps a request's words to the library ("sick of chicken", "dairy-free", "Asian", "the same meal for lunch and dinner"), negatives and allergies included, and `PlanAssistant` reads it with Apple's on-device model too (Private Cloud Compute only when the device's can't, the setting allows it and the app is entitled), keeping the model's reading only for the foods and flavours the request names (`PlanAssistant.merge`). `swap`, `replan` and `shuffle` ("Other dishes") keep the days before today and the meals logged, and `swappable` lists the dishes still to come.
- **HealthBridge** gained per-app permissions, food writes (food correlations keyed by entry id, replaced on edit), dietary water, and weigh-in reads and writes. Writes and deletes for one food entry run one at a time in the order made, so an Undo waits for the save it undoes, and `disable()` keeps tests and UI tests out of Health. `weighInChanges` reads weigh-ins from where the last read left off (an anchor the app keeps on the device), with the ones deleted in Health.

## Platforms

One target runs natively on iPhone, iPad, the Mac and Apple Vision Pro, synced through Macro Track's own CloudKit container. Layout goes by width, not device: a compact width (an iPhone, or an iPad in a narrow Split View or Slide Over) is the phone app exactly; a regular width (an iPad full screen or in a wide split, the Mac, Vision Pro) gets the desktop layout. The phone stays built for the least time in the app; the iPad and the Mac are where you find and compare foods, browse nutrients, plan the week and the grocery list, and look back over history, with logging a keystroke away.

**A second device.** A second device, or a reinstall, that finds the app not set up asks iCloud once whether the app has records there (`ICloudArrival`, SystemStore): the account, then one record id from the store's zone. Nothing there, no account, or no answer within 10 seconds goes straight to onboarding, after a blank moment rather than a flash of the wait. Records there show "Bringing your food log from iCloud" instead of onboarding until this launch's first import has ended; the data arriving opens Today, and a failure to set up or import ends the wait with its reason under the spinner. "Set up as new" skips the wait. Settings' Sync row follows CloudKit's own events (`SyncState`): "Syncing…", "Up to date" with when it last synced, or "Not syncing" with the reason in words (not signed in, storage full, iCloud turning records down because its schema hasn't got them), and the shared setup's state when that's the part failing.

- **The sidebar.** Today, then **Food** (Foods, Meal Plan, Supplements) and **Review** (Plan, Progress, History). On the iPad the tabs are iPadOS's bar along the top, Today, Food and Review (a section opens on its first destination), with the whole sidebar on the toggle at its start, as in Lift Track and Mind Track. Narrowed onto a screen only the sidebar has, the app moves to the nearest one the tab bar has.
- **Foods** (`FoodsView`): the search over every food into a table at the portion each would log (the person's last one, else the food's own) with kcal, protein, carbs, fat and fiber, and sugar, sodium, processing and source as columns to show (the Columns menu, or the header's menu on the Mac). Every column sorts, foods without the value last. All, Recent, Favorites and My Foods filter by every word typed. The best match is selected as you type; its amount, every nutrient for that amount and when it was eaten are in the inspector, with Log (⌘Return). Return in the search field or the table logs the selection; pressed straight after typing, before the rows for what was typed are in, it waits for them and logs the new best match (`FoodSearchReturn`), and a refined query selects its own best match rather than keeping the last one's; a double-click on the Mac logs a row; a row's context menu logs its usual portion or stars it. The search field has the cursor on arrival, and ⌘F, ⌘L, Log Food and a widget's search all land in it, except on an iPad in portrait, where the tab bar along the top leaves the toolbar no room for the field: there it's the toolbar's search button, and once open it takes the tab bar's place until it's closed (⌘1–⌘7 still go anywhere). Rows come from `FoodBrowser` (FoodDatabase), searched off the main thread.
- **Today** (`TodayBoard`): the summary, weight, actions, planned meals, supplements, drinks and "Log again" in a column beside the day's log as a table of every macro, grouped into moments while it's sorted by time and one list when sorted by anything else. A selected entry is edited in an inspector (Save is ⌘S); its context menu logs it again, adds to its moment, or deletes it (Delete on the Mac). The toolbar steps the day (⌘[ and ⌘] in the Day menu, ⌘T for today) or picks it from a calendar, and has Log Food, Describe, Photo, Quick Add and Weigh In: the quick-log bar above the tabs is the phone's.
- **History** (`HistoryView`): a sortable table of every day from the first log or weigh-in: calories against that day's own target and how far over or under (green or orange only on days that count), protein, carbs, fat, fiber, the status as the analysis reads it (complete, fasted, partial, unknown, today, with a life mode named), the weigh-in that counts and the trend. Days without a value sort last. The selected day's log is beside it, with its completeness to set and Show in Today (also a double-click). On an iPad in portrait, with the day beside the table, carbs, fat, fiber and the calorie target give way so the other columns stay readable (the day's log has them). Rows are `DayHistory.rows` (NutritionEngine), worked out off the main thread whenever the analysis changes.
- **Meal Plan** (`MealPlanBoard`): the request field, the week as a grid of days by meal (today outlined, frozen and logged marked, the dish's contents as a tooltip, swap and log in each cell's context menu), the dishes, and the grocery list by aisle and prep day beside them rather than behind a picker (in one column beside the week when the window is narrower).
- **Supplements** (`SupplementsTab`): the stack and its reminders in a list, and the selected supplement's editor beside it; adding one (by barcode, name, a photo of the label or typed in) happens in the same pane. Opening Supplements from Today, Plan or a link goes here rather than to a sheet.
- **Plan and Progress**: cards side by side instead of one column; Progress has one range (1M, 2M, 6M, 1Y) for both charts, taller charts, and the week's vitamins and minerals as a table.
- **Logging without a camera** (the Mac, Vision Pro): Photo chooses a photo from Photos or a file (iPad too, beside its camera), or takes one dropped anywhere on the window; Barcode is typed, with a label read from a photo or file for a food the catalogs don't know; supplements' labels the same. The live scanner stays on iPhone and iPad.
- **Apple Health** isn't on the Mac: onboarding and Settings say where it lives and that weigh-ins read from it on the iPhone or iPad reach the Mac with the rest of the log, while food logged on the Mac goes to Health only from a device that has it.

The Mac has one window (the model holds what's on screen, so a second would only mirror it; 900×580 at least, 1280×820 to start), a Settings window (⌘,) in panes (General, Intelligence, Health & Life, Data), and menus:

## DEBUG launch arguments

`-uiTesting` is what the UI tests launch with: the in-memory store and temporary folder described next, Health turned off, no notification prompt and no reminders scheduled, no widget reloads, StoreKit not started, and no animations. `-inMemoryStore` keeps nothing between launches: the store lives in memory and never syncs, and the shared container and the widgets' snapshot go in a temporary folder (and "Not now" on suggestions in memory), so it neither reads nor writes what the other apps publish; with `-inMemoryStore -seedSampleData`, Lift Track, the hub and the widgets don't see the sample. `-resetData` erases the store first, `-simulateICloudData` shows the wait a second device shows while its log comes down from iCloud (over a store that never gets any), and `-unlocked` skips the purchase (it only matters once purchases are switched back on).

`-seedSampleData` (three and a half weeks of a person eating a little under a true maintenance of 2,600 kcal, logged from real catalog foods; the same person every launch, since its randomness is seeded), with `-sampleProcessed` (the same person eating the convenient way, toaster pastries, cola, nuggets, fries and pizza, for the food-quality views), `-seedPlan` (also starts the recommended plan) and `-seedMealPlan` (also a week of meal prep for every meal, started yesterday, so Today shows its meals); `-seedMealTiming` (meal timing on, with a 16-hour fast from a dinner earlier in the week; needs `-seedSampleData`), `-seedFast` (also a 24-hour fast running from last night's dinner), `-showFast` (opens the fast screen), `-seedSupplements` (a fish oil, a multivitamin morning and evening, a pre-workout on training days and a sleep gummy at bedtime, two weeks of them taken, coffee most mornings, energy drinks some afternoons and some water, with drinks turned on; it needs Macro Track set up, which `-seedSampleData` does), `-tab plan|progress` (and, where there's a sidebar, `-tab foods|mealplan|supplements|history`), `-daysAgo 1` (Today on an earlier day), `-log search|scan|camera|photoLibrary|describe|quickAdd` to open a logger, `-photo meal.jpg` with `-log photoLibrary` (a picture in the app's Documents, read the way a photo-library pick is, since a simulator can't be made to pick one), `-showSupplements` or `-showSettings` to open those screens, and `-liveScanDemo`, which feeds the live scanner (`-log scan`, or Supplements → Scan the bottle) a rendered package and a label taller than the screen, panned frame by frame through the real OCR, since the simulator has no camera.
