# Stadig Macro

Stadig Macro is built for the least time in the app. Most visits are one of these:

- **Today is the logger** (on a phone; `TodayLogger`). At the top, "kcal left" as the hero numeral over a tall ruler, with what's eaten and the target under it, and protein, carbs and fat as "g left" over thin rulers in their colours (carbs and fat only if shown); the eyebrow over it says the day and where the plan is ("Wed, Nov 11 · Cut, week 1 of 4"). Below, the day as a thread of meals (a moment each, `Timeline.moments`, with its time and calories), the newest nearest the field: a tap on a food opens it in place (`InlineAmount`): its amount in big numerals between − and + (held, they repeat), a ruler to drag it from nothing to two and a half times what was logged, half to double as chips, and a … menu with the full amount panel (units, time, nutrients) and Delete; the numbers move as the amount does, and Done, typing or opening another food saves it once, with an Undo. It opens on the amount as its unit is measured (90 g, not the 89.53 g half a cup came to), and a chip sets its own amount (45 g is 45 g; ± and the ruler move in steps). A food that isn't a catalog food (a quick add, an estimate) opens the amount panel instead. A swipe or a long press deletes. Above the field, **Usually around now**: four tiles (two at the accessibility text sizes, where the header and the foot also stop growing at the largest standard size so the thread keeps its room) from Log again's ranking for this hour, each logging its usual amount now in one tap; while a meal is open (its last food under 45 minutes ago) they're **Goes with …** instead, the foods most often eaten in the same moment as that meal's (`GoesWith`, NutritionEngine), and neither offers what's in the meal already. From 5 PM, with no meal open and 1,200 kcal or less left, they give way to **What fits tonight**: up to three of the usual foods for now, alone or two that have been eaten together in the last eight weeks, that don't go over what's left (by more than 60 kcal) and fill at least a quarter of it, ranked by the protein left they cover (going well past it counts against them) and then by how closely they land (`WhatFits`, NutritionEngine); each says what it leaves, or "On target", with the calories and protein after it on thin rulers, and logs in one tap. Foods on the thread are said the way someone would say them (`FoodLabel.spoken`, NutritionEngine): what the food is and how it was cooked, its state or its fat ("Chicken breast, roasted", "White rice, cooked", "Greek yogurt, lowfat", "Espresso"), never a fruit's "raw"; the tiles, What fits and the chips of foods typed before say only what it is ("Chicken breast"), and a typed match leads with the short name and follows it with the rest of the catalog's detail, which tells "meat only" from "meat and skin". The full name is on the food's amount. The meal just logged is highlighted with an **Undo** in its header for 6 seconds, and the numeral says what it took off. At the foot, one field, "Add food, or say what you ate", with the camera (a barcode or label; a long press offers a photo of the plate) and the microphone (a description read by Apple's model, or the keyboard's dictation without one). Typing reads what's typed (`AddFoodText`, NutritionEngine): commas separate foods (not one between digits: "rice 1,5 kg"), each part's number and unit are its amount ("chick 200" is 200 g, "2 eggs" two of the food's portion, "1/2 cup oats", "half a banana", "yogurt x2" twice the usual, "2 x 150 g", "6 oz steak", a drink's ounces fluid ones; a weight for a food counted only in servings is its usual amount), and "500 kcal" is a quick add. Matches rise above the field, the person's own foods first for the same words (`AddFoodRanking`), the best one nearest the field with a "return" key cap; Return logs the best match for every part at once, at the amounts typed, with one Undo, and keeps the keyboard up for the next food (typed on while it's still finding them, the field keeps only what came after, so a second Return doesn't log them twice). Words nothing matches are worked out where they were typed, where Apple's model can read a description (on the device, or Private Cloud Compute when allowed and entitled, as Describe does): their match says "Estimate “burrito bowl”", Return puts a row, "Working out “burrito bowl”", on the thread at once, and the foods the model names that the catalog has take its place as estimates ("· estimate" after the amount, ≈ on the calories), logged at the time Return was pressed, with the fresh meal's Undo; under them, "Estimated from “burrito bowl”" with **Looks right**, which keeps them as they are (it sits with the foods rather than in the meal's header, which a long meal has scrolled up under the numbers) (`InPlaceEstimate` in FoodIntelligence, `ThreadEstimates` in NutritionEngine). Words that name no food (`VagueWords` in NutritionEngine: a stand-in like "whatever", "something", "stuff" or "fridge", or nothing but a meal, as in "a big dinner", "lunch out", "fast food" or "leftovers") aren't logged: the row turns to "Log this for “whatever was in the fridge”?" with what the model made of it and its total, **Log** (which logs those foods at the time Return was pressed, with Undo, no longer estimates since they've been looked at), and ··· with Change in the Logger and Don't Log. What the model names that the catalog hasn't, and a read that fails, say so in a row of their own with **Logger**, which opens the full logger with the words, and ×; the tiles drop to two while such a row is up. Without Apple Intelligence the words go on to the logger and are searched there. While typing, the header folds to one line, "1,601 → 1,271 kcal left", with the macros after it. So Today reads as its design does (what's left, the day's food, the usual foods, the field), the rest of the day is folded under one quiet line after the thread, **More today** (`MoreToday`), in the thread's own voice: the eyebrow, and on the right what's waiting ("Your plan is ready · Weigh in · 2 supplements", or the day's weight once it's in, a fast running, the meal plan; protein to go isn't said, since the numbers say it). Closed, it's the thread's end, kept in sight over the foot; it's put away while typing. A short day sits at the foot, as the design's thread does (the list's content is anchored to its bottom): before anything's logged, "Nothing logged yet today" and More today sit just above the tiles rather than under the numbers with a gap below, and the first food lands there without the line moving. A tap opens it in place, under its line, and it stays as it was left: the targets' note, the plan's actions and the weekly check-in (and a nutrient over its safe upper limit), the weigh-in, From your plan, Same as yesterday and saved meals, "Done logging today?", then meal timing, supplements (a reminder's tap opens it and scrolls to them), drinks and "This week's food" (the ultra-processed swap, a short nutrient's fix, a fermented food). The widget shows what's left on the Home Screen and Lock Screen. Today only ever shows today, and logs at now whichever day the Log tab was left on.
- **One composer, on every tab.** On a phone, Log, Plan and Progress have Today's composer at their foot too, above the tab bar (`TabComposer`): the same field, matches, chips, camera and microphone, logging where you are rather than jumping to Today. Typing raises the same matches over it, Return logs the best match for each part (comma lists, quick adds, the amounts typed) with the toast and its Undo over the field, the keyboard staying up for the next food; words nothing matches are worked out in place, their row ("Working out …", "Log this for …?", or why not, with Logger and ×) over the field while it's a row, and their foods land on Today's thread, where **Looks right** is. While it has the keyboard the page gives way to Today's typing state, so typing reads the same on every tab: the ground, what's left in a line at the top ("2,200 → 1,870 kcal left", for the Log tab's day), the matches rising over the field, and with nothing typed today's usual foods over it, a tap each (`tab.usual.N`; not for a day other than today). An iPad's page stays, with the composer at its foot. Log, Plan and Progress scrolled to their end rest clear of the field and its fade (`roomForComposer()`, and Log's list reading `composerRoom` itself: the composer's inset doesn't reach a scroll view inside the tab's navigation stack, which made room for the tab bar only). The tab bar never shrinks to a button on scrolling (`tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.never)`), since the composer standing on it wouldn't follow it down. A tap on the screen or a drag down it puts the keyboard away. On the Log tab it logs to the day shown there, at this time of day, and says so ("Add food to Tuesday", or the date further than a week off); words nothing matches for a day other than today go to the logger with them, which logs to that day, since estimates in place are today's thread's. While foods are picked out on the Log tab the selection's bar takes the field's place. The weigh-in that was in the old bar is Progress's scale button, at the top of the screen, and under More today on Today. The app reopens on the tab it was left on, and widgets, links, Siri and controls land on today at now.
- **The day, meal by meal.** On iPhone the Log tab is the day's meals as cards under a pinned head: the day as an eyebrow over the title "Log", a round calendar button for any other day (and **Today** beside it on another day), the week strip (five days back, today and tomorrow, the day on screen on the card colour, today's date in the accent, a dot on a day with food), and one line of what's left ("1,601 kcal · 102 g protein left", or calories eaten where there's no calorie target) over a thin ruler of the day's calories. With room, a bar for each macro follows while Show carbs and fat is on. A card is a meal as the engine reads one (foods within 45 minutes of each other), named Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner or Snack from its time and size, or after the saved meal it is, with its time, its protein (and carbs and fat while they're shown) beside coloured dots, its calories as a numeral, and a **…** menu: Add to the meal (on any day), Select to move or copy, Repeat now, Save as meal and Remove; while foods are picked out the menu gives way to a button that takes them into this meal. Its foods are rows of the name a person would say, the amount and the calories. A meal from the week's meal prep is an outlined card where it falls ("DINNER · FROM YOUR PLAN", the dish, its calories and protein) with **Log**, or a tick once it's logged. Each food has a marker read from its name and the name a person would say ("Chicken breast"), with the catalog's full name on its amount panel. The marker follows a food everywhere it's listed or opened (but the phone's Log cards, which keep to name, amount and calories), in the same square: search and Log again, a saved meal's foods, History, the wide Log tab's rows, the Foods table, the amount panel, the Foods inspector, and Edit Food and a scanned label, where it follows the name as it's typed. A row of several foods has a fork and knife in the square instead. Between the cards the open hours fold into one quiet row, "ADD AT" with up to three one-tap hours (**+ 9 AM** logs at that hour on that day) and **More** for the rest (`LogHours.offered`, NutritionEngine): on today the soonest hours after now, or before now the latest; on another day breakfast, lunch and dinner (8 AM, noon, 6 PM) where the run has them, then its first hours; a run wholly outside the Log tab's hours stays behind Earlier or Later. So a day with nothing logged reads as a few calm rows rather than a wall of hours, and any hour stays two taps away. More opens every hour of the run, 7 AM to 8 PM unless set otherwise: Settings has a first and last hour, and Automatic ends the hour before the bedtime set under Drinks, where there is one. Other hours stay under Earlier and Later, and food in them always shows (at the accessibility text sizes the hours are always open, four to a row). On a past day, the usual meals not yet logged sit in dashes where they'd go: **+** logs that meal at that time, and a tap on the rest logs something else then. With foods picked out, each **+** and hour takes them there. On today, a line marks the hour it is, with Log again's chips under it. Today opens on now, another day on its first meal. On a day ahead the line counts all the day's food; on today, what's been eaten, with what's coming beside it. Under a past day's meals is its foot, the same as Today has for today: how complete it was, the night's fast, its doses and its drinks. With room (iPad, the Mac, Vision Pro) the day stays hour by hour, each hour with a **+** and each food's macros in fixed columns; a + there logs at now in the hour it is, a minute after the hour's last food otherwise, and at the top of an empty hour. Entries keep their minutes, and a day is still moments underneath: foods logged within 45 minutes of each other are one for the meal in the logger, saved meals, catch-up and meal timing. Tapping a food opens its amount; a swipe deletes it or logs it again; on a Mac, a right-click has what the swipes do. The logger, opened from the composer's camera or for words it couldn't match, defaults to now, with "30m ago", "1h ago", "2h ago" or any time one tap away. A time picked on the wheel for today goes on the day nearest now (11:30 PM picked just after midnight is last night, 7 PM picked at noon is this evening), and the logger's title, its time bar and the Add button name the weekday when that isn't the day on screen. A picked time lasts only while that logger is open.
- **One logger, one amount sheet.** Search, scan, photo, describe and quick add are one logger, and each ends back at its search with the meal in sight and Undo over it. On a phone it's the composer opened, so searching reads as Today's typing state (`LoggerSheet`, `LoggerComposer`, `LoggerSearch`): at the top an eyebrow saying where the foods go ("TODAY · NOW", "TODAY · 9:00 AM", "THU, NOV 12 · 7:00 PM"), a **Quick add** chip and Done as a round ×, and under them Today's typing header for the logger's day (`TypingHeader`: "2,200 → 1,969 kcal left" over a thin ruler, "Then 122 g protein · … left", the "→" being what the best match would log). The matches rise from the field at the foot, the best nearest it, as the composer's do; with nothing typed the suggestions do the same, the likeliest group (Same as yesterday, then My meals, Log again, Favorites, My foods) and each group's best nearest the thumb. At the foot, on the composer's ground with the list fading under it, the meal so far as a tile (put away while typing), the search's suggestions while typing, the time as chips (Now, 30m ago, 1h ago, 2h ago and the clock) and the composer's field: 54 points, round-ended, the accent's line while focused, "Search foods", and with it empty the camera (a barcode or label; held, a photo of the plate or one chosen) and the green microphone (describe), with text a clear button and the green arrow. The other ways (scan, photo, describe, quick add) keep the same top with their name in the header's place, and at the foot the time and the field at rest, a tap on which is the search with the keyboard; its camera and microphone go between the ways. A review the model read stays in reach from the search under the header ("From your words · 4 foods", Back to it). Rows are the composer's matches (`MatchRow`, shared with `ComposerMatches`): the marker on the surface, the name it's said by with the rest of the catalog's name quieter beside it, a line of the amount, calories and protein, and a round **+** that logs right away; a tap opens the amount. The best match of what's typed sits on a card outlined in the accent with a `return` key cap and no + (the field's arrow is its +), and Return logs it, as the composer's does, keeping the keyboard for the next food; with nothing typed Return puts the keyboard away. An amount typed with the words is read as the composer reads it, one food at a time: "chick 200" finds chicken, its rows say 200 g, and the header, + and Return log that (`LoggerSession.typedPart`). Calories typed as calories ("300 kcal", "coffee 120 cal") are a quick add, read as the composer reads them: "Quick add 300 kcal" is the best row, Return or its + logs it, and a tap opens quick add with the calories (and name) filled in. In the pane beside the Log tab (iPad, the Mac, Vision Pro), for a pointer and a keyboard, the search stays a capsule at the top (the way's name under the day in the other ways) with the same rows read down from it, the best (with its +) first, and the meal so far, the time and the ways to log as a segmented row at the bottom, which gives way to the search's suggestions while something's typed. An amount is one sheet, as Today's open food: the food's source (or that the numbers are yours) and quality tags, the amount in big numerals between − and + (held, they repeat), a ticked ruler to drag from nothing to two and a half times the usual amount, chips from half to double (with Delete beside them for a logged food), the units as chips, the numbers on a card with vitamins and minerals folded under them, and a keypad of raised keys with ½ ¼ ¾ ⅓ (off for grams, millilitres and calories); the numeral opens selected, so the first key replaces it. Where there's a pane (iPad, the Mac, Vision Pro) the logger sits beside the day, and a food's amount takes the pane's place until it's done.
- **Food for later.** A food with a time ahead of now is an ordinary entry: there's no planned state. It counts for nothing until its time, then counts like any other, whether or not it was eaten, unless it's been removed: on its day a 6:30 PM dinner joins the ring, Health and any fast at 6:30, and a wholly planned day that passes counts in check-ins like any other. Until then it's dimmed with a small clock, read aloud as "Later", and the line of what's left says "1,230 left · 624 later". Days ahead open from the week strip, the calendar or ⌘]: what's left there is the target minus all that day's food, and a day to come refuses what has to have happened (marking it done or fasted, doses, water and weigh-ins). A food's time is noticed by a timer, a day or clock change, or coming back to the app, so with the app closed its Health write waits for the next launch, foreground or sync wake, dated correctly. Widgets and complications hold what's coming through tomorrow.
- **Moving and copying.** Holding a food picks it out, and then a tap picks out more; a tap on an hour's name picks out its foods (the rest of them, when some are), and on a Mac ⌘-click and ⇧-click do. On a phone the composer at the Log tab's foot becomes the selection's bar: Move or Copy, To Now, To Today, To Tomorrow, To Dates (several, from a month), or Delete, and each hour's **+** becomes a way to take them there. To Now is a meal eaten now, in order, a second apart; an hour's arrow keeps their gaps; To Today, To Tomorrow and To Dates keep each food's time of day. Moving to several dates moves to the earliest and leaves copies on the rest. Everything has Undo, and that one takes the copies away with the move. Holding an hour's name offers Repeat Now, Save or Update Meal and Remove. A selection survives changing day, and leaving the tab clears it. A food's amount on the Log tab has Move and Copy too, which pick it out with anything typed there saved first; from the meal in the logger it doesn't, since the selection's bar and the hours are out of reach.
- **Which day.** There's one selected day. It goes back to today on leaving the Log tab and Foods (hopping between them keeps it, so a food found for Monday lands on Monday), on launch, at midnight, after 10 minutes away unless a selection or a logger is open (on the Mac, another app in front or the Mac asleep counts as away, and on an iPad another app taking the focus), and on anything arriving from outside the app, a supplement reminder for an earlier day included. A Today button shows on any other day.
- **One tap.** "Log again" ranks foods by how recently and how often you eat them at this time of day, and logs the same amount with one tap. "Same as yesterday" copies what you ate around this time on a recent day. Every log shows Undo, and so does every delete, even of a whole moment ("Deleted 3 foods"). An entry reopens in the portion it was logged in while that portion still holds the logged amount, otherwise in g, ml or servings at the amount logged, so a catalog update can't change it. Changing only the time keeps its numbers.
- **The meal so far, and saved meals.** In the logger's foot, above its time chips, the meal being added to stays in sight: the foods one logged at the logger's time would join (its moment, whether they went in from this logger or before it opened), with its time, its foods and its total, so a meal of several foods doesn't vanish into a toast at each **+**. A tap opens it to change an amount, take a food out, or **Save as Meal** under a name (left blank, its three biggest foods: "Bread, cheese and mayonnaise +2"). A saved meal logs again as its foods, each its own entry at the time picked, so the log, food quality and each food's next portion are as if they'd been logged by hand: **+** on it in the logger's My Meals (above Log Again, best for now first), or a tap opens it to untick a food for this time, change an amount for good, or rename or delete it (with Undo). A search finds meals by name, or by a food in them ("turkey" finds the sandwich), above the foods. Today's Log Again offers up to three meals eaten within 90 minutes of this time of day on other days in the last month, by name, until it's eaten within 90 minutes of now (by the clock: dinner at 11:30 PM still counts at 12:15 AM), and drops "Same as yesterday" when that is one of them. Holding an hour's name saves its foods as a meal, or, when they're a saved meal with an amount changed, updates the meal. A meal counts as eaten in any moment that holds all of its foods, logged as the meal or food by food. On a desktop, Foods has Meals beside My Foods, with the selected meal and when it was eaten in the inspector (a double-click or Return logs it), and the inspector shows the meal a food would join above its Log button.
- **Catching up from habit.** A day that wasn't logged as it happened is filled in from what you usually eat. On a past day on the Log tab (Today's **Catch up** when yesterday is empty, the week strip, or History's Show in Log), each time you usually eat with nothing logged near it is a row in its hour: the time, and what you last ate then on another day ("Same as yesterday · 650 kcal", by its name when it's a saved meal), which **+** logs at that time on that day; a tap on the rest of the row opens the logger at that time for something else, and **Log all**, on the line of what's left, logs every one left with one Undo ("Logged 3 meals"). A usual time is one with a meal (50 kcal or more, so not a black coffee) within the hour of it on a quarter of the days with a meal in the last four weeks, and two days at least, taken most-eaten first with each day's nearest meal, so lunch at 12:30 and snacks at 2 and 4 stay three times; it sits at the middle of those meals, to 5 minutes. Catching up yesterday learns from today too, so today's lunch is the one offered for yesterday's. A day marked complete or fasted has nothing left to catch up. The logger's suggestions follow its time, not the clock: at 9 PM, a time picked at noon gets lunch. My Meals and Log Again rank for the time being logged at ("Log again for 12:30 PM"), recency still counted from today; "Same as yesterday" is the meal of two or more foods eaten around then on another day of the last week, until that day has one then; and on a past day the time bar's chips are the usual times, so noon, then 3, then 7 is a tap each with the suggestions following, and the logger's title names the day. Today's usual times so far are in the time menu and above the time wheel.
- **Your shape.** The six scores as one figure (`ScoreShape`), with a row each saying which app measures it (`SourceChip`) and what it says, from `StatsBoard.lines` over what both apps publish. Each row ends in how far its score moved this month ("+2" in green, "steady" in grey, "−1" in amber, from the scores both apps record day by day; the trend word until there's a week of history, nothing for someone new), and the figure has the scores as the month three back began dashed behind it, named under it ("Now", "August"; `StatsBoard.earlier`). Stadig Macro measures Vitality and nutrition's half of Discipline; with Stadig Lift not set up, its scores show "–" and "Measured by Stadig Lift. Also free." On a phone, Progress opens on a row into it (the figure small and the scores this app has), and Your shape is a page of its own, pushed as Stadig Lift's pages are (its eyebrow and large title under the bar's round back button, the tab bar away, `DetailHeader`, and the composer too, `pushedPage()`), as are Plan's Check-ins (maintenance over its likely range on a ruler, and each week's check-in a row of a card) and Progress's All nutrients; with room it's the first card. Progress itself, under its eyebrow ("Cut, week 1 of 4") and a round Weigh In button: the trend's weight as the hero numeral with how fast it's moving (in green on pace, amber faster or slower than the phase asks, red the wrong way) and a sentence on the phase ("On pace to reach 79.8 kg by Dec 9.", from `PhasePace`), the scale's readings as dots under the trend line with 1M, 2M, 6M and 1Y; calories as bars (partly logged days grey) against the target (solid) and maintenance (dashed), with when the phase began; nutrition discipline as a score over a ruler; then food quality (on the palette's good-to-bad scale: whole food and staples green, processed amber, ultra-processed red), meal timing, and vitamins and minerals, each a thin ruler with its target marked two-thirds along, over a row into **All nutrients**: every nutrient followed, on a page of its own, in cards for what's running short, what's over, what's on target and what too little of the food lists, each with why it matters and what the number rests on.
- **Vitamins and minerals, quietly.** Every entry keeps its micronutrients, scaled to the portion. Progress shows the last week of complete days against your targets (the US National Academies' recommended intakes for your age and sex; fibre at 14 g per 1,000 kcal; sodium and saturated fat as limits), with "unknown" rather than "low" when too little of what you ate lists a nutrient: packaged foods often carry only the label basics. When a watched one runs short, Today offers one food that fixes it, preferring ones you already eat, at an ordinary portion, logged with one tap; foods you open show their vitamins and minerals as a share of your targets. They go to Health and the CSV export too.
- **Food quality, not just macros.** Protein shakes and Pop-Tarts can hit any macro target, so every food carries how processed it is (NOVA: whole, kitchen staple, processed, ultra-processed) and whether it's fermented with live cultures, and entries keep both. Progress shows the last week's calories by group, ultra-processed against an aim of under 20% (people eat more and gain more fat on the same macros when most of it is ultra-processed), fermented foods against "most days", and fibre. Over 30% ultra-processed, Today names the biggest source and what does its job as whole food ("Oats with fruit fill the same spot"), going by whole words in its name, so "chocolate" isn't taken for cola or "barbecue" for a bar; cookies, brownies, doughnuts and candy get "Fruit, or yogurt with honey", and pastrami or ham gets "Chicken, eggs or fish cooked plain", however much protein it has. Short on fermented foods, it offers one to log with one tap, yours first. Open Food Facts supplies its own NOVA group (or one from the product's category); USDA's are estimated from each food's group and name. Foods without one (quick adds, your own) count as unknown, not as whole or processed.
- **Meal prep in an afternoon.** The Plan tab plans a week of meals sized to your targets: pick which meals to prep (any of breakfast, lunch, dinner and a snack), your diet and what to leave out. Lunches and dinners are two bowls (a protein, a carb and a vegetable in one of seven flavours, Japanese curry among them, alternating so no day repeats a dish), or one for all of them from a single batch ("One dish all week" in the setup, which carries over to the next week, or "the same meal for lunch and dinner" in a request), breakfast is overnight oats, egg muffins or a yogurt bowl, and the snack is whichever fills what's left, protein first. Every portion is solved from catalog nutrients, so a full day lands within a few percent of your calories and protein; meals you don't prep keep their room ("Dinner is yours: about 700 kcal, 45 g protein"). Then: the grocery list by aisle, in the units things are sold in (3 cans, 1 jar, 2.9 lb), with checkboxes and Share; and prep day as a timed list that overlaps the pot, the oven and your hands (about 2 hours for 14 containers and 7 jars; a grain two bowls share is cooked once, and each bowl's roast is its own step), with what goes in each container and where it keeps (fridge Mon–Wed, freezer Thu–Sun, a reminder each evening to move tomorrow's). Swap any dish with one tap, or type "no chicken, something spicy": word rules map the request to the building blocks, and Apple's on-device model (Private Cloud Compute when allowed and the app is entitled) reads it too, for wording the rules get the wrong way round ("anything but chicken"). The model only decides whether something the request names is wanted or left out; anything else it returns is dropped, since it lists the whole menu or an allergy from words that name none of it ("high protein" came back as no rice, no pasta, no potatoes). The diet, cheaper, quicker and one dish come from the words alone. Negatives and allergies leave things out ("I don't want fish", "my partner doesn't like fish", "no fish and pork", "allergic to soy", "lactose intolerant", "a nut allergy", "coeliac"), while "no rice and use quinoa" and "no pasta, just rice" still ask for the rest. A dish's name is read whole ("Japanese curry" is the Japanese curry, not teriyaki and an Indian curry), and words are matched whole ("breakfast" isn't "fast"). "High protein" changes nothing, since every plan is sized to the protein target. Once the week has started, a swap, "Other dishes" or a typed change touches only the dishes still to come: days already eaten and meals logged keep theirs. On the day, "From your plan" on Today logs each meal with one tap, as one entry that keeps its processing and fermented flags. On a phone the meal plan ("Meal plan") is cards on the ink ground: the week opens on when it is ("THIS WEEK · NOV 11 – NOV 17"), how many dishes and containers it is and what they're sized to, then each dish with its containers as a big numeral ("7×"), its calories and protein a portion and **Swap**; then rows that open the grocery list ("16 items") and prep day ("Sunday · 2 h") as pages of their own (where what's in each container is), the request field and the days; **Start prep** at the foot opens prep day's steps. It's a page of its own, as the design's is, with neither the tab bar nor the composer, and so are the grocery list, which opens on what's still to buy ("16 to buy", "All in the basket"), and prep day, on how long and how many containers ("About 2 h, 14 containers"). With room (an iPad, the Mac, Vision Pro), **Start Prep** in the Meal Plan's toolbar and beside Prep day opens counter mode (below).

- **Supplements, ticked off.** The Supplements screen (Plan tab, Settings, or Edit on Today) holds your stack. - **Meal timing and fasts, if you want them.** Off by default, since nudges about when to eat aren't right for everyone. Turned on in Settings, everything comes from the log, with nothing to tap: a meal is a moment of 50 kcal or more (black coffee, tea or a splash of milk isn't), and an eating day runs from 4 AM, so a snack after midnight ends the evening. Only complete days (and today so far) are read: an unlogged snack would look like a long gap. **Start fast** sets a goal (13 to 72 hours with a tap, up to 7 days on the stepper) from the end of the last meal or a time picked; Today then shows how long as a numeral over a ruler toward the goal, the phase (digesting, insulin low, fat burning, ketosis, deep ketosis, as ranges with what's reasonably established in people) and when the next begins, with End fast. Logging a meal ends it, on any device, and its Undo puts the fast back; dessert added to the meal it began from moves its start instead. Starting and ending both have Undo, a start can't be set before the last meal, and turning meal timing off ends the fast. Past its goal, a fast counts only as far as its goal for the scale, the estimate and fasted days unless it was ended by hand, and one with no meal logged 12 hours past its goal is taken as over at its goal. The first goal over a day asks you to read who shouldn't (insulin or a sulfonylurea, an SGLT2 inhibitor, pregnancy, an eating disorder, under 18 or underweight, gout or heart medicines) and when to stop; under 18 or underweight, nothing over a day is offered; over 3 days says to involve a doctor; a lean bulk or recomp says what a long fast costs it. Notifications come at 12 hours, a day (salt and minerals) and 3 days, and at the goal with how to break it. Whole days inside a fast count as fasted days. A fast of a day or more leaves its weigh-ins and the 3 days after out of the trend, the estimate and the check-in learn as if those days were eaten at maintenance, the check-in holds meanwhile, and Today says the scale is water. The fast is on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (a Live Activity, started again when the app opens, since the system ends one after 8 hours), in a Fasting widget (Lock Screen and Home Screen), on the Watch, in History's Overnight column and in the CSV export (`fasts.csv`).

Medium: the same number and ruler, with each macro's grams left over its ruler and the search, camera, scanner and (until it's done) weigh-in buttons beside it. ## Apple Watch

The Watch app is the shortest visit of all: a glance at what's left, and a tap to log. It runs on its own, with or without the iPhone nearby. The Digital Crown moves down through its pages:

- **Today.** A face, the whole screen: "kcal left today" as an eyebrow beside the time, the number big, and a level in the accent that rises from the foot as the day's calories are eaten ("602 eaten · of 2,340" across its foot); protein's line crosses the face at the height of how much protein is eaten (on a good day it leads the level), "Protein · 130 g left" over it, and the edge is marked in tenths. Whatever the level has risen over is drawn again in ink on it. Scrolled on: carbs and fat (when they're shown on the phone), the day's badge (the phase, "Day 5 of 21", "Your targets", or a life mode with its note), **Weigh In** (Weighed In once there's been one today), and **Mark Day Complete** once something's logged.
- **Log Again.** A face each, the whole screen: "Usually now" (or "Same as yesterday" while nothing's logged near now, then the ten foods `LogAgain` ranks for this time of day, as on the phone), each with its marker on a tile, its amount big, its short name (`FoodLabel.short`), calories and protein, where it is in the list as a column of dots at the edge, and **Log** as a band across the foot with the calories left after it. The Digital Crown moves from face to face, and past the last on to the next page. Log, or Double Tap on the face in view, logs the same amount, now, with a haptic, and the logged face takes the screen for five seconds: "Logged", each food with its marker, short name, amount and calories, kcal left before and after ("1,455 → 1,204 left"), and **Tap to undo** across the foot, a line over it running out while the chance lasts; a tap anywhere else puts it away (`LoggedFace`). Water, a dose, a weigh-in, a fast and the day's mark keep a few seconds of Undo along the foot.
- **Water**, when drinks are on: water and drinks so far against the day's aim, and a glass or a bottle with a tap (8 oz and 16.9 oz for people who weigh in pounds).
- **Fasting**, when meal timing is on: the running fast as a ring toward its goal with its phase, and End Fast; or the time since the last meal, with a 13, 16, 18 or 24 hour fast a tap away (longer ones start on the phone, where the safety notes are). Undo takes back a start or an end.
- **Supplements**, when the day has doses: the open ones whose time has come (the time of day nearest now, and earlier ones still unmarked), a tap each or **Take All**, and how many are due later.
- **Weigh In.** A face: "The scale says" (once there's a reading today; "This morning" before) over the weight big, the trend under it ("trend 81.4", `WristToday.trendKg`), and a scale at the edge beside the crown with the trend's mark and the reading's. The Digital Crown corrects it, from the last weigh-in, 0.1 kg or 0.2 lb a detent, inside the weights the trend accepts; **Keep** (or Save, before today's reading), or Double Tap. Today's reading kept unchanged saves nothing new. The edge swipe goes back.
- Back after a couple of minutes away, it opens on Today again.
- **Complications.** Calories Left and Protein Left, each as a circular, rectangular, inline and corner complication, Today (both, with rulers) for the Smart Stack, Fasting (the fast toward its goal, or the time since the last meal), whose timeline steps through the phases with the app closed, Water (circular and inline: the litres or ounces so far over a level rising toward the day's aim, a dash while drinks aren't tracked) and Your Shape (circular: the six scores as one figure in the accent over the hexagon and its spokes, from what both apps publish, read through the family's shared files with `StatsBoard.lines`; a score not measured yet leaves its corner out). They're drawn the phone's way (`WatchShared/ComplicationViews.swift`): circular is the number with its unit under it in the macro's colour ("102" over "g P"), rectangular an accent eyebrow ("Kcal left today"), the number as the hero with "of 2,200", and a ticked ruler, the corner the number curved into it with the gauge bent along the bezel, Today both numbers over their rulers on the ink surface, and Fasting the hours or the running time over a ruler toward the goal, in the accent. On a tinted face only the eyebrows, symbols and fills take the tint, and the ruler's ticks are gaps. Tapping one opens the app. The app writes `WristSnapshot` into the Watch's App Group whenever the numbers change and reloads them; a timeline entry starts the new day at nothing eaten at midnight, and the timeline is read again just after, so the new day's planned food counts with the Watch app closed.

**The same numbers as the phone.** The Watch opens Stadig Macro's own CloudKit store (`RecordStore(name: "MacroTrack", cloudKitContainer: "iCloud.dev.svindland.stadig.macro")`) and reads and writes the same records as the iPhone, iPad and Mac. They're declared once, in `Apps/MacroTrack/Records`, which both apps compile; custom foods, favourites and meal plans stay in the iPhone app, which has the catalog and the planner. It also mirrors the family's shared files through `FamilySync` (read only: the phone writes them), so Stadig Lift's calendar (deload weeks), summary (training days, steps, measurements) and the life modes reach it. `MacroAnalyst.analyse` runs over all of it, off the main thread, and `WristToday` (NutritionEngine) turns the analysis into the pages. An entry carries its own numbers, so logging again needs no food catalog (off.db is 590 MB; FoodIntelligence and MealPlanner don't build for watchOS). The Watch never applies the weekly check-in, which is the phone's job; until the phone has, the Watch shows the targets the due check-in sets, as the phone would. CloudKit's pushes wake it (the `remote-notification` background mode) so the complications follow what's logged elsewhere: it stays awake for the import, then until the analysis has written the complications' snapshot and reloaded them (`WristModel.analysisFinished`, at most 12 seconds more), so watchOS doesn't suspend it halfway.

**Apple Health, once.** The Watch writes nothing to Health: a sample's sync identifier only replaces samples from the same app, and the Watch app is a source of its own, so a food written by both would be there twice. Instead the iPhone (and the iPad and Vision Pro, the same app) writes what syncs in from devices that don't write it themselves: `HealthBackfill` checks the last 30 days of foods, water, doses taken and typed weigh-ins against Health by sync identifier and version, at launch, whenever another device's changes arrive, each time the app comes to the front, when the phone is unlocked and when Health is connected. What Health already holds from Stadig Macro on any device is left alone; the rest is written. Every write, here as something's logged and by the backfill, takes its version from `Date.healthVersion` (the record's `updatedAt` in whole milliseconds, rounded as the store rounds it), so the backfill knows the phone's own samples; one written by an earlier version, which truncated, a millisecond lower, counts as the same. A ledger on the device remembers what's been handled, so a sample deleted in Health isn't put back. A Health copy comes out only for a deletion the store holds (an Undo on the Watch, a delete on the Mac) or a dose unmarked; a record that's only missing (the store emptied while iCloud changes accounts), written by a newer app version this one can't read, or dated back out of the 30 days keeps its copy. The store's deletions are read only when something handled has gone missing, and only of its kind. It waits while the phone is locked or Health isn't connected yet (and tries again on those triggers), writes nothing while "Save to Health" is off (what arrived meanwhile stays out), and skips an ignored weigh-in and the weigh-in setup saved. So the Mac's logs reach Health too, once the iPhone or iPad sees them.

`Scripts/build.sh macro-watch` builds the Watch app and its complications; the iPhone build (`Scripts/build.sh macro`) carries the Watch app, the Mac and Vision Pro builds don't. On a Watch simulator: `xcrun simctl install <udid> <DerivedData>/Build/Products/Debug-watchsimulator/MacroTrackWatch.app`, then `xcrun simctl launch <udid> dev.svindland.stadig.macro.watchkitapp -seedSampleData -seedPlan -seedSupplements -inMemoryStore`. Its DEBUG launch arguments: `-inMemoryStore` and `-resetData` as on the phone; `-seedSampleData` (the phone's sample person, with USDA numbers in place of the catalog, and today logged up to lunch), `-seedPlan` (starts the recommended plan, so there are calorie targets), `-seedSupplements` (a stack due through the day, two weeks taken, coffee and water, drinks on), `-imperial` (pounds and fluid ounces), `-page logAgain|water|supplements|weigh`, `-seedHour 9` (with `-seedSampleData`, today logged up to that hour whatever the time, before then dated a minute ago, so the level shows at night), `-scrollToEnd` (Today scrolled down), `-demoLog` (logs the first Log Again food and keeps its Undo up), `-complications` (every complication drawn in the app, since a script can't put one on a face: the first screen is the design's face, calories in the corner and across the middle, Your shape, protein and water along the foot, the design's shape where no scores are published yet), `-shareSnapshot` (with `-inMemoryStore`, writes the sample's snapshot to the App Group anyway, so the real complications show it) and `-largeText`.

## Packages

- **NutritionEngine** is pure Swift over SystemCore: records, the trend, energy balance, check-ins, the phase planner, targets, the analysis behind every action, "Log again" ranking and CSV tables. `MealTiming` reads meals, gaps, overnight fasts and the week's pattern from the log; `Fasting` has fasts' runs, phases, milestones, fasted days and what a long fast does to the scale and the estimate (`Fasting.adjustment`), and `FastSafety` who shouldn't, when to stop and how to break one. Supplements (the stack, dose schedules, taken and skipped doses, upper limits) and drinks (water, fluid from drinks by name, caffeine timing and the cut-back) live here too. So do the estimates on Today's thread (`ThreadEstimates`: which rows and unconfirmed foods are still there, and what "Looks right" does to an entry), the swap lines (`QualitySwap`) and the rules behind the one-tap fixes (`FoodFix`: which food to offer, at what portion); the app finds the foods in the catalog and the person's own. The week desk's week is `WeekDesk`: its days' meals (a moment each, or a plan's dish to come), each day against its own target, the average day, the scale, and a meal held over another day as both days and the week would be, in words.
- **FoodDatabase** ports the React Native app's catalogs and search: SQLite FTS5 over `usda.db` (bundled in the package) and `off.db` (bundled from `Apps/MacroTrack/Catalogs`), ranking with stemming, synonyms, spelling and brand detection (packaged foods' brands, the chains USDA writes in capitals before a food, and each found food's own brand wherever its words were typed), sizes in inches, the portion-units engine, barcode forms, and the nutrition label parser. `NutritionLabel.frame` reads whatever part of a panel one camera frame shows, and `LiveLabel` and `LiveSupplementLabel` merge frames by vote into one panel. `SupplementCatalog` reads `supplements.db`, and `SupplementLabel` reads Supplement Facts panels; the rules that turn label names and units into nutrients (`SupplementIngredients`) are in SystemCore, shared by both. `FoodUnits.logged` decides which portion a logged entry reopens in, and `FoodUnits.parseCount` reads other scripts' digits ("١/٢", "１/２") and refuses counts over 1,000,000. Each catalog keeps at most 4 idle SQLite connections, and `FoodCatalog.releaseMemory()` drops its caches on a memory warning and when the app goes to the background.
- **FoodIntelligence** ports the two-pass AI: the model names foods and amounts, candidates come from the catalog, a second pass breaks near-ties, and amounts resolve from catalog portions. A name the model gives is read as a typed search's is: "hot dog" or "hotdog" is USDA's frankfurter, a bell pepper its sweet pepper, "soy milk" its soymilk and a milkshake its "Milk shakes", a kiwi its kiwifruit, "mac and cheese" its macaroni and cheese, and alone "ranch" is the dressing and "spaghetti" or "macaroni" cooked pasta, while a hot dog bun is still the roll. A restaurant chain the model gives as a food's brand is read as a typed one is: the chain's own item where the catalogs have it under that brand (McDonald's fries, a Domino's pepperoni pizza), and otherwise the food as such a place serves it (fries from Five Guys are fries from fast food, Olive Garden's breadsticks a restaurant's), not another brand's product or a label with the chain's word in its name. Label photos go through Vision OCR and the parser, nutrition and Supplement Facts panels alike; `LabelOCR.frame` reads one live camera frame in a single pass, and `checkLabel` and `checkSupplement` have the model read a live scan's text, keeping only a number printed on that nutrient's own row (never a % Daily Value) and lines Stadig Macro tracks. Without Apple's model, `LabelOCR.label(in:)` reads a label photo in one Vision pass. The on-device model's guardrails refuse requests that call a product a dietary supplement, so those prompts say "a product label". `InPlaceEstimate` decides what a read of words Today matched to nothing logs in place (every food matched to the catalog, weak ones too, as estimates) and what's said instead. It picks the on-device model first (with vision for photos) and Private Cloud Compute when allowed and `PrivateCloud.isEntitled` (SystemCore reads `SystemAIPrivateCloud` from the Info.plist); without the entitlement it never builds a Private Cloud Compute model, not even to ask whether one is there.
- **MealPlanner** builds a week from 55 whole-food building blocks tied to USDA foods (so the numbers never move), seven flavour profiles, breakfasts and snacks, as two bowls for lunches and dinners or one (`PlanPreferences.oneDish`). A small bounded least-squares solver sizes each container to its share of the day; the grocery list converts cooked amounts to raw and dry ones with cooking yields; the prep guide schedules oven rounds of two trays, pots and hands-on jobs on one timeline, one pot per grain. `PlanRequests` maps a request's words to the library ("sick of chicken", "dairy-free", "Asian", "the same meal for lunch and dinner"), negatives and allergies included, and `PlanAssistant` reads it with Apple's on-device model too (Private Cloud Compute only when the device's can't, the setting allows it and the app is entitled), keeping the model's reading only for the foods and flavours the request names (`PlanAssistant.merge`). `swap`, `replan` and `shuffle` ("Other dishes") keep the days before today and the meals logged, and `swappable` lists the dishes still to come.
- **HealthBridge** gained per-app permissions, food writes (food correlations keyed by entry id, replaced on edit), dietary water, and weigh-in reads and writes. Writes and deletes for one food entry run one at a time in the order made, so an Undo waits for the save it undoes, and `disable()` keeps tests and UI tests out of Health. `weighInChanges` reads weigh-ins from where the last read left off (an anchor the app keeps on the device), with the ones deleted in Health.

## Platforms

One target runs natively on iPhone, iPad, the Mac and Apple Vision Pro, synced through Stadig Macro's own CloudKit container. Layout goes by width, not device: a compact width (an iPhone, or an iPad in a narrow Split View or Slide Over) is the phone app exactly; a regular width (an iPad full screen or in a wide split, the Mac, Vision Pro) gets the desktop layout. The phone stays built for the least time in the app; the iPad and the Mac are where you find and compare foods, browse nutrients, plan the week and the grocery list, and look back over history, with logging a keystroke away.

**A second device.** A second device, or a reinstall, that finds the app not set up asks iCloud once whether the app has records there (`ICloudArrival`, SystemStore): the account, then one record id from the store's zone. Nothing there, no account, or no answer within 10 seconds goes straight to onboarding, after a blank moment rather than a flash of the wait. Records there show "Bringing your food log from iCloud" instead of onboarding until this launch's first import has ended; the data arriving opens Today, and a failure to set up or import ends the wait with its reason under the spinner. "Set up as new" skips the wait. Settings' Sync row follows CloudKit's own events (`SyncState`): "Syncing…", "Up to date" with when it last synced, or "Not syncing" with the reason in words (not signed in, storage full, iCloud turning records down because its schema hasn't got them), and the shared setup's state when that's the part failing.

## DEBUG launch arguments

`-logHour 15` picks that hour today for the logger, as an hour's + does. `-sidebar` opens an iPad on its sidebar. In a Mac run from a script, add `-NSTreatUnknownArgumentsAsOpen NO` beside any argument with a value (`-tab log`): AppKit reads the bare word as a file to open, and no window comes up.

`-uiTesting` is what the UI tests launch with: the in-memory store and temporary folder described next, Health turned off, no notification prompt and no reminders scheduled, no widget reloads, StoreKit not started, and no animations. `-inMemoryStore` keeps nothing between launches: the store lives in memory and never syncs, and the shared container and the widgets' snapshot go in a temporary folder (and "Not now" on suggestions in memory), so it neither reads nor writes what the other apps publish; with `-inMemoryStore -seedSampleData`, Stadig Lift and the widgets don't see the sample. `-resetData` erases the store first, `-simulateICloudData` shows the wait a second device shows while its log comes down from iCloud (over a store that never gets any), and `-unlocked` skips the purchase (it only matters if purchases are ever switched back on, as `-access undecided|free|lapsed|extended|subscribed|bought` does).

`-seedSampleData` (three and a half weeks of a person eating a little under a true maintenance of 2,600 kcal, logged from real catalog foods, with breakfast and lunch saved as meals, and their scores recorded day by day since they began, Discipline up two or three and Vitality down one or two; the same person every launch, since its randomness is seeded), with `-sampleProcessed` (the same person eating the convenient way, toaster pastries, cola, nuggets, fries and pizza, for the food-quality views), `-sampleEmptyYesterday` (the same person with nothing eaten logged yesterday, so Today offers **Catch up**), `-sampleNoMeals` (the same person with nothing saved as a meal), `-seedUpcoming` (dinner at 7 PM if that's still to come, and lunch and dinner on each of the next two days), `-seedToday` (today eaten as yesterday was up to 5 PM, where that time has passed, so an evening shows What fits tonight), `-seedPlan` (also starts the recommended plan), `-seedOwnTargets` (a coach's 180 g protein, 210 g carbs and 60 g fat, 2,100 kcal, as the person's own targets: from today with `-seedPlan`, else set at onboarding so the assessment ran under them) and `-seedMealPlan` (also a week of meal prep for every meal, started yesterday, so Today shows its meals); `-prepCounter 6` (with `-seedMealPlan`) opens counter mode on the plan at that step, run at the plan's pace so far, so its timers are part way and those long done already taken off; `-seedLongHistory 5` (years of `LongHistory`, 12 foods a day with weigh-ins, check-ins, doses, water and fasts, on a store that's empty: for measuring the app, in Instruments or by eye; ## What the hub left

The hub app left the repo on 2026-11-11, and Today's "Your day" strip, which opened it, went with it. Since 2026-11-12 Stadig Macro doesn't read the hub's rhythm: the Log tab's automatic hours run from 7 AM to the hour before the caffeine bedtime set in Settings, or to 8 PM without one, whatever wake and bed the hub last published. It no longer writes when you usually eat (`apps/macroTrack/meals.json`) either, since only the hub read it. Both files stay where they are, untouched. [shared-container.md](shared-container.md) has the contract.
