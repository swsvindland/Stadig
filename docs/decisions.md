# Decisions to check

- **iPhone, iPad and Watch only.** The doc says "iOS first", so the first pass's Mac and Vision Pro targets are gone. iPad runs the iPhone app with readable widths.
- **Purchases are switched off for testing.** Neither app is in App Store Connect yet, so `Purchases.enabled` is `false` in both: everything is unlocked, StoreKit is never asked, and the Unlock rows are hidden. Set it to `true` in each app's `Purchase/Purchases.swift` before release; the gates below then apply as written.
- **What the unlock covers.** The assessment, logging, history, export and backup are free, because the data is yours. Generating real training blocks after the assessment needs the one-time unlock. That's a product call; it lives in `NextBlockView.accept` and `Purchases`.
- **Strength standards** are approximate public tables, scored at beginner 20, novice 40, intermediate 60, advanced 80 and elite 100. Age adjustment isn't modelled yet.
- **Macro Track's unlock** gates starting the phase plan. Logging (including photos and descriptions), the assessment, export and backup are free. The StoreKit price (24.99) is a placeholder.
- **Macro Track's weekly check-ins apply themselves**, damped and capped at 150 kcal a week within a phase (a planned phase change starts at its own calories; see the quality pass below), with the reason on Today, rather than asking each week.
- **The step aim's numbers.** The 8,000 floor (7,000 from 60), a cut's extra 2,000, 1,000 a week, nothing prescribed over 10,000 outside a cut, 1,000 steps as "the phone stayed on a desk", and a check-in holding at most once for fewer steps are calls made without sign-off. Macro Track gets steps only from Lift Track; on its own it doesn't read them.
- **Shipping `off.db`.** It's about 590 MB and bundled from `Apps/MacroTrack/Catalogs`, which isn't in git. Shipping it in the app bundle versus downloading it with Background Assets is undecided. `supplements.db` (43 MB, public domain) is bundled the same way.
- **Supplements and drinks, calls made without sign-off.** Unmarked doses count for nothing (unknown, like an unlogged day); a supplement's calories count only on days with food; upper limits are checked only when supplements contribute, and vitamin A's isn't (it applies to retinol, which labels rarely split out). A barcode in both catalogs is a supplement unless it has 50 kcal or more a serving (protein powders stay food). Drinks are off by default; water aims are 3 L and 2.2 L; caffeine uses a five-hour half-life, flags 50 mg or more at bedtime, and cuts back a fifth a week.
- **Mind Track, calls made without sign-off.** Two weeks of assessment; the split's weights (Japanese twice a week, writing riding along with maths); 15 new words a session and the recall thresholds that slow or speed them; mastery at four of five; two missed reviews demoting a topic; review weeks every sixth week without Lift Track; Intelligence as the mean of the subject scores and their formulas; Wisdom as ten points a ladder tier; the ladder's tiers and the maths levels themselves; vocabulary scores against rough JLPT word counts (the JLPT publishes no lists); and the unlock gating sessions after the assessment (check-ins stay free). The Mac runs it as the iPad app ("Designed for iPad"), since the doc lists the Mac for this one. `japanese.db` (22 MB, CC BY-SA) isn't in git and is bundled like `off.db`; where it's published under the share-alike licence is your call.
- **Mind Track v2, calls made without sign-off.**
  - **Courses:** three heavy Focus courses as the cap; four minutes of Maintenance reviews a session; two sessions a week per Focus language.
  - **First session:** with all four subjects on Focus it's all placements (reading's test fills its last six minutes), so new Japanese words start in session two.
  - **Reading:** audiobooks and books read before at half weight, tiers you set yourself at 0.3; text-levelled classics counted at half, since text alone is about 1.8 tiers out against the hand-levelled books (the Communist Manifesto lands at tier 10); a 15-minute first block and the climb rules. Google Books may need an API key, and both lookup services' terms want reading before release.
  - **Languages:** the vocabulary test drops out of Intelligence once every skill in use is measured (with speaking on but not yet tested, once the other three are; see the quality pass below); a scaffold fades when its memory lasts three weeks; foreign readers don't move Wisdom; swear words are left out of the Spanish deck; a check-in takes about 16 minutes per language; in the dialogues you're "Alex", from the UK.
  - **Maths:** "weak numeracy" is below level 5; target times from 5 to 26 seconds a problem, and a test answer passes within 1.5× its target; up after four quick right answers in five, down after three misses; placement of 6, 6 and 5 problems; the first-choice strand two maths sessions in three; a measured strand stays in the score.
  - **Writing:** grammar lessons one writing session in three while skills are left; Franklin once in each kind's four-step cycle; the first benchmark lengthens one early session by about 12 minutes; blind pairs five weeks apart; craft starts at 50 and moves 5 points a comparison, your pick and the AI's weighted equally; the benchmark always uses the first kind chosen; whether the public-domain passages JSON belongs in the repo.
- **Not built yet:**
  - Macro Track: a Watch app, saved meals and your own recipes (the meal planner uses its own building blocks), and better amount estimates for bowls and plates (the model often says 100 g for a bowl of oatmeal or a fillet of salmon)
  - Foundation Models help with free-text injury descriptions
  - camera-based mobility tests
  - velocity-based training
  - lifting-history import
  - the hub
  - Mind Track: piano (MIDI scoring and sheet music, deferred), Korean, Japanese grammar lessons (grammar is met in sentences only), kanji as cards of their own, widgets, reminders, and the iPad on a piano stand
  - Body Track migration (deferred, as the doc says)

## Calls made in the quality pass (2026-10-18)

Made without sign-off while fixing and testing. Each says what the code does now, then what's open.

### Lift Track

- **The assessment's early finish.** The final-week rule asks for more sessions than the schedule holds (six weeks' worth in a four-week assessment), so in practice the assessment completes only after its last day, or when you finish it early once your ratings have proven reliable; nothing tests final-week completion until this is settled. Open: complete once every session through the second-last week is logged (reachable from the start of the final week), once the whole schedule is, or only after the last day.
- **Body weight.** The newer of Health's weigh-in and the weight typed on the tape counts (Health's when both are from the same day), and Physique (FFMI, its band and the direction sent to Macro Track) uses the same weight; Health's weigh-in has no age limit. Open: whether the typed weight should win a same-day tie, since it was entered on purpose.
- **Typed body fat.** A body fat typed into a measurement counts however old it is, ahead of the tape estimate, until a newer one is typed (the order is Health from the last 14 days, then typed, then tape), and it reaches Macro Track. Open: honouring a typed reading only for about 28 days, or only on the newest entry.
- **Typed loads.** The set logger refuses a load over 1,500 kg (about 3,300 lb), a crash guard only (`GymIncrements.maximumLoadKg`), and the list of loads a gym can make stops at twice that. Open: about 500 kg (1,100 lb), which also catches a slipped digit but could block a big leg press or sled, or a limit for each gym from its plates.
- **Sauna- or mobility-only sessions.** A session with only the sauna or steam room, or only mobility, is saved as the day's planned session, so it moves the program on and counts toward adherence. Open: saving them as unplanned extras, or not saving sauna-only sessions at all.
- **Empty sessions, and Save to Health off.** Finishing with nothing logged saves nothing and shows no summary, and with nothing logged or with Save to Health off the phone tells the Watch to discard its mirrored workout; when the Watch doesn't get the session's end, the phone saves the workout itself. Open: whether a session started on the Watch should follow the setting too; it still saves its workout, since the Watch isn't told the setting (that needs `saveToHealth` on `PhoneContext`).
- **Setting up a second device.** Onboarding doesn't wait for iCloud: data that arrives part-way switches the app to its tabs, onboarded settings or a block already synced win over the new answers (only the profile and measurements just given are saved), and someone who finishes before anything arrives keeps the fresh setup. Open: a "Looking for your data in iCloud" state for 10 to 20 seconds or until the first change arrives, with a Start fresh button.
- **Workouts deleted in Health.** A Health workout deleted in Lift Track stays deleted, but one deleted in the Health app stays in Lift Track. Open: deleting imported workouts Health no longer returns, which risks deleting them on every device when one device's Health doesn't hold them (an iPad without Health sync), or doing that only on the device that imported them.
- **Ending a mode the day it started.** A mode that started earlier ends yesterday; one that started today is called off (deleted) if it's a vacation or crunch, while an injury or illness keeps today and its ramp or lighter week back starts tomorrow. Open: calling off every kind, or keeping today for every kind, as before.
- **Pain with no result.** A mobility test flagged "It hurt" needs a result ("Can't" or a count of 0 will do) before the check-in saves. Open: saving pain alone as 0, a floor score that would make the next pain-free result look like a big gain.
- **Blocks planned ahead.** Once a next block is accepted, Plan shows a read-only "Next block starts <day>" card and the current block keeps its deload and check-in until then; of two blocks with the same start, the one made later is current. Open: Replan and Cancel on that card.
- **The early deload.** "Deload this week" is offered only after two loading weeks, and the deload goes on Plan, the calendar, today's session and the shared calendar only once it's taken. Open: applying it as soon as fatigue calls for it.
- **Trips and deloads.** A trip that starts during the deload, even on its first day, keeps the deload's start and only stretches its end, and a trip during an early deload stretches that too. Open: whether a trip should push the deload back instead.
- **Rep-outs during an injury ramp.** There's no rep-out, at a check-in or in a loading week, while a lift ramps back after an injury, extending the rule for re-entry weeks, so that check-in has no rep-out for the lift. Open: whether losing that measurement is acceptable.
- **Sessions and days.** Sets more than three hours apart are separate sessions, so a workout past midnight is one session, and a session's day is its day in the time zone where it was recorded (older records read in the phone's current zone). Open: the three-hour gap.
- **Priorities with the same id.** They merge into one, emphasis summed (as the generator did anyway) and reasons joined. Open: whether summing is right, or the larger emphasis should stand.
- **Sessions across an update.** A saved live session reads leniently after an update (fields added later take defaults, and an unreadable rest timer is dropped, so the session comes back without its rest), but only in six types: `LiveSession`, `LiveItem`, `LiveSet`, `SessionPlan`, `PlannedItem` and `ConditioningLog`; the `PhoneContext` and `Gym` saved with the Watch's session, and the SystemCore types inside a session, are still strict, so a new required field there sets the Watch's session aside. Open: lenient decoders with chosen defaults for those too.
- **Sessions left open.** A session left open for more than six hours, on the phone or on a Watch that died or restarted, is saved as it stood at its last change, without feedback, instead of being dropped, and one the phone saves this way shows its summary at the next launch. Open: asking on the next open instead, or a window other than six hours.
- **Stop on the Watch.** In a session led by the iPhone, Stop on the Watch's heart-rate screen, or the system ending that workout, discards the Watch's workout, and the phone saves one workout under the session's id when the session ends, without the Watch's energy. Open: sending the session's id to the Watch when mirroring connects, so the Watch keeps its own workout with heart rate and energy (it needs LiftEngine, HealthBridge and app changes).
- **Losing the phone mid-workout.** When mirroring fails to start, the Watch discards the workout it just started; when the iPhone drops off later, the Watch keeps measuring until Stop. Open: ending and saving the workout when the phone disconnects, or discarding it.

### Macro Track

- **Check-ins across phases.** The 150 kcal weekly cap applies within a phase: a planned change (a diet break, maintenance after a cut, a bulk after a mini-cut) starts at the new phase's calories, and a check-in made before the phase began lends only its maintenance, so the new calories apply from the first day even when the check-in is late. Open: whether a phase change should ease in instead.
- **Misreads.** A weigh-in is a misread when it spikes past 3% on the same side of both neighbours, so sparse readings on a steady loss stay in, and a misread holds the check-in once, at the first check-in after the reading that shows it, as the steps rule does. Open: also requiring both neighbours within a few days, which needs a threshold.
- **What the other apps see.** The days published to Lift Track and Mind Track carry hits only for fully logged days, each judged by that day's own targets; partial days and today are logged with no hits, which changes Lift Track's protein note and Mind Track's protein data. Open: whether partial days should count.
- **Ending a phase early.** The ended phase keeps the days it ran, and the diet break or mini-cut starts the day the review is accepted, at its full length; an older build that re-saves the plan drops the early end, and the phases then overlap by up to six days. Open: whether the full length from that day is right.
- **Easing a surplus.** Accepting "Weight is rising faster than planned" cuts the surplus by 30%, and since the 21-day trend still reads fast, the rule fires again straight away and each acceptance cuts another 30%. Open: easing only once until something else changes the plan, or not again for about 14 days.
- **Reviews without Lift Track.** Today shows "Plan finished" every day after the last phase ends, with no Lift Track needed; a review mid-plan still needs Lift Track's block to end that day. Open: offering a review in the two days after each phase ends when there's no Lift Track calendar, or only on the day it ends.
- **Changing a meal plan mid-week.** A swap, "Other dishes" or a typed change once the week has started keeps the days before today and every logged meal; unlogged meals from today on get the new dishes, with no warning, even after prep day. Open: warning first ("Already prepped; this changes the rest of the week"), and changing from tomorrow rather than today.
- **"Other dishes".** It builds new dishes for everything still to come in one go (`MealPlanner.shuffle`), never bringing back a dish the week has had while there's another, and takes whichever other snack fills the week best, while a single snack swap takes the next snack in line (swapping dish by dish left a shuffled week about 10% short on calories and 19% on protein). Open: whether a single snack swap should also take the best-filling snack.
- **Planning on a Monday or Tuesday.** "Plan a week" defaults to next Monday; a line meant to choose otherwise does nothing. Open: defaulting to "Tomorrow, prep today" on those days.
- **Two settings records.** When a new device finishes setup before the first device's settings sync, the settings set up first (earliest `onboardedAt`) are in force, and new settings use a fixed id; the other record stays in the store unread. Open: the newest edit winning, as before, which let a new device's defaults shadow the real settings, or a field-by-field merge.
- **Undo for deletes.** Deleting an entry or a whole moment shows "Deleted … Undo", as logging does, instead of asking first. Open: a confirmation on "Delete all" instead, or both.
- **Picked times.** A time picked for today goes on the nearest day (11:30 PM picked just after midnight is last night; 7 PM picked at noon is this evening), replacing the rule that a time later than now meant yesterday. Open: whether the nearest day is the right rule.
- **First weight.** Onboarding's Continue stays off for an implausible first weight (`Weight.isPlausible`: over 20 and under 400 kg); height has no range. Open: a height range, such as 120 to 230 cm.
- **No training days.** Onboarding allows 0 training days and publishes 0 to the shared profile, though the shared schedule runs 1 to 7 and Lift Track never schedules 0. Open: 1 to 7 with a separate "I don't lift" setting, 1 to 7 accepting an energy estimate about 4% high for non-lifters, or Lift Track asking again when the shared value is outside 1 to 6.
- **Caffeine cut-back.** A cut-back leaves the daily limit in place: stopping early keeps the old limit, stopping once the goal is reached makes the goal the limit, and a week's average above the limit means Today uses the limit until the steps go below it (from 600 mg with the 400 mg default, the sheet says "This week: 480 mg" and Today 400 mg). Open: starting the cut-back from the limit, or lifting the limit while it runs.
- **Food amounts.** A count over 1,000,000 is refused, an entry reopens in its stored unit only while count × unit is within 1% of the logged amount (otherwise in g, ml or servings showing the logged amount), the last count is offered again only while it still makes the last amount, and supplement search ranks every match, not the first 400. Open: what to do when a catalog update switches an Open Food Facts food between per serving and per 100 g, since an entry stored as 2 servings then reopens as 2 g.
- **Long descriptions.** The model reads a typed or dictated description up to 2,000 characters, the API's limit, so long logs keep their last foods, though one over 500 characters takes about 1 to 2 seconds longer; photo notes stay at 500. Open: keeping the model at 500 and refusing anything longer with "That description is too long", so nothing is cut silently but long dictations fail.
- **Amount words.** "A few", "several", "a bunch", "a handful", "a lot" and "a little" state no amount, so the model's stands; a number before % or "inch" isn't a count ("2% milk", "6 inch sub"); a label food whose serving counts pieces ("1 serving (3 cookies)") is counted in those pieces, and a count of any other unit, such as "1 pack", is marked ≈. Open: whether a pack should count as one serving.
- **Milk with coffee.** An espresso drink takes its milk and size only from the part of the description that orders it; milk or coffee listed as an item of its own stays beside the drink, and "with oat milk" goes into it. Open: whether "a latte, oat milk" (a comma, no "with"), or a photo note of just "oat milk", should go into the drink too; today the milk stays its own item.
- **Meal requests.** A negation carries over "and" through a list ("no fish and pork" avoids both), a verb such as use, try, make or have straight after "and" ends it ("no rice and use quinoa" wants quinoa), and an allergen named by its tag ("peanut", "soy", "eggs") avoids the tag. Open: whether that verb list is the right one.
- **Swap tips by name.** Names match whole words, so "chocolate" no longer reads as cola; cookies, brownies, doughnuts, candy and "chocolate chip" get the sweet-spot line, and a processed-meat name decides over the protein share (pastrami and sliced ham get the processed-meat tip, not the protein one). Open: whether lean cured meats should get the protein tip, as before.
- **Desserts and breads.** A cake, pie or tart with no keyword gets the sweet tip only when sugar is 35% or more of its calories, so a commercial apple pie (about 27%) gets the meal tip, and "hot dog buns", "hamburger buns" and "white sandwich bread" get the processed-meat or meal tip, since those checks run before bread. Open: naming dessert words (with savoury pies and fish or crab cakes sent to the meal tip) or a sugar share of about 25%, and checking bread words first when the name ends in one.
- **Weigh-ins deleted in Health.** A weigh-in deleted in Health now leaves Macro Track (only those read from Health, matched by sample id; typed ones never), unlike Lift Track's workouts, but one deleted before this device's first read of changes stays. Open: a one-time reconcile against what Health still has on that first read.

### Mind Track

- **Vocabulary in a language's score.** With speaking on but never tested (switched on later, or its test skipped with "Can't speak now"), the three-skill score stands once listening, reading and writing are measured, so vocabulary drops out then; once speaking is tested, the four-skill series starts that day and the earlier three-skill points leave it. Open: recording when speaking was switched on and using the three-skill score only before then, or vocabulary until speaking is tested, which brings back the jump when the setting changes.
- **Extra practice.** Time left after the plan gets one round of extra practice (up to 80 cards ahead of schedule, none answered or learned that day), then the session ends; a review week's practice also leaves out cards reviewed that day. Open: more rounds over cards not answered today, with a guard against looping.
- **Skipping a part.** Skipping a planned part before its time is up means the session counts only if it ran 60% of the planned minutes, so "Not today" on a reading-only day with no book leaves the day missed; skipping extra practice, or a part whose time is used, isn't a skip. Open: treating "Not today" on a reading-only day as a rest day, or counting only a skipped last part.
- **Schedule changes.** Discipline and the week view judge each day by the schedule in force that day, and earlier schedules are kept for about five weeks. Open: judging the whole window by today's schedule, as before.
- **Newer data on an older build.** Settings keep enum values they don't know and write them back, and a session drops domains it doesn't know; every other stored enum (test, study and attempt kinds, book status) still fails its whole record on an unknown case, so a session with a new purpose from a newer version is lost, and Mind Track's records, apart from the life modes, are all still at schema 1. Open: making a new case in any stored enum a shape change that bumps the record's `schemaVersion`, decoding every stored enum leniently, or both.
- **Starting unlocked.** Mind Track starts unlocked from a saved copy of the last entitlement StoreKit confirmed, so a buyer opening a deep link isn't shown the paywall before StoreKit answers; StoreKit corrects it within moments, a tampered device could flip it, tests ignore it, and it's latent while purchases are off. Open: dropping it and relying on checking entitlements first and the paywall closing itself, or a "ready" flag that holds the gate.
- **Unfinished writing.** A piece being written stays on the device, one draft per task, and comes back with its task; the End dialog says so ("The piece you're writing stays on this device and comes back with its task."), and a draft whose task never returns isn't cleaned up. Open: saving it as an unfinished piece instead, which would count in the metrics, or only warning that it will be lost.
- **Test results with missing details.** A check-in result missing its details reads without them ("Numeracy: 80% right", "Reading test done") rather than as "level 0 of 10" or "0 words a minute". Open: whether that wording is right.
- **Maths read aloud.** VoiceOver reads a number grouped with a thin space as one number (`86\,000` as "86000"), and a thin space anywhere else as a space (`x\,dx` as "x dx"). Open: whether one number is right; before, "86 000" was read as two.

### Shared and the System

- **UK units.** Only the US measurement system now defaults to pounds and inches; the UK and everywhere else get kilograms and centimetres (`UnitPreference.regional(for:)`); it's only the onboarding default, and Lift Track and Macro Track both have unit pickers. Open: inches for UK length, with weight still in kilograms.
- **Open-ended life modes.** Only an injury or illness can run "Until it's better"; a vacation or crunch always has an end, so all three editors offer the toggle only for those, and Save stays off for anything `LifeModePeriod.isValid` refuses (an end before the start, an open-ended trip or crunch, an injury with no part named). Open: letting a vacation or crunch run open-ended too, by relaxing `allowsOpenEnd`.
- **Life-mode editors.** All three save through SystemCore's `LifeModePeriod.edited(openEnded:)`, so a period given an end gets a week by default (Mind Track used to give it one day), and they hide the end button ("End today"; in Lift Track "End it now", "It's better: ramp back in" or "I'm better") until a period has started, so Delete cancels one still to come. Open: turning "End today" on a period still to come into a delete rather than hiding it.
- **Life-mode deletes.** A delete is a tombstone shared through `core/life-modes.json` and each app's store; the newest edit wins by `updatedAt` to the millisecond, a delete wins a tie, and tombstones are never pruned (a handful a year). The file and `LifeModeRecord` are at schema 2 in all three apps, so a build that only knows 1 can't write a deleted period back, but it also no longer sees periods a newer build saves. Open: pruning tombstones after about 90 days, which needs that threshold settled, and whether schema 2 everywhere is right.
- **Birth years.** A birth year that makes someone under 10 or over 100 counts as unknown (`SharedProfile.plausibleBirthYears`), so heart-rate zones, the step floor, endurance norms, micronutrient targets and the energy formula use the default age, 35; Lift Track won't save one outside that range, and Macro Track's onboarding picker offers only years inside it (it used to run from 1940 to 14 years ago). Open: whether 10 to 100 is the right range, with Macro Track's old 1940 to 14 years ago as the other candidate.
- **Restoring a backup.** A backup restores only into the app that wrote it and is refused before anything is written ("This backup is from Mind Track. Restore it there."), with no exception for the hub; `AppID.knowledge` now shows as "Mind Track". Open: taking what every app shares (the profile and life modes) from another app's backup, or accepting the hub's.
- **CSV exports.** A text field that starts like a formula (`=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a return) gets a leading apostrophe, which Excel hides and Numbers shows; plain numbers such as -5 are left alone. Open: doing it only in Macro Track, whose food names come from Open Food Facts, or using another marker, such as a leading space.
- **A save against a delete.** In the record store, a save in the same millisecond as a delete wins (Macro Track deletes a day's state and saves it again under the same id), while imports, de-duplication and the life-mode merge let the delete win a tie; the pass tried making the delete win here too, and it lost those saves. Open: keeping that exception, or making a delete win every tie, with Macro Track no longer deleting a day's state before saving it.
- **Apple's models.** SystemAI tries each engine, on-device and Private Cloud Compute, at most once a request, doesn't retry a refusal, and stops when cancelled. Open: whether a failed engine should get a second try.
- **Health on returning.** Health is read again each time the app comes back, at most every ten minutes in Lift Track and every five in Macro Track, and both start their Health observer at every launch, not only after "Connect Apple Health". Open: the intervals, and whether the two should match.
- **Test launches.** `-inMemoryStore`, `-uiTesting` and the unit-test host keep the shared container, the widget snapshot, Lift Track's `live-session.json` and Mind Track's drafts in a temporary folder, so a sample run never reaches the App Group, the other apps or the widgets, and the two flags work only in DEBUG builds. Open: letting `-inMemoryStore -seedSampleData` publish to the App Group again, so the other apps and the widgets show the sample.
- **UI test retries.** The generated UI test plans retry a failed test once (`retryOnFailure`, two runs at most), since a slow simulator frame can fail a good run, so a flaky test can pass without anyone noticing. Open: no retries, so every flake shows.
- **Days on other calendars.** A `Day` is now always the Gregorian date in the device's time zone; days saved on a phone set to another calendar before the fix sit at the wrong dates (Buddhist ones 543 years ahead), and there's no migration, since nothing has shipped. Open: a migration, if a test device ran another calendar.
