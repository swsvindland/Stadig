# Tests: the logic is the product

The app tests, and how to run everything, are in [testing.md](testing.md).

- **SystemCore (122 tests).** Includes simulated users trained for 12 weeks through the real progression, autoregulation and calibration code:
  - An **honest novice** earns trust (0.3 → 0.73), and estimates track true strength within 12%.
  - A **sandbagger** is caught in week one. Machine rep-outs pin their real strength within 10%; their barbell estimate stays conservative but in range (above 80%).
  - An **overconfident veteran** loses trust (0.7 → 0.54), and estimates don't run ahead of reality.
  - An **honest lifter whose reps fall** (each set takes 4.5% of their 1RM from the next, about two reps on a set of eight) misses 18% of sets, earns trust (0.5 → 0.76) with no bias to speak of (−0.17), and progresses on fine and coarse steps. Holding the reps as well as the weight made 31% of their sets misses and read them as overconfident (bias −0.73). This lifter and the overconfident veteran pin calibration's benefit of the doubt from both sides.
  - **Strength and size blocks taking turns**, over 24 weeks for the honest lifter whose reps fall: the bench aims for 7 in the in-between block, then 8, 5 and 8 (size, strength, size); the strength block goes more than 5 kg heavier than the size block before it (72.5 → 82.5 kg); the next size block restarts more than two steps under the strength block's heaviest (72.5 kg), without a miss; size block to size block the weights still climb (72.5 → 80); the estimate is within 12%; trust rises (0.5 → 0.62) with the bias within 0.15 of a size range throughout (both −0.64; at 5–8 throughout it's −0.37), and under a fifth of sets are misses.
  - A **plateaued veteran** gets a stall that reads as a programming problem at maintenance, expected in a cut, and a recovery problem when beat up.
  - A **growing lifter** is never flagged as stalled.
  - Sets after a rated one: 225 lb (102.5 kg) squats for 8 rated Just right, with 150 s of rest, aim for 6–7 at the same weight (fewer after 45 s, more after five minutes); a miss keeps the weight while the reps stay in the range (7 missed → 5 next) and comes down a little below it; Just right at the bottom of the range comes down a little to stay in it; a check on a Just right set asks for the reps the rest gives back, one step up; a shorter rest works the target out again; reps falling as fatigue says aren't misses that hold progression back (8, 7, 6 isn't; 8, 5, 2 is).
  - Also covered: strength standards, autoregulation for every rating, assisted exercises, finding weights (at the middle of the range: 5 for 3–6, 7 for 5–8, 8 for 6–10, 10 for 8–12, 13 for 10–15, 16 for 12–20), a block's first week (after bench 100 × 5 Just right, a size block's first week aims for 8 at no more than 90 kg; later in a block the same history holds at 97.5), deloads, injury ramps, vacation reflow, early deloads, life-mode effects, the discipline band, Fatigue Budget states, physique, agility, rest rules and quests.
  - Steps: the usual count from before the phase (desk and illness days left out), a cut's target and its 1,000-a-week climb, holding outside a cut, when the aim shows, long days on foot, and a fall in steps against the month before.
  - Private Cloud Compute counts as entitled only when the Info.plist sets `SystemAIPrivateCloud` to true (not false, a string or nothing), and the test runner isn't.
  - Days: a phone set to the Buddhist, Japanese, Hebrew, Islamic or Persian calendar still counts Gregorian days (ordinals, weekdays, ISO strings, 400 days in a row and the way back to a date); days follow the time zone over three years at 6-hour steps in four zones, through New York's clock changes and São Paulo's skipped midnight in 2018; only real dates parse ("2026-02-30" and a 19-digit year, month or day don't), and a corrupt timestamp lands on 1970 instead of crashing. A time on the clock (07:00, 23:30) keeps its time and its day across New York's 2027 clock changes, a skipped 02:30 comes out as 03:30, and the Buddhist and Japanese calendars give the same instants.
  - Units: only the US defaults to pounds and inches (the UK gets kilograms and centimetres), and the shared least-squares slope gives nothing for fewer than two points, mismatched lengths or every x the same.
  - Progression and the schedule: a deload during an injury ramp scales the load actually lifted (bench at 87.5 kg stays on 2.5 kg steps; an assisted pull-up with 20 kg of help at 80 kg gets more help, not less); no rep-out while a lift ramps back, at a check-in or in a loading week; a workout past midnight is one session; after a deload the stall prompt still builds on the last real session (stalled at 100 kg, deloaded at 87.5: 102.5 next); a vacation that starts inside the deload keeps the deload and only stretches its end, and a trip during an early deload, or two overlapping trips, stretch it to cover them; an injury's overrides toggle between allowed and set aside without listing an exercise twice.
  - Scoring and stats: each training priority appears once (legs ahead and pressing ahead of pulling merge, emphasis summed, both reasons kept); every strength ratio table is complete and rising; a mobility tie goes to the first test in all 24 orders of the results; FFMI uses the current weight over one typed long ago; trajectories for goals that go down and goals past their deadline; the heart-rate recovery trend.
  - Supplement labels: a tablespoon or teaspoon serving isn't a tablet, and a macro line with no unit counts as grams while vitamins and misreads stay out. A birth year that makes someone under 10 or over 100 gives no age, so the apps' default applies.
  - Shared contracts: golden JSON in the shape the apps write today, with SystemJSON's settings (whole milliseconds since 1970, sorted keys), for Day, DayRange, LoggedSet, TrainingBlock (with and without its overrides), SystemCalendar and SharedProfile, plus a fully filled AppSummary round trip. A change that stops one decoding needs a schema bump and a migration, or an optional field. Stored names don't follow renames in the code: the 2.4 km run is still "run2_4k".
  - Life modes across apps: a delete is kept as a tombstone that beats an older live copy in either order, a newer edit beats an older delete, millisecond ties settle the same way everywhere, the merge comes out in a fixed order, and a corrupt timestamp doesn't crash it; the engines ignore tombstones, a deleted injury ramping nothing back afterwards; editors save only what makes sense (no end before the start, an open end only for injury and illness, an injury with a part), a trip or crunch with no end gets a week, and moving the start past the end carries the end; periods written before tombstones (schema 1, no `deletedAt`) still decode, and live ones write the old shape.
  - Launch options: flags and values (a negative number is a value, a flag after a flag isn't); `-inMemoryStore` keeping the store in memory without going quiet, in DEBUG builds; and both `-uiTesting` and the unit-test host keeping everything in memory and quiet.
