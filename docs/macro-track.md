# Stadig Macro

Stadig Macro is built for the least time in the app. Most visits are one of these:

- **A glance.** Today is a dashboard, with no food rows: calories left as a ring, protein (and carbs and fat, if shown) as bars, today's weight and trend, and the one or two things worth doing. The widget shows the same on the Home Screen and Lock Screen. Under them come the plan's actions and the weekly check-in (and a nutrient over its safe upper limit), then From your plan and Log again, since logging is what most visits are for, then "Done logging today?", then meal timing, supplements, drinks and "This week's food" (the ultra-processed swap, a short nutrient's fix, a fermented food). Today only ever shows today; the bar above the tabs logs from anywhere. The app reopens on the tab it was left on, and widgets, links, Siri and controls land on today at now.
- **The day, hour by hour.** The Log tab is a day's 24 hours, each with a **+**, thin when empty, under a pinned week strip (five days back, today and tomorrow, a dot on a day with food), the day's name with a calendar for any other day, and a slim line of what's left. Today opens on the hour it is, with Log again's chips under it; another day opens on its first food's hour, or 7 AM if it has none. An hour's **+** logs at now in the hour it is, a minute after the hour's last food otherwise, and at the top of an empty hour. Entries keep their minutes, and a day is still moments underneath: foods logged within 45 minutes of each other are one for the meal in the logger, saved meals, catch-up and meal timing. Tapping a food opens its amount; a swipe deletes it or logs it again; on a Mac, a right-click has what the swipes do. Logging from the bar defaults to now, with "30m ago", "1h ago", "2h ago" or any time one tap away. A time picked on the wheel for today goes on the day nearest now (11:30 PM picked just after midnight is last night, 7 PM picked at noon is this evening), and the logger's title, its time bar and the Add button name the weekday when that isn't the day on screen. A picked time lasts only while that logger is open.
- **One logger, one amount sheet.** Search, scan, photo, describe and quick add are one logger, and each ends back at its search with the meal in sight and Undo over it. Every **+** logs right away; Done, the ways to log and the time sit at the bottom, the ways to log hidden while something's typed. An amount is one small sheet: the quantity selected so typing replaces it, a keypad in the sheet with ½ ¼ ¾ ⅓ (off for metric weights and volumes), the units, and chips from half to double. Where there's a pane (iPad, the Mac, Vision Pro) the logger sits beside the day, and a food's amount takes the pane's place until it's done.
- **Food for later.** A food with a time ahead of now is an ordinary entry: there's no planned state. It counts for nothing until its time, then counts like any other, whether or not it was eaten, unless it's been removed: on its day a 6:30 PM dinner joins the ring, Health and any fast at 6:30, and a wholly planned day that passes counts in check-ins like any other. Until then it's dimmed with a small clock, read aloud as "Later", and the line of what's left says "1,230 left · 624 later". Days ahead open from the week strip, the calendar or ⌘]: what's left there is the target minus all that day's food, and a day to come refuses what has to have happened (marking it done or fasted, doses, water and weigh-ins). A food's time is noticed by a timer, a day or clock change, or coming back to the app, so with the app closed its Health write waits for the next launch, foreground or sync wake, dated correctly. Widgets and complications hold what's coming through tomorrow.
- **Moving and copying.** Holding a food picks it out, and then a tap picks out more; a tap on an hour's name picks out its foods (the rest of them, when some are), and on a Mac ⌘-click and ⇧-click do. The bar above the tabs becomes the selection's: Move or Copy, To Now, To Today, To Tomorrow, To Dates (several, from a month), or Delete, and each hour's **+** becomes a way to take them there. To Now is a meal eaten now, in order, a second apart; an hour's arrow keeps their gaps; To Today, To Tomorrow and To Dates keep each food's time of day. Moving to several dates moves to the earliest and leaves copies on the rest. Everything has Undo, and that one takes the copies away with the move. Holding an hour's name offers Repeat Now, Save or Update Meal and Remove. A selection survives changing day, and leaving the tab clears it.
- **Which day.** There's one selected day. It goes back to today on leaving the Log tab and Foods (hopping between them keeps it, so a food found for Monday lands on Monday), on launch, at midnight, after 10 minutes away unless a selection or a logger is open (on the Mac, another app in front counts as away), and on anything arriving from outside the app, a supplement reminder for an earlier day included. A Today button shows on any other day.
- **One tap.** "Log again" ranks foods by how recently and how often you eat them at this time of day, and logs the same amount with one tap. "Same as yesterday" copies what you ate around this time on a recent day. Every log shows Undo, and so does every delete, even of a whole moment ("Deleted 3 foods"). An entry reopens in the portion it was logged in while that portion still holds the logged amount, otherwise in g, ml or servings at the amount logged, so a catalog update can't change it. Changing only the time keeps its numbers.
- **The meal so far, and saved meals.** Under the logger's search, above its time bar, the meal being added to stays in sight: the foods one logged at the logger's time would join (its moment, whether they went in from this logger or before it opened), with its time, its foods and its total, so a meal of several foods doesn't vanish into a toast at each **+**. A tap opens it to change an amount, take a food out, or **Save as Meal** under a name (left blank, its three biggest foods: "Bread, cheese and mayonnaise +2"). A saved meal logs again as its foods, each its own entry at the time picked, so the log, food quality and each food's next portion are as if they'd been logged by hand: **+** on it in the logger's My Meals (above Log Again, best for now first), or a tap opens it to untick a food for this time, change an amount for good, or rename or delete it (with Undo). A search finds meals by name, or by a food in them ("turkey" finds the sandwich), above the foods. Today's Log Again offers up to three meals eaten within 90 minutes of this time of day on other days in the last month, by name, until it's eaten within 90 minutes of now (by the clock: dinner at 11:30 PM still counts at 12:15 AM), and drops "Same as yesterday" when that is one of them. Holding an hour's name saves its foods as a meal, or, when they're a saved meal with an amount changed, updates the meal. A meal counts as eaten in any moment that holds all of its foods, logged as the meal or food by food. On a desktop, Foods has Meals beside My Foods, with the selected meal and when it was eaten in the inspector (a double-click or Return logs it), and the inspector shows the meal a food would join above its Log button.
- **Catching up from habit.** A day that wasn't logged as it happened is filled in from what you usually eat. On a past day on the Log tab (Today's **Catch up** when yesterday is empty, the week strip, or History's Show in Log), each time you usually eat with nothing logged near it is a row in its hour: the time, and what you last ate then on another day ("Same as yesterday · 650 kcal", by its name when it's a saved meal), which **+** logs at that time on that day; a tap on the rest of the row opens the logger at that time for something else, and **Log all**, on the line of what's left, logs every one left with one Undo ("Logged 3 meals"). A usual time is one with a meal (50 kcal or more, so not a black coffee) within the hour of it on a quarter of the days with a meal in the last four weeks, and two days at least, taken most-eaten first with each day's nearest meal, so lunch at 12:30 and snacks at 2 and 4 stay three times; it sits at the middle of those meals, to 5 minutes. Catching up yesterday learns from today too, so today's lunch is the one offered for yesterday's. A day marked complete or fasted has nothing left to catch up. The logger's suggestions follow its time, not the clock: at 9 PM, a time picked at noon gets lunch. My Meals and Log Again rank for the time being logged at ("Log again for 12:30 PM"), recency still counted from today; "Same as yesterday" is the meal of two or more foods eaten around then on another day of the last week, until that day has one then; and on a past day the time bar's chips are the usual times, so noon, then 3, then 7 is a tap each with the suggestions following, and the logger's title names the day. Today's usual times so far are in the time menu and above the time wheel.
- **Vitamins and minerals, quietly.** Every entry keeps its micronutrients, scaled to the portion. Progress shows the last week of complete days against your targets (the US National Academies' recommended intakes for your age and sex; fibre at 14 g per 1,000 kcal; sodium and saturated fat as limits), with "unknown" rather than "low" when too little of what you ate lists a nutrient: packaged foods often carry only the label basics. When a watched one runs short, Today offers one food that fixes it, preferring ones you already eat, at an ordinary portion, logged with one tap; foods you open show their vitamins and minerals as a share of your targets. They go to Health and the CSV export too.
- **Food quality, not just macros.** Protein shakes and Pop-Tarts can hit any macro target, so every food carries how processed it is (NOVA: whole, kitchen staple, processed, ultra-processed) and whether it's fermented with live cultures, and entries keep both. Progress shows the last week's calories by group, ultra-processed against an aim of under 20% (people eat more and gain more fat on the same macros when most of it is ultra-processed), fermented foods against "most days", and fibre. Over 30% ultra-processed, Today names the biggest source and what does its job as whole food ("Oats with fruit fill the same spot"), going by whole words in its name, so "chocolate" isn't taken for cola or "barbecue" for a bar; cookies, brownies, doughnuts and candy get "Fruit, or yogurt with honey", and pastrami or ham gets "Chicken, eggs or fish cooked plain", however much protein it has. Short on fermented foods, it offers one to log with one tap, yours first. Open Food Facts supplies its own NOVA group (or one from the product's category); USDA's are estimated from each food's group and name. Foods without one (quick adds, your own) count as unknown, not as whole or processed.
- **Meal prep in an afternoon.** The Plan tab plans a week of meals sized to your targets: pick which meals to prep (any of breakfast, lunch, dinner and a snack), your diet and what to leave out. Lunches and dinners are two bowls (a protein, a carb and a vegetable in one of seven flavours, Japanese curry among them, alternating so no day repeats a dish), or one for all of them from a single batch ("One dish all week" in the setup, which carries over to the next week, or "the same meal for lunch and dinner" in a request), breakfast is overnight oats, egg muffins or a yogurt bowl, and the snack is whichever fills what's left, protein first. Every portion is solved from catalog nutrients, so a full day lands within a few percent of your calories and protein; meals you don't prep keep their room ("Dinner is yours: about 700 kcal, 45 g protein"). Then: the grocery list by aisle, in the units things are sold in (3 cans, 1 jar, 2.9 lb), with checkboxes and Share; and prep day as a timed list that overlaps the pot, the oven and your hands (about 2 hours for 14 containers and 7 jars; a grain two bowls share is cooked once, and each bowl's roast is its own step), with what goes in each container and where it keeps (fridge Mon–Wed, freezer Thu–Sun, a reminder each evening to move tomorrow's). Swap any dish with one tap, or type "no chicken, something spicy": word rules map the request to the building blocks, and Apple's on-device model (Private Cloud Compute when allowed and the app is entitled) reads it too, for wording the rules get the wrong way round ("anything but chicken"). The model only decides whether something the request names is wanted or left out; anything else it returns is dropped, since it lists the whole menu or an allergy from words that name none of it ("high protein" came back as no rice, no pasta, no potatoes). The diet, cheaper, quicker and one dish come from the words alone. Negatives and allergies leave things out ("I don't want fish", "my partner doesn't like fish", "no fish and pork", "allergic to soy", "lactose intolerant", "a nut allergy", "coeliac"), while "no rice and use quinoa" and "no pasta, just rice" still ask for the rest. A dish's name is read whole ("Japanese curry" is the Japanese curry, not teriyaki and an Indian curry), and words are matched whole ("breakfast" isn't "fast"). "High protein" changes nothing, since every plan is sized to the protein target. Once the week has started, a swap, "Other dishes" or a typed change touches only the dishes still to come: days already eaten and meals logged keep theirs. On the day, "From your plan" on Today logs each meal with one tap, as one entry that keeps its processing and fermented flags.
- **A weigh-in in seconds.** The scale button in the quick-log bar opens the weigh-in with your last weight ready to nudge (± 0.1 kg or 0.2 lb) and save; Today has an inline field until you've weighed in; and there's a Weigh In control for the Lock Screen and Control Center. Health weigh-ins from a smart scale arrive on their own: Stadig Macro watches Health from launch and reads it again whenever you come back to the app (at most every 5 minutes; pulling down on Today reads it at once). A weigh-in deleted in Health leaves Stadig Macro too; one typed here stays. A weight the trend can't use (20 kg or less, or 400 kg or more) is refused by the weigh-in, Today's field and onboarding, and Siri asks for it again in your unit.
- **A few taps.** The quick-log bar sits above the tabs on every screen: search, camera, barcode, describe and weigh in.
  - **Search** is offline and instant over 3.2 million foods: USDA, plus Open Food Facts' packaged foods. Your own foods rank first, and each result shows the portion it would log (your last one, or a natural unit like "1 container"). A packaged food can always be logged by the serving its label prints: "serving" is among its units beside the label's own wording (¾ cup, 28 g, 21 pieces), which it still opens in. That serving is the label's own amount, else Open Food Facts' reading of it, else the label first imported from the producer or USDA's branded foods. About a quarter of US packaged foods have no serving anywhere in Open Food Facts (nearly all added through apps such as Yuka, which send values per 100 g only), so they're logged by weight or volume. A brand typed before or after the food is read as one, packaged brands and the restaurant chains USDA lists alike, and that brand's food comes first, even where the other words usually mean something else: "papa johns 14 inch pepperoni" is Papa John's pepperoni pizza, not sliced pepperoni, and "tillamook cheddar" is cheese, not cheddar crackers. A brand of two or three words typed alone ("trader joes", "great value", "ben and jerrys") lists that brand's foods, the most scanned first. A food named after the one searched for comes after it: "trader joes cake" is the store's cakes before its cake pretzels, cake fries and coffee cake muffins, and "peanut butter" the spread before peanut butter cups. A size in inches (14", 14-inch, 14in) puts that size first without being required, and typos and joined words ("papajohns") are corrected. Restaurant food is USDA's: SR Legacy's chain items (analysed 2006–2012) and FNDDS 2021–2023's 191 fast-food and restaurant foods ("Chicken nuggets, from fast food", "Pizza with pepperoni, from restaurant or fast food, thin crust", "Big Mac (McDonalds)"), with portions such as "1 small fast food order" or "1 McDonald's Big Mac". A chain's name for an item finds the food FNDDS counts it as: "mcrib" is a barbecue rib sandwich, "frappuccino" a frozen coffee drink, "egg mcmuffin" an egg sandwich on an English muffin with ham, each just after the chain's own item where USDA or Open Food Facts has one. Most chains' own menus aren't in the catalogs yet; see `docs/decisions.md`. The **+** logs it, clears the search and keeps the keyboard up for the next food. A portion switched to pounds reads in tenths (200 g is 0.4 lb).
  - **Scan** is live, like filming the package: the barcode, the front and the Nutrition Facts in one sweep. A barcode the catalogs know opens the food with the usual portion ready. An unknown one is kept while the panel is read: a few frames a second go through Vision on the device, each frame's part of the panel is parsed, and every value fills in on screen as it's read, trusted (a check mark) once two frames agree and most frames that read it do. A misread frame is outvoted, and a label too tall or curved for one photo is read a slice at a time as the camera moves down it. Only what Stadig Macro tracks is kept: cholesterol, trans fat and added sugars are read for the label's own checks, then dropped. Every couple of seconds while scanning, Apple's on-device model reads the text gathered so far, copies the panel's values and names the product ("Checked by Apple Intelligence" on the card). Its reading counts as two votes on each value: it settles a tie such as the per-serving column against the per-container one and fills a line the frames missed, but it can only use a number printed on that nutrient's own row of the panel, never a % Daily Value, and three frames that agree outvote it. Review runs a last check (Private Cloud Compute too, when allowed). The hint says what's still missing ("Keep going: still reading protein"), and there's a torch. Review checks the numbers, and the food is kept with its barcode for next time. A photo of the label still works from the ⋯ menu.
  - **Photo** and **Describe** ("two eggs, toast with butter and a flat white") use Apple's on-device model, and Private Cloud Compute when allowed once the app has Apple's managed entitlement: without it, Foundation Models reports Private Cloud Compute as available and then stops the app on the first request, so until the Info.plist sets `SystemAIPrivateCloud` to true everything runs on the device and Settings hides the switch. The model only names foods and amounts; nutrients always come from the catalog. The review screen fixes a wrong match in one tap and logs everything at once. An amount you say wins over the model's guess and is read whole ("1/2 bagel", "1½ slices", "a couple of", "half a dozen", "an 8 oz steak"); "a few" or "a handful" leaves the model's amount, and "2% milk" or a "6 inch sub" isn't a count. The model's weight for a food sold in slices or pieces is checked against them: four slices' worth or more (a photo's whole loaf, a whole cake) becomes the pieces it counted, at least one, unless you said the amount. A coffee drink takes its milk and size only from its own words ("a latte with an almond croissant" isn't an almond-milk latte), and milk or coffee ordered as an item of its own stays one. A description reaches the model whole up to 2,000 characters (a photo's note, 500).
  - **Quick add** takes calories and protein.
  - **Your numbers, when the package disagrees.** The pencil on a food's Amount screen (and in Foods' inspector on a desktop) opens its name, brand, calories, protein, carbs and fat for the amount on screen ("Per 1 serving · 28 g"; per 100 g or one serving when the amount is in calories), to type what the package says. A catalog food keeps them as a correction under its own id, so its barcode, searches, favourites and Log Again find the corrected food in the catalog's place on every device, and its header says "Your numbers, corrected from Open Food Facts". Only calories and the four macros change; fiber, sodium, vitamins, portions and processing stay as the catalog has them. Entries already logged keep their numbers, but whatever is logged from then on takes the person's, however it was found: Log Again (whose chips show the corrected numbers), a moment or saved meal again, Same as yesterday, a description or photo the catalog matched. "Use the numbers from Open Food Facts" (or USDA) takes the correction away. One of the person's own foods is changed in place. The Watch has no catalog, so its Log Again copies the last entry logged, which carries the corrected numbers once the food has been logged on the phone.
- **Supplements, ticked off.** The Supplements screen (Plan tab, Settings, or Edit on Today) holds your stack. Scan a bottle's barcode and its label comes from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database (127,000 US labels, 98,000 with barcodes, offline); search it by name (every match is ranked, so a current label isn't lost behind hundreds of old ones); or keep the camera on the Supplement Facts panel, read live line by line like a food's and checked by Apple's model the same way (a photo works too), which a parser turns into lines ("Vitamin D3 (as cholecalciferol) 25 mcg (1,000 IU)" is 25 µg of vitamin D; folic acid becomes µg DFE; EPA and DHA add up to omega-3), with Apple's model only naming the product. Only the lines Stadig Macro tracks are kept (vitamins, minerals, omega-3s, caffeine, calories and macros): a bottle's herbs, extracts, amino acids and "fish oil concentrate" add nothing it counts, so the NIH labels, photos, live scans and the editor all leave them out. Each supplement gets one or more doses: a time of day (morning, midday, afternoon, evening, bedtime), how many (2 softgels, 1 scoop), and which days (every day, chosen weekdays, every other day, or training and rest days from the shared schedule and Stadig Lift's sessions). Today lists the day's doses by time of day: a tap ticks one off, "Take all" a time's worth, swipe to skip, and the section folds to one line once everything's marked. Optional reminders fire at each time with doses still open, with a Taken button that ticks them off without opening the app (a tap on one opens Today at that day's supplements, in place of any sheet that's up); "Take my supplements" does the same from Siri. Doses taken add their vitamins, minerals and caffeine to the week's report as measured (a capsule has no calories, so it doesn't stretch the estimate for foods that skip a nutrient), and their few calories count on days with food logged. Anything over a safe upper limit with supplements part of why (vitamin D over 100 µg, supplemental magnesium over 350 mg, iron, zinc, B6 and the rest of the National Academies' ULs) goes first on Today, naming the supplement and the fix: every other day, or a lower strength. Scanning a supplement in the food scanner offers to take a dose or add it; protein powders the food catalog also knows stay food. Doses go to Health and the CSV export.
- **Drinks and caffeine, if you want them.** Off by default, since counting every glass is a chore. Turned on in Settings, Today gets a Drinks card: water against a daily aim (3 L for men, 2.2 L for women, from the National Academies' intake less what food supplies) with a tap for a glass or a bottle, plus the fluid in coffee, milk, juice and energy drinks already in the food log. Coffee, espresso and tea are one tap each (logged as food from USDA, so their caffeine is exact), and your own drinks follow. Caffeine from food and pre-workouts counts against a limit (400 mg, or your own), with when the last one was and how much will still be active at bedtime at a five-hour half-life; past the limit, or with 50 mg or more left at bedtime, Today says what to do ("Caffeine before 2:30 PM leaves less in the way of deep sleep"). Drinks logged without a caffeine value (a café latte, a can Open Food Facts lists without it) get a typical one by name and brand. "Cut back" starts from the week's real average and steps down a fifth a week (at least 25 mg) to your goal, so withdrawal stays mild; each week's limit shows on Today. The daily limit itself stays as it was: stopping early keeps it, and stopping once the goal is reached makes the goal the limit. Water goes to Health.
- **Meal timing and fasts, if you want them.** Off by default, since nudges about when to eat aren't right for everyone. Turned on in Settings, everything comes from the log, with nothing to tap: a meal is a moment of 50 kcal or more (black coffee, tea or a splash of milk isn't), and an eating day runs from 4 AM, so a snack after midnight ends the evening. Today shows the time since the last meal on a small ring toward 5 hours (under 2 hours orange, 3–5 green) and the night's fast ("Overnight fast 13 h · 8:05 PM to 9:10 AM"); each meal's header has the gap before it ("+3 h 20 m"). Progress has the week: gaps by band, meals a day, the typical gap, overnight fast, eating window and last meal before bed, meals with about 0.4 g/kg of protein in a lean bulk or recomp, and one piece of advice (grazing first, then too few protein meals while building, an overnight fast under 12 hours, eating within 2 hours of bed). Only complete days (and today so far) are read: an unlogged snack would look like a long gap. **Start fast** sets a goal (13 to 72 hours with a tap, up to 7 days on the stepper) from the end of the last meal or a time picked; Today then shows a ring toward the goal, the phase (digesting, insulin low, fat burning, ketosis, deep ketosis, as ranges with what's reasonably established in people) and when the next begins, with End fast. Logging a meal ends it, on any device, and its Undo puts the fast back; dessert added to the meal it began from moves its start instead. Starting and ending both have Undo, a start can't be set before the last meal, and turning meal timing off ends the fast. Past its goal, a fast counts only as far as its goal for the scale, the estimate and fasted days unless it was ended by hand, and one with no meal logged 12 hours past its goal is taken as over at its goal. The first goal over a day asks you to read who shouldn't (insulin or a sulfonylurea, an SGLT2 inhibitor, pregnancy, an eating disorder, under 18 or underweight, gout or heart medicines) and when to stop; under 18 or underweight, nothing over a day is offered; over 3 days says to involve a doctor; a lean bulk or recomp says what a long fast costs it. Notifications come at 12 hours, a day (salt and minerals) and 3 days, and at the goal with how to break it. Whole days inside a fast count as fasted days. A fast of a day or more leaves its weigh-ins and the 3 days after out of the trend, the estimate and the check-in learn as if those days were eaten at maintenance, the check-in holds meanwhile, and Today says the scale is water. The fast is on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (a Live Activity, started again when the app opens, since the system ends one after 8 hours), in a Fasting widget (Lock Screen and Home Screen), on the Watch, in History's Overnight column and in the CSV export (`fasts.csv`).
- **No app at all.** Siri and Shortcuts: "Log food in Stadig Macro" (a description logged in the background), "How many calories are left", "Log my weight", "Take my supplements", "Log water", "Start a fast", "How long have I been fasting", "End my fast", quick add, and "Open food logger" on the camera, scanner or weigh-in for the Action Button. Controls: Snap a Meal, Scan Barcode, Log Food and Weigh In. Siri, Shortcuts and a reminder's Taken button catch up to today first, so an app left open overnight doesn't log to yesterday, and a widget link, a control or the Action Button opens its screen in place of any sheet that's up.

## Apple Watch

The Watch app is the shortest visit of all: a glance at what's left, and a tap to log. It runs on its own, with or without the iPhone nearby. The Digital Crown moves down through its pages:

- **Today.** Calories left as the outer ring, protein inside it, carbs and fat beneath (when they're shown on the phone), the day's badge (the phase, "Day 5 of 21", or a life mode with its note), **Weigh In** until there's been a weigh-in today, and **Mark Day Complete** once something's logged. The scale button at the top opens the weigh-in any time.
- **Log Again.** "Same as yesterday" while nothing's logged near now, then the ten foods `LogAgain` ranks for this time of day, as on the phone. A tap logs the same amount, now, with a haptic and a few seconds of Undo.
- **Water**, when drinks are on: water and drinks so far against the day's aim, and a glass or a bottle with a tap (8 oz and 16.9 oz for people who weigh in pounds).
- **Fasting**, when meal timing is on: the running fast as a ring toward its goal with its phase, and End Fast; or the time since the last meal, with a 13, 16, 18 or 24 hour fast a tap away (longer ones start on the phone, where the safety notes are). Undo takes back a start or an end.
- **Supplements**, when the day has doses: the open ones whose time has come (the time of day nearest now, and earlier ones still unmarked), a tap each or **Take All**, and how many are due later.
- **Weigh In.** The Digital Crown, starting at the last weigh-in, 0.1 kg or 0.2 lb a detent, inside the weights the trend accepts; Save.
- Back after a couple of minutes away, it opens on Today again.
- **Complications.** Calories Left and Protein Left, each as a circular, rectangular, inline and corner complication, Today (both, with bars) for the Smart Stack, and Fasting (the fast toward its goal, or the time since the last meal), whose timeline steps through the phases with the app closed. Tapping one opens the app. The app writes `WristSnapshot` into the Watch's App Group whenever the numbers change and reloads them; a second timeline entry starts the new day at nothing eaten at midnight.

**The same numbers as the phone.** The Watch opens Stadig Macro's own CloudKit store (`RecordStore(name: "MacroTrack", cloudKitContainer: "iCloud.dev.svindland.stadig.macro")`) and reads and writes the same records as the iPhone, iPad and Mac. They're declared once, in `Apps/MacroTrack/Records`, which both apps compile; custom foods, favourites and meal plans stay in the iPhone app, which has the catalog and the planner. It also mirrors the family's shared files through `FamilySync` (read only: the phone writes them), so Stadig Lift's calendar (deload weeks), summary (training days, steps, measurements) and the life modes reach it. `MacroAnalyst.analyse` runs over all of it, off the main thread, and `WristToday` (NutritionEngine) turns the analysis into the pages. An entry carries its own numbers, so logging again needs no food catalog (off.db is 590 MB; FoodIntelligence and MealPlanner don't build for watchOS). The Watch never applies the weekly check-in, which is the phone's job; until the phone has, the Watch shows the targets the due check-in sets, as the phone would. CloudKit's pushes wake it (the `remote-notification` background mode) so the complications follow what's logged elsewhere: it stays awake for the import, then until the analysis has written the complications' snapshot and reloaded them (`WristModel.analysisFinished`, at most 12 seconds more), so watchOS doesn't suspend it halfway.

**Apple Health, once.** The Watch writes nothing to Health: a sample's sync identifier only replaces samples from the same app, and the Watch app is a source of its own, so a food written by both would be there twice. Instead the iPhone (and the iPad and Vision Pro, the same app) writes what syncs in from devices that don't write it themselves: `HealthBackfill` checks the last 30 days of foods, water, doses taken and typed weigh-ins against Health by sync identifier and version, at launch, whenever another device's changes arrive, each time the app comes to the front, when the phone is unlocked and when Health is connected. What Health already holds from Stadig Macro on any device is left alone; the rest is written. Every write, here as something's logged and by the backfill, takes its version from `Date.healthVersion` (the record's `updatedAt` in whole milliseconds, rounded as the store rounds it), so the backfill knows the phone's own samples; one written by an earlier version, which truncated, a millisecond lower, counts as the same. A ledger on the device remembers what's been handled, so a sample deleted in Health isn't put back. A Health copy comes out only for a deletion the store holds (an Undo on the Watch, a delete on the Mac) or a dose unmarked; a record that's only missing (the store emptied while iCloud changes accounts), written by a newer app version this one can't read, or dated back out of the 30 days keeps its copy. The store's deletions are read only when something handled has gone missing, and only of its kind. It waits while the phone is locked or Health isn't connected yet (and tries again on those triggers), writes nothing while "Save to Health" is off (what arrived meanwhile stays out), and skips an ignored weigh-in and the weigh-in setup saved. So the Mac's logs reach Health too, once the iPhone or iPad sees them.

`Scripts/build.sh macro-watch` builds the Watch app and its complications; the iPhone build (`Scripts/build.sh macro`) carries the Watch app, the Mac and Vision Pro builds don't. On a Watch simulator: `xcrun simctl install <udid> <DerivedData>/Build/Products/Debug-watchsimulator/MacroTrackWatch.app`, then `xcrun simctl launch <udid> dev.svindland.stadig.macro.watchkitapp -seedSampleData -seedPlan -seedSupplements -inMemoryStore`. Its DEBUG launch arguments: `-inMemoryStore` and `-resetData` as on the phone; `-seedSampleData` (the phone's sample person, with USDA numbers in place of the catalog, and today logged up to lunch), `-seedPlan` (starts the recommended plan, so there are calorie targets), `-seedSupplements` (a stack due through the day, two weeks taken, coffee and water, drinks on), `-imperial` (pounds and fluid ounces), `-page logAgain|water|supplements|weigh`, `-scrollToEnd` (Today scrolled down), `-demoLog` (logs the first Log Again food and keeps its Undo up), `-complications` (every complication drawn in the app, since a script can't put one on a face), `-shareSnapshot` (with `-inMemoryStore`, writes the sample's snapshot to the App Group anyway, so the real complications show it) and `-largeText`.

## Packages

- **NutritionEngine** is pure Swift over SystemCore: records, the trend, energy balance, check-ins, the phase planner, targets, the analysis behind every action, "Log again" ranking and CSV tables. `MealTiming` reads meals, gaps, overnight fasts and the week's pattern from the log; `Fasting` has fasts' runs, phases, milestones, fasted days and what a long fast does to the scale and the estimate (`Fasting.adjustment`), and `FastSafety` who shouldn't, when to stop and how to break one. Supplements (the stack, dose schedules, taken and skipped doses, upper limits) and drinks (water, fluid from drinks by name, caffeine timing and the cut-back) live here too. So do the swap lines (`QualitySwap`) and the rules behind the one-tap fixes (`FoodFix`: which food to offer, at what portion); the app finds the foods in the catalog and the person's own.
- **FoodDatabase** ports the React Native app's catalogs and search: SQLite FTS5 over `usda.db` (bundled in the package) and `off.db` (bundled from `Apps/MacroTrack/Catalogs`), ranking with stemming, synonyms, spelling and brand detection (packaged foods' brands, and the chains USDA writes in capitals before a food), sizes in inches, the portion-units engine, barcode forms, and the nutrition label parser. `NutritionLabel.frame` reads whatever part of a panel one camera frame shows, and `LiveLabel` and `LiveSupplementLabel` merge frames by vote into one panel. `SupplementCatalog` reads `supplements.db`, and `SupplementLabel` reads Supplement Facts panels; the rules that turn label names and units into nutrients (`SupplementIngredients`) are in SystemCore, shared by both. `FoodUnits.logged` decides which portion a logged entry reopens in, and `FoodUnits.parseCount` reads other scripts' digits ("١/٢", "１/２") and refuses counts over 1,000,000. Each catalog keeps at most 4 idle SQLite connections, and `FoodCatalog.releaseMemory()` drops its caches on a memory warning and when the app goes to the background.
- **FoodIntelligence** ports the two-pass AI: the model names foods and amounts, candidates come from the catalog, a second pass breaks near-ties, and amounts resolve from catalog portions. Label photos go through Vision OCR and the parser, nutrition and Supplement Facts panels alike; `LabelOCR.frame` reads one live camera frame in a single pass, and `checkLabel` and `checkSupplement` have the model read a live scan's text, keeping only a number printed on that nutrient's own row (never a % Daily Value) and lines Stadig Macro tracks. Without Apple's model, `LabelOCR.label(in:)` reads a label photo in one Vision pass. The on-device model's guardrails refuse requests that call a product a dietary supplement, so those prompts say "a product label". It picks the on-device model first (with vision for photos) and Private Cloud Compute when allowed and `PrivateCloud.isEntitled` (SystemCore reads `SystemAIPrivateCloud` from the Info.plist); without the entitlement it never builds a Private Cloud Compute model, not even to ask whether one is there.
- **MealPlanner** builds a week from 55 whole-food building blocks tied to USDA foods (so the numbers never move), seven flavour profiles, breakfasts and snacks, as two bowls for lunches and dinners or one (`PlanPreferences.oneDish`). A small bounded least-squares solver sizes each container to its share of the day; the grocery list converts cooked amounts to raw and dry ones with cooking yields; the prep guide schedules oven rounds of two trays, pots and hands-on jobs on one timeline, one pot per grain. `PlanRequests` maps a request's words to the library ("sick of chicken", "dairy-free", "Asian", "the same meal for lunch and dinner"), negatives and allergies included, and `PlanAssistant` reads it with Apple's on-device model too (Private Cloud Compute only when the device's can't, the setting allows it and the app is entitled), keeping the model's reading only for the foods and flavours the request names (`PlanAssistant.merge`). `swap`, `replan` and `shuffle` ("Other dishes") keep the days before today and the meals logged, and `swappable` lists the dishes still to come.
- **HealthBridge** gained per-app permissions, food writes (food correlations keyed by entry id, replaced on edit), dietary water, and weigh-in reads and writes. Writes and deletes for one food entry run one at a time in the order made, so an Undo waits for the save it undoes, and `disable()` keeps tests and UI tests out of Health. `weighInChanges` reads weigh-ins from where the last read left off (an anchor the app keeps on the device), with the ones deleted in Health.

## Platforms

One target runs natively on iPhone, iPad, the Mac and Apple Vision Pro, synced through Stadig Macro's own CloudKit container. Layout goes by width, not device: a compact width (an iPhone, or an iPad in a narrow Split View or Slide Over) is the phone app exactly; a regular width (an iPad full screen or in a wide split, the Mac, Vision Pro) gets the desktop layout. The phone stays built for the least time in the app; the iPad and the Mac are where you find and compare foods, browse nutrients, plan the week and the grocery list, and look back over history, with logging a keystroke away.

## DEBUG launch arguments

`-uiTesting` is what the UI tests launch with: the in-memory store and temporary folder described next, Health turned off, no notification prompt and no reminders scheduled, no widget reloads, StoreKit not started, and no animations. `-inMemoryStore` keeps nothing between launches: the store lives in memory and never syncs, and the shared container and the widgets' snapshot go in a temporary folder (and "Not now" on suggestions in memory), so it neither reads nor writes what the other apps publish; with `-inMemoryStore -seedSampleData`, Stadig Lift, the hub and the widgets don't see the sample. `-resetData` erases the store first, `-simulateICloudData` shows the wait a second device shows while its log comes down from iCloud (over a store that never gets any), and `-unlocked` skips the purchase (it only matters once purchases are switched back on).

`-access undecided|free|lapsed|extended|subscribed|bought` starts the person there, with made-up prices and buying that just works, leaving StoreKit out. `-unlocked` is `bought`, and it's where any run that keeps nothing (tests, UI tests, `-inMemoryStore`) starts unless it passes `-access`.
