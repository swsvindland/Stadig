# Mind Track

Mind Track mirrors Lift Track's structure for study. A session is a focused block (25 minutes by default, five days a week), and the onboarding is the first session. Piano, the doc's fourth subject, is deferred.

- **A session** opens with reviews from every subject (the warm-up), then the day's subjects: new material, then practice. Time a part doesn't use carries on to the next, and whatever's left at the end goes to reviews ahead of schedule, once, so a 25-minute block stays one; cards answered or learned that day stay out of them, so a miss still comes back tomorrow. Missed reviews come round again a few questions later, at most twice, without being rescheduled twice. Left open past midnight or into another time zone, the app plans for the new day, and a session never starts from yesterday's plan.
- **The split.** Main courses have the days: Japanese gets two a week, maths (with a writing segment) and reading the rest, never the same group two days running. A subject that stalls or slips at a check-in gets an extra day (switchable off), as does one the hub prioritises. Secondary courses have no days of their own; one of them closes each session with a short block (Courses, below).
- **Spaced repetition** uses the open FSRS model (version 4.5, its published weights) at 90% recall. Grades are never self-rated: right or wrong and how long it took give Again, Hard, Good or Easy.
- **Assessment.** Two weeks. The first session places each subject: a Japanese vocabulary test from common words to rare until a band is mostly unknown, a maths placement that steps up and down the skill graph in about a dozen problems (a right answer marks everything beneath known, a wrong one everything built on it unknown), and one writing item per skill. Weekly "What we've learned" notes on Today.
- **Progressive overload.** Japanese: 15 new words a 25-minute session, fewer when recall on reviews drops under the aim or the week's reviews pile up, more when recall is strong. Words below the placement level get a quick check instead of a lesson, in the first session too, once its placement has measured the level. A retry after a miss, and the first ask after a failed check, get other choices in another order, so remembering where the right one was doesn't pass them. Without kana, words start in the first session, with romaji under each kana not yet mastered (and under a kanji word while its reading's kana aren't), and kana come in beside them: eight a session until the basic and voiced hiragana are in, then six (the combinations, then katakana), the words a session fewer by half the kana (11 of 15) and the two taken in turns. A word's reading isn't asked until that first hiragana is in (listening asks again instead), loanwords wait for katakana, and the practice block starts at eight words known. Maths and writing: a topic or skill is mastered at four of the last five right, ending on a right one; three wrong of five sets it aside for the day with worked solutions; two missed reviews in a row put it back into learning, and what builds on it waits.
- **Review weeks** are the system deloads when Lift Track publishes a calendar, otherwise every sixth week after the assessment: nothing new, reviews ahead of schedule (never of cards answered earlier that day), sentence listening and reading for pleasure. The **check-in** lands at the end, when fresh: vocabulary (five words from each frequency band, with "Don't know" and guesses corrected for), listening (ten sentences by ear, easy to hard), a mixed maths check over what's been learned, and ten writing items across every skill. Tests measure and never schedule.
- **Honest measures.** Intelligence is the mean of Japanese (words known on a log scale, with listening), maths (the highest level answered right at the check, scaled by accuracy) and writing (check accuracy). Wisdom is the reading ladder's comfort tier: the highest tier finished without it feeling hard. Neither moves on time spent, reviews done or cards learned. "No subtitles" episodes are logged as a plain self-rated check-in, never a score.
- **Life modes.** Vacation, illness and crunch pause Mind Track (crunch offers an optional five-minute review); the days never count. In the editor only an illness or injury can run "Until it's better" (an illness started in Settings does), the end is never before the start, the end it shows is the end it saves, and "End today" shows only once a period has started. A delete is shared like an edit (a tombstone), so it reaches the app that made the period and the person's other devices instead of coming back from their copies. Discipline is planned study days with a session that counted, in the 80–90% band. A session counts once its planned parts have all run their course (ending during extra practice still counts) or when it lasted 60% of its planned minutes; check-ins and the placement always count. A part skipped before its time is up, "Not today" on the reading part included, means the session counts only on the 60%. Discipline and the week view judge each past day by the schedule in force that day (changes are kept five weeks), so changing study days doesn't rewrite the weeks before.

## Version 2: courses, language packs and subject modules

The first playtest's notes (the doc's "Mind Track Rework") are in. The shared core:

- **Courses.** Each subject is Main, Secondary, Maintenance or off. **Main** courses have days of their own in the split (full sessions; each language gets its own days), and at least one is needed. **Secondary** courses learn new material more slowly: every regular session ends with one Secondary course's block, about 30% of what the session has after its warm-up and at least five minutes (about seven of 25), the Secondary courses taking turns (one still to be placed first, then the one that has waited longest; never one already in the session). A Secondary course places like a Main one, is tested at check-ins, and in a review week its block is practice; a Secondary language's new words are paced for the block, with at most one skill check in it. **Maintenance** is reviews only, about four minutes in each warm-up; a Maintenance maths or writing course with nothing learned is placed once (with the rest in a first session, or one a session after the warm-up), so it has something to keep, while a Maintenance language or reading course, whose test cards nothing, only reviews what was learned before. A load check warns past three heavy Main courses (languages and maths), more Main sessions than days, or Secondary courses coming up fewer than twice a week each, in onboarding and Settings. Main is stored as `focus`, its name before there were three, so settings saved before read back with every subject as Main; settings from a newer version keep the courses, modes, maths strands, writing kinds and genres this one doesn't know, and write them back (an older build sees a Secondary course as off, and keeps it).
- **Language packs.** One engine for every language: a pack names the locale, the catalog, the card-key prefix (`jp`, `es`) and an optional script layer (kana and kanji, hangul). Japanese is the first pack; Spanish is a course already and gets its catalog next. Results carry their language; results saved before carry none and count as Japanese. Speech uses each language's best installed voice, and notices one installed while the app is open. Four skills are tracked separately, listening and speaking weighted most.
- **Subject modules.** Each subject is a `SubjectModule` (its placement, plan parts, check-in parts, questions and score) with `PartRunner`s that drive its parts of a session, so subjects change without touching the session engine. Its step kinds include a reading-test passage, something to say, something to write and a blind comparison, each with its subject's own screen.
- **Records.** Writing pieces (text, source, feedback, metrics), blind comparisons and looked-up book metadata sync like the rest; books gain format (print, ebook, audiobook), ISBN and language. Onboarding asks each subject's own questions on its own page: kana and speaking, maths strands (numeracy always on), kinds of writing (creative by default), genres and only the last year's reading. Changes synced from another device show while the app is open (during a session, once it ends), and a setup that arrives by sync during onboarding is kept rather than overwritten. A backup restores only into Mind Track: another app's is refused before anything changes, and Settings shows progress while a backup is made or restored.
- **AI.** Every AI call goes through `SystemAI`: light jobs stay on the device, heavy ones prefer Private Cloud Compute when the setting allows (on by default) and the app declares Apple's managed entitlement (`SystemAIPrivateCloud` in its Info.plist; without it, Foundation Models reports PCC as available and then stops the app on the first request, so until Sam has the entitlement heavy jobs run on the device). A failure falls back to the other model when that could help, and nothing core waits on it.

Each subject's rework is under Subjects below. Spanish has its own catalog, `spanish.db`, built by `Scripts/build-spanish-catalog.py` into `Apps/MindTrack/Catalogs`; like `japanese.db` it isn't in git, and CI fetches it from the catalogs release named in `Scripts/catalogs.manifest`. Piano is still deferred, and Korean is the next language pack.

## Subjects

- **Languages (Japanese and Spanish; one engine, languages are content).** The aim is understanding real people and being understood. Each language is a pack: a catalog, a voice, a locale for on-device speech recognition and a script layer (kana and kanji for Japanese, none for Spanish). Sessions teach new words on spaced repetition (meaning, reading and listening rotate across reviews; drills are support), then a practice block of real use: a short dialogue (eight per language, three levels, written for Mind Track; your lines said out loud, or picked when speaking is off (no wrong option means the same in English or would count for the line), shown in the language for the first two run-throughs and then only in English), sentences by ear, saying words from their meaning and sentences back, picture matching (SF Symbols), sentences to read, writing words and what you hear, and for Japanese telling written forms apart. Weighted about three listening, three speaking, two reading and two writing in every ten. Practice sentences, and the sentences in each skill test, are a fresh sample each day, so the same few don't come back for months. **Four skills** each have their own level, like separate lifts, from their own banded test (listening and reading by multiple choice, speaking and writing produced and marked): a skill's reach is how far into the frequency list its sentences are understood (each band's share times its width, on the vocabulary test's log scale), levels 0–6 doubling from 100 words. The language's score is the four together, listening and speaking weighted 0.3 and reading and writing 0.2; until listening, reading and writing are measured it's the vocabulary test, so the first skill test never reads as a change, and speaking joins at its first test, so switching speaking on never drops the score back to vocabulary. Skills are first measured, two a session, once the vocabulary test finds 150 words, and all four at every check-in. **Fading scaffolds** show per item until that item is mastered (memory lasting three weeks, or known before and never lapsed; uncarded words below the placement level count as known): furigana over each kanji word, romaji under each kana for someone still learning kana, a syllable-and-stress guide (co·ra·ZÓN) under Spanish words. Never on a question that asks the reading, never in tests. **Speaking** uses SpeechAnalyzer with a SpeechTranscriber for the language's locale (DictationTranscriber where that isn't available), the model downloaded on first use; answers are marked leniently (accents, case, punctuation, katakana against hiragana, digits for numbers, an article or not; a sentence counts at 75% of its words; Japanese words found by stem or kana). Numbers heard as digits match Spanish ones written in words up to 999,999 and Japanese kanji numerals, positional or digit by digit (十二, 二〇二四), as do digits mixed with 万 the way recognition writes big numbers (3万5千円). "Can't speak now" skips without a gap and pauses speaking for twenty minutes; with speaking off nothing asks for the microphone. Leaving a step while the model downloads or the microphone starts stops it, so the microphone is never on without its screen. Other audio, a podcast or music, is ducked only while the voice speaks or the microphone listens, and comes back up as soon as neither is. **Graded readers** per language (20 Spanish, 16 Japanese, levels 1–6, free ones linked to Project Gutenberg or Tadoku) are books with `language` set and their own reading level beside English; the English reading block ignores them. Episodes without subtitles and real conversations are logged on Progress per language and never scored. `japanese.db` is built from JMdict and KANJIDIC2 (EDRDG, CC BY-SA 4.0) and Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR): 20,000 words ordered by how often everyday sentences use them, particles left out as free, 85,000 example sentences each graded by its rarest word, and 3,100 kanji. `spanish.db` (`python3 Scripts/build-spanish-catalog.py`, about 105 MB downloaded once, 30 s) is built from Wiktionary's Spanish entries via kaikki.org (CC BY-SA 4.0), OpenSubtitles frequencies from FrequencyWords (CC BY-SA 4.0) and Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR): 15,000 words in the order they're said, nouns with their article (el agua), forms that could be two words split by frequency and, for a noun spelled like a verb form, by the translations; articles, contractions and object pronouns free; 70,000 sentences. Card ids are a hash of the word, stable across rebuilds. DEBUG launch arguments: `-study spanish`, `-sampleSpanish` (sample data with Spanish as a second Main course on a six-day week) and `-fakeSpeech` (the simulator "says" the right answer).
- **Maths.** Three strands, each with its own level from 1 to 10, shown on Plan (where practice is), Progress (the level at the last test) and in each check-in.
  - **Numeracy** is drilled, not taught topic by topic: mental arithmetic, estimation, percentages, fractions and unit conversions, ten levels each, scored on speed as well as accuracy (every level has a target time, from 5 seconds for a level-1 sum to 26 for a two-step conversion). Like Lift Track's limiter slot, a timed block opens every maths session whatever the main topic: three minutes, or five while numeracy is under level 5. Every other problem comes from the weakest skill. A skill steps up at four quick, right answers of the last five and down at three misses; a right but slow answer counts for neither, and shows the quicker way. Levels are replayed from the drill history, so they need no record of their own, and review weeks practise without moving them. Estimates are multiple choice with options at least twice apart, so no sensible estimate picks wrong; other answers outside fractions are plain decimals (0.34 kg, not 17/50).
  - **Algebra to calculus**: 45 topics from order of operations to integration by parts, each with prerequisites, a short lesson and a generator of endless problems with exact answers (fractions typed as 3/4, thousands as 8,400) and worked steps.
  - **Statistics and logic**: 24 topics on a graph of their own, from reading a chart and "and, or, not" through sets, if–then reasoning, two-way tables, sampling and bias, relative and absolute risk, base rates, the normal distribution, correlation and causation, binomial probability and Bayes, to confidence intervals, p-values and least squares. Ten of them use the person's own numbers about half the time when the other apps have published enough: Lift Track's steps (a week as a labelled bar chart, four weeks for spread and z-scores) and strength days, and Macro Track's logged days and protein hits (training days against protein days as a two-way table). The app reads them from the shared container and passes them in through the catalogs; every problem has a made-up version for when there's nothing to use.
  - **Placement** is one short adaptive test of about 17 problems in place of asking about school: six timed numeracy problems on a staircase (the level is the one that best explains the passes and misses, so one slip doesn't sink it), then six and five on the other two graphs, stepping up after a right answer and down after a wrong one, the strand that matters more asked one more. Weak numeracy starts the others lower. A strand switched on later places itself after the next numeracy block; a first-version placement keeps the ladder and only numeracy and statistics are placed.
  - **Priorities**: after the numeracy block, the strand chosen first gets two sessions in three and the other the third; with numeracy alone, the whole block is numeracy. Onboarding and Settings ask which strands and which matters more, never how far school went (the old answer still decodes and nothing reads it).
  - **Honest measures**: each check-in tests every strand (eight timed numeracy problems from the working level, eight algebra and six statistics problems across what's learned, made-up numbers only). Maths' share of Intelligence is the mean of the strands measured so far, ten points a level with a check's reach scaled by its accuracy; a strand never leaves the mean once measured, so switching off the weakest can't raise it, and drills and lessons never move it.
  - DEBUG launch arguments for these screens: `-courses maths` keeps only the courses named (as Main; `-courses japanese,reading,maths:secondary,writing:maintenance` names their modes), `-mathsStrands discrete,classic` sets the order after numeracy, and `-skipParts N` moves past parts (it and `-steps` run in the order given). With `-seedSampleData` and nothing published by the other apps, sample Lift Track and Macro Track numbers stand in.
  - Maths is drawn natively from a small TeX subset (fractions, roots, powers, integrals, limits, binomials, set and conditional notation, means), with charts drawn by Swift Charts. VoiceOver reads the same parse as words: a fraction as "over", a binomial as "choose", a bar as "mean of", a conditional bar as "given", and roots, powers, integrals and limits in words.
- **Writing.** Real pieces for the kinds chosen in onboarding (creative by default, practical, argument and essay, any mix), one a session, the kinds taking turns through a four-step cycle each: the Franklin method (read a passage and take brief notes; at least two days later rewrite it from the notes alone, then see it beside the original), short creative prompts, scene rewrites (another point of view, tense or mood), practical rewrites of terse or rambling emails, notices and messages, and arguments that answer the best objection. Franklin passages come from the book being read when it's on the ladder, else near its tier, or from a page of your own book read in with the camera. Write by keyboard, with Apple Pencil on iPad, or on paper, scanned with VisionKit's document camera or photographed; Vision reads handwriting on the device into text you check before handing it in, and "Read my handwriting" takes the strokes it has read off the page. A piece being written (its text, Pencil drawing or page of your own) is kept on the device, saved a second after typing stops, and comes back with its task until it's handed in, so ending the session or the system closing the app doesn't lose it; the End dialog says so. Each piece comes back with its slips marked and two or three specific fixes from Apple Intelligence (grammar and spelling, clarity, flow, craft), never a grade: any line that grades is dropped, a failure says why in plain words, and notes can arrive after the session. Measured deterministically and shown only as trends in words, with a direction called only past two standard errors: slips per 100 words (the system dictionary under British and American English, capitalised words left alone, plus rules with their exceptions: a doubled word, a lower-case "i", "a apple" and "an banana", "could of", "rather then", "alot" and other joined words, spacing and doubled punctuation, a sentence without its capital, where a lower-case word after etc., et al. or U.S. isn't a new sentence), range of vocabulary (the type–token ratio over moving 50-word windows) and variety in sentence length (the standard deviation). The first benchmark comes during the assessment, then one a term (77 days, so every other check-in), always on the first kind's prompt. Check-ins set up to two older and newer pieces of the same exercise and kind, at least five weeks apart, side by side, unlabelled and in a fixed random order (the newest benchmark meets last term's), usually from the second check-in on. Apple Intelligence judges each pair blind in both orders, and its pick counts only when the two agree. Writing's score is half the grammar check and half craft, which starts at 50 and moves five points with each comparison (the person's and the AI's picks averaged); writing more never moves it. The 15 grammar skills and 150 objective items stay as support: placement, a skill one writing session in three while any are left (or five minutes beside a long writing slot), and reviews. 27 passages from 26 public-domain books on Project Gutenberg (Austen, Melville, the Spectator papers Franklin practised on, Darwin, Thoreau, Douglass and others, all published before 1929, with Gutenberg's header, licence and trademark removed) are built by `Scripts/build-writing-passages.py`, which caches the downloads outside the repo; the 67 prompts and tasks were written for Mind Track and are checked by `Scripts/check-writing-prompts.py`. Private Cloud Compute needs Apple's managed entitlement, so `SystemAI` uses it only once the app sets `SystemAIPrivateCloud` in its Info.plist; until then heavy jobs run on the device. DEBUG: `-checkIn writing`, `-pencil`, `-autoWrite`, `-autoPick`, `-readImage page.png` with `-handIn` (a picture in the app's Documents, read as handwriting), `-samplePair` (an early piece, so a forced check-in has a pair) and `-writingScreen plan|progress|pieces|piece`.
- **Reading.** Two measures kept apart, and a prescription:
  - **Level**, 1–10 on the ladder, comes from a real **reading test**, not from books read years ago. Onboarding asks only what you're reading now and the last twelve months, each marked print, ebook or audiobook. The first session's test is short passages, each a tier up or down from the last (steps of two until the first turn, then one), read once and timed quietly, then two or three comprehension questions without the passage. A maximum-likelihood fit over every answer sets the level (two of three right counts for less than three of three; answers on a passage read under 60 words a minute count half). Check-ins re-test with three passages not shown before. Thirty passages, three a tier: 25 public-domain excerpts from Project Gutenberg (Christie, Fitzgerald, Austen, Dostoevsky, Eliot, Melville, Proust, Faulkner, Joyce, Kant and others; each names its ebook) and five written for Mind Track. Finished books then fold in: easy says the level is at least half a tier above the book, just right puts it at the book, a slog a little below; audiobooks count half, read-before books half, and a tier set by hand counts for less than one from the ladder.
  - **Stamina** is focused minutes in one sitting, from reading blocks on print and ebooks only, with a three-tap focus rating after each (held, drifted, lost: Lift Track's colours). A session's reading block is that long: it climbs two minutes (or a tenth) while focus holds, jumps to what was read when the reading ran past the timer, holds when focus drifts and drops a quarter when it's lost. A block cut short by the session's length says nothing against a longer one. The first block is 15 minutes; audiobooks get the whole share.
  - **The next book** starts just below the tested level and climbs: a tier after an easy book, half after a just-right one, held after a slog, back a tier after one set aside as a slog (set aside as easy or just right is taste, not difficulty). It stays within two tiers below and one above the level. Suggestions are three ladder books near it, liked genres first, no two by one author.
  - **Wisdom** is the level, ten points a tier. It moves only on reading tests and finished books, never on time read.
  - **The ladder** has 345 books levelled by hand across seven genres, every tier and genre covered (at least 15 a tier). `Scripts/level-gutenberg.py` levels public-domain classics from their Gutenberg text: words a sentence, syllables a word, the share of words fewer than a third of the books use (proper names and dialect left out), commas and semicolons a sentence and vocabulary range, plus whether it argues or tells and its age, in a ridge regression fitted to 45 hand-levelled anchors. Each anchor predicted with itself left out comes within 1.8 tiers on average (rank correlation 0.48): text can't see what makes Faulkner hard, so those 147 classics join the ladder marked "levelled from its text" and count half. The texts download once into `Scripts/.gutenberg/` (which ignores itself); only `Resources/gutenberg-levels.json` is committed.
  - **Adding a book**: scan the ISBN (an EAN-13 starting 978 or 979; the camera's barcode detector, as in Macro Track), photograph or choose a photo of the cover (Vision reads it on the device and Apple's model picks the title and author as a light job, giving up after six seconds for a plain guess from the largest words and a name-shaped line), or search. The ladder and earlier lookups answer offline; then Open Library, then Google Books (its keyless quota often runs out, so it's the backup). A lookup that's used is kept as a book-meta record. Any other book's level is estimated from the ladder, the author's other books (on the ladder or finished here), or genre, length and age, and refined by how it felt. Plan and Progress show level, the tests, focus, the next tier and block; the shelf shows each book's tier and where your feedback puts it.
  - Sources: passages and classics from Project Gutenberg (public domain in the US; the Gutenberg licence covers its own files, and the excerpts carry no Gutenberg trademark); book details and covers from Open Library and Google Books, shown under their terms (worth checking before release). DEBUG: `-showShelf` opens the shelf; `-findBook <title, author or ISBN>` searches for a book there.

## Platforms

One target builds natively for iPhone, iPad, the Mac and Apple Vision Pro (not Catalyst, not "Designed for iPad"). Mind Track is meant to be studied at a desk, so the Mac, and an iPad or Vision Pro with a keyboard, are where it does most. Layouts go by width: an iPhone, or an iPad in a narrow split, keeps the iPhone's screens exactly.

- **iPhone.** Three tabs (Today, Plan, Progress); a session covers the screen; the shelf is a sheet; your writing and the maths topic map are reached from Progress and Plan. The barcode and cover cameras, VisionKit's document scanner and handwritten pages photographed or scanned all work as before.
- **iPad.** The tabs become iPadOS's bar along the top, Today, Plan, Progress and Library (a section opens on its first destination), with the whole sidebar on the toggle at its start; Library is a section of its own: **Shelf** (every book in a list beside the one selected: its level, where you are, how it felt, what you noted, and Finished it, Edit and Set it aside), **Writing** (drafts in progress, every piece with its slips, notes and source beside it, and what you noted after reading, all searchable) and **Courses** (a language's words as a sortable table: memory state, next review, recall and frequency, filtered by state and rebuilt when the cards change or the day does, the selected word's card and sentences in an inspector; and the maths topic map). Today puts the session beside what's been learned; Plan and Progress lay their course cards out in columns. A session still covers the screen, laid out for the width: a long prompt or a chart beside its answers, a maths lesson beside its worked example, a passage at a comfortable measure, writing with the passage, the text being rewritten or the notes beside the editor, the reflection and page log as roomy forms. Apple Pencil writing stays iPad-only. A handwritten page can also come in as a file (a photo or a scanned PDF) or be dropped on the editor.
- **The Mac.** The same sidebar and destinations in a window of 1180×800 (at least 760×540). A session opens in a **window of its own** (940×800, at least 640×560) with the main window behind it for the plan and progress; closing it ends the session the way End does (everything answered is kept, a piece being written stays as a draft), and so does quitting (⌘Q) with it open: the model ends the session as the app quits, since the window's own close needn't run then, and ending twice (close, then quit) ends once. Today shows "A session is open" with a way back to it, and nothing else starts while it is: Today's actions are hidden, Study's start items and Write Now are disabled, a `mindtrack://start` link leaves it alone, and the model refuses a second session outright (one built beside the first would save its reviews over the first's). Books wait too: the shelf's Finished It, Edit, Set It Aside, Start Reading and Add a Book, and the graded readers on Progress, are disabled with a note saying why (the session's reading block saves the book it began with, so a change made beside it would be written over, or leave two books on the go), and Settings' Restore from a Backup waits until the session ends. The unlock opens in a window of its own (from Study while locked, or Settings), so it's seen even with the main window closed or behind Settings. Settings is the Mac's Settings window (⌘,) with General, Subjects, Life, Intelligence and Data panes; CSV files and backups are saved with the save panel. Spelling is checked by NSSpellChecker; reading and writing are set a size up (the Mac's 13-point body is small for a page). Speaking uses the microphone without AVAudioSession, which the Mac doesn't have. Books: type, paste or search an ISBN or title, choose a cover from Photos or a file, or drop one on the finder. A handwritten page comes in from Photos, a file (a picture or a scanned PDF, read a page at a time and at most twelve pages), dropped on the editor, or from an iPhone's camera through Continuity Camera (File ▸ Import From Device). The app menu says Mind Track (the bundle name is localised in `Resources/InfoPlist.xcstrings`, since the menu bar reads it rather than the display name).
- **Vision Pro.** The iPad's layout in a glass window: the sidebar as an ornament, no opaque page backgrounds, hover effects on choices, books, ratings and filters, the words table's details as a sheet (visionOS has no inspector). No cameras: covers and pages come in as photos or files. Purchases go through the environment's purchase action, which every platform has.

**Menus and shortcuts** (the Mac's menu bar, and an iPad's when ⌘ is held): Go ▸ Today ⌘1, Plan ⌘2, Progress ⌘3, Shelf ⌘4, Writing ⌘5, Courses ⌘6. Study ▸ Start Today's Session (or Show the Session) ⌘N, Study Anyway ▸ each course, Extra Review ⇧⌘R, End Session… ⇧⌘E. Shelf ▸ Add a Book… ⇧⌘B. File ▸ Import a Handwritten Page… ⇧⌘I (while writing), Import From Device (Continuity Camera, Mac). There's one main window, so File ▸ New Window is gone. On iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro, where a session covers the screen, Settings, Go and `mindtrack://` tab links wait until it ends; on the Mac the main window stays usable beside the session's, and a session started from the menu bar opens its window even when the main one is closed.

**Keys in a session**, whenever a keyboard is attached (the rules, and which are live on each step, are `SessionKeys` in MindEngine): 1–9 pick a choice in the order shown (or a suggested book); Return checks a typed answer, moves on, finishes a passage or reading block, or starts and stops the microphone; ⌘Return hands writing in or saves a reflection; Space or R plays the prompt again and ⌥R plays it slower; ? or ⌘D is "Don't know"; A and B pick in a blind comparison; Esc asks to end, as End does, except while the step has a sheet, a picker or the scanner up (Esc is that sheet's then: on iPad a presented sheet still hears the keys of what presented it). A key that moves on acts only on the step it was pressed on (`StudyRun.next(fromStep:)` and `respond(_:onStep:)`), so Return pressed twice, or arriving as the step changes, never skips the next lesson, speaking task, comparison or answer's feedback; on the Mac a held key acts once, its repeats ignored. While a text field has the keyboard only ⌘ keys are live, so digits, letters, spaces and Return go into the answer (Return in a typed answer checks it). Choices show their keys, and a line under each step's buttons lists the rest, on the Mac always and on iPad and Vision Pro while a keyboard is connected (`GCKeyboard`).

**The widget** (Today's Study: small and medium, and the Lock Screen's on iPhone and iPad) shows today's session as Today does, the week's count, and taps through to start it. Drawn on a later day than the app last ran, it says only what it still knows: a pause that hasn't reached its end (or has none set) is still a pause, a day before the next study day the app planned is a rest day, the next study day asks to open the app, and past that it just asks; yesterday's parts and courses go, the phase goes once its last day has passed (`StudyCalendar.lastDay(ofPhaseOn:)`), and a tap starts a session only when the widget, as drawn, says one waits.

**A second device.** A second device, or a reinstall, that finds the app not set up asks iCloud once whether the app has records there (`ICloudArrival`, SystemStore): the account, then one record id from the store's zone. Nothing there, no account, or no answer within 10 seconds goes straight to onboarding, after a blank moment rather than a flash of the wait. Records there show "Bringing your courses from iCloud" instead of onboarding until this launch's first import has ended; the data arriving opens Today, and a failure to set up or import ends the wait with its reason under the spinner. "Set up as new" skips the wait. Settings' Sync row follows CloudKit's own events (`SyncState`): "Syncing…", "Up to date" with when it last synced, or "Not syncing" with the reason in words (not signed in, storage full, iCloud turning records down because its schema hasn't got them), and the shared setup's state when that's the part failing.

**What doesn't change by platform:** the engine, the records and their sync, the shared container and its family sync, and what a session teaches. A reading test's passage still isn't shown beside its questions, however wide the window: the questions come after it, without it, so the test measures reading rather than looking back.

## DEBUG launch arguments

`-uiTesting` (what the UI tests launch with: the in-memory store and temporary folders of `-inMemoryStore` below, no permission prompts and no animations; nothing asks for the microphone, speech recognition or the camera, and StoreKit isn't asked), `-seedSampleData` (eight and a half weeks of study through the assessment, a block, a review week and its check-in, every session planned and run by the real engine and answered by a student with a true level in each subject), `-inMemoryStore` (the store in memory and never synced, with a shared container and writing-drafts folder of its own in a fresh temporary folder, so it neither reads what the other apps publish nor writes where they read), `-resetData` (erases the store and the writing drafts before anything is read, so none of the old data is published on the way out), `-onboarded` (skips setup and starts the first session), `-simulateICloudData` (the wait a second device shows while its courses come down from iCloud, over a store that never gets any), `-startSession`, `-study japanese|spanish|maths|writing|reading` to start a session of one subject, `-steps N` to answer N steps of it and `-skipParts N` to move past N parts (the two run in the order given), `-checkIn japanese|spanish|maths|writing|reading` to start a check-in of one subject now, `-courses maths,writing` to keep only the courses named (each Main unless it names a mode: `maths:secondary`, `writing:maintenance`), `-mathsStrands discrete,classic`, `-sampleSpanish` (Spanish as a second Main course in the sample data), `-fakeSpeech` (speaking answers without a microphone, in UI tests too), `-showShelf` and `-findBook` (the shelf and its book finder), writing's `-pencil`, `-autoWrite`, `-autoPick`, `-readImage page.png` with `-handIn`, `-samplePair` and `-writingScreen plan|progress|pieces|piece`, `-tab plan|progress|shelf|writing|courses` (the last three are the sidebar's destinations in a wide window; ## Tests

`Scripts/build.sh unit mind` runs the app's unit and integration tests (`Apps/MindTrack/Tests`, 92 in 17 suites, Swift Testing, hosted in the app; `Scripts/build.sh unit mind mac` runs them natively on the Mac), and `Scripts/build.sh ui mind` the UI tests (`Apps/MindTrack/UITests`, 17, XCUITest; all 17 run on an iPad with `Scripts/build.sh ui mind ipad`, the iPhone's reaching the tabs through the sidebar, and the three in `DesktopUITests` skip on an iPhone). The packages' own tests run with `Scripts/build.sh test` ([package-tests.md](package-tests.md)). [testing.md](testing.md) has the simulator and DerivedData folder they use.

- **Unit and integration tests** build the app model over an in-memory store, a shared container and drafts folder in a fresh temporary folder, and a fixed clock (Wednesday 14 October 2026, 10:00), never the real ones. Under the test host the app itself comes up empty and in memory, and no launch argument runs. The app model: onboarding saves the setup and books, starts the placement session and survives a relaunch; a setup synced from another device during onboarding is kept; sample data gives an analysis and today's plan, and seeding twice adds nothing; a session finished or left halfway keeps its attempts and cards; saving a step rewrites only the session; answering keeps a step's screen, while moving on, a new session or a check-in set up over a running one gets a new one, and moving on twice from one question skips nothing; the bookmark stays within the book; the sidebar's destinations open by link and keep the iPhone's three tabs, Study Anyway brings maths its writing segment, Writing lists the drafts newest first, and the cards' revision moves only when cards are read again; starting a book sets aside the one in the same language; a schedule change keeps the old one for earlier days; an episode is a plain check-in; a change written by another store reaches the open app; the model deallocates once dropped; the test launch never touches the App Group. Two apps over one shared container: a life mode saved here reaches the other app and pauses both, deletes are shared as tombstones both ways and stay deleted, an older copy doesn't undo a newer edit, invalid periods are refused, reading the shared files again writes nothing, a life mode from an older build's backup reads as live, the hub summary is published once and not again by a relaunch with nothing changed, a backup restores into a fresh install, another app's is refused, and the CSV export writes a table per kind. Also the maths parse and its VoiceOver text (a sweep over every lesson, prompt, choice and worked step and every numeracy level finds no TeX read out), handwriting lines into paragraphs, answers as shown, test result lines (no "level 0" for a result missing its details), slips marked in the text (any outside it left alone), scaffolds for Japanese and Spanish, onboarding pages as courses change, maths strand choices, the life-mode editor's rules, deep links, Today's action cards starting what they say, the Plan card's new words following the session length, offline book lookup, and writing drafts (one per task, back until handed in, gone when the piece is cleared).
- **UI tests** launch with `-uiTesting` plus the arguments that set up the screen. On an iPad at full width they also open the shelf with `-tab shelf`, then Writing, Courses and Today with ⌘5, ⌘6 and ⌘1, start a suggested book from the shelf in the sidebar (whichever is first: the sample has a book on the go only on some days), and answer a session's first question with choices with 1 (XCUITest on the simulator delivers no Return or Escape, so Continue and End are tapped). They cover each tab of a seeded launch, Settings opening and closing, and `mindtrack://` links; setup on first run through to the first session, setup needing one Main course, and a second device waiting for its courses from iCloud rather than setup, until “Set up as new”; the Plan tab saying which Secondary courses take turns; the first session counting when ended after three answers (Today reads "Done for today"), a maths session answered through to its summary, and Start or "Study anyway" on Today opening a session; a vacation pausing Today until it's deleted, and an illness running "Until it's better"; a book found on the ladder going on the shelf, and an episode logged on Progress. Two read the real date: the episode test fails if a run crosses midnight, and the Today test takes Start or "Study anyway" by weekday. Book searches still go online under `-uiTesting`, so the shelf test relies only on the ladder's offline result.
- **Leaks**: `Scripts/leaks.sh mind` walks launch, Plan, Progress, a session, the shelf and Settings over sample data and finds none in the app's own code. On Settings it finds a one-time 32 bytes from Foundation Models' availability check, which is Apple's.
